<heading>Missing medical stores at Natal Command</heading>

<question by="#wood" to="#minister_of_defence">

<num>*10.</num>

<from>Mr. L. F. WOOD</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Defence:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether any drugs, medicines and/or surgical equipment have been reported missing from the stores and/or the dispensary at Natal Command during the past six months; if so, what drugs were involved;</li>

<li>(2) whether any or all of the supplies or equipment has been recovered; if so, which;</li>

<li>(3) whether any person or group of persons has been questioned or apprehended in this connection; if so,</li>

<li>(4) whether this person or these persons were qualified to handle drugs; if not, what were their qualifications;</li>

<li>(5) whether any other Commands in the Republic or South-West Africa have been implicated in this matter; if so, which Commands;</li>

<li>(6) whether any other Commands have reported drug shortages;</li>

<li>(7) whether any evidence has been found of an organized network of persons being involved in this matter;</li>

<li>(8) whether he will undertake an immediate inquiry into the handling of drugs at all military commands, depots or storage points.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_defence" to="#wood">

<from>The MINISTER OF DEFENCE:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1), (2), (3), (4), (5) and (7) A case of missing medical stores at Natal Command is being investigated. As the case is still <i>sub judice</i> I cannot now answer questions about it.</li>

<li>(6) In spite of all the existing control and precautionary measures sporadic shortages in medical stores still occur. For example, since the beginning of 1969 there were only 5 cases of shortages in medical stores in which minimal quantities of habit-forming or potentially harmful drugs were involved out of approximately 354 points, viz. hospitals, sick bays, stores depots, field medical establishments during exercises, ships, etc., where such drugs are kept. Each case of shortages is investigated thoroughly.</li>

<li><span class="col_667-668" refersTo="page_0433"/>(8) Notwithstanding the existing control and precautionary measures for the safe custody and handling of habitforming and potentially harmful drugs, I have asked the Surgeon General to investigate the institution of even more stringent measures.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>Charges for trunk calls on variable time interval system</heading>

<question by="#malan" to="#minister_of_posts_and_telegraphs">

<num>*14.</num>

<from>Mr. E. G. MALAN</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Posts and Telegraphs:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether his statement on 23rd March, 1971, that trunk calls via exchanges served by the variable time interval systems will be at half the normal rates between 5 p.m. and 8 a.m. also applies to trunk calls for 168 seconds or less for distances up to and including 50 km; if not, why not;</li>

<li>(2) whether the service will be available to all subscribers connected to exchanges having the variable time interval system; if not, (a) why not, (b) at which such exchanges will it be available, (c) for what estimated number of subscribers and (d) from what date.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_posts_and_telegraphs" to="#malan">

<from>The MINISTER OF POSTS AND TELEGRAPHS:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) The minimum charge for a trunk call on the variable time interval system, irrespective of when it is made or how long it lasts, is 4c, i.e. the same as that for a local call, as the equipment cannot register fractions of call units. The half-rate concession consists in the metering periods, over any <span class="col_669-670" refersTo="page_0434"/>distance, being twice as long during the half-rate hours as those which apply during the full-rate hours.</li>

<li>(2) Yes; (a), (b), (c) and (d) fall away.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

</ol>

</question>

<heading>Reply:</heading>

<ol>

<li>(1)

<ol>

<li>(a) Mr. P. H. Ferreira.</li>

<li>(b) Because it is agricultural land, the advice of the Department of Agricultural Technical Services was obtained.</li>

<li>(c) From 1st April, 1970, to 31st March, 1971.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(2) No.</li>

<li>(3) No. The seller requested permission to remain as tenant for a year to wind up his affairs and to find other accommodation. However, he suddenly decided to vacate the farm before the expiry of his tenancy. In view of the time factor and the fact that it was necessary to attend to the land immediately in preparation for the next crop, the adjoining farmers were informed verbally that the land was available for letting. There was insufficient time to call for tenders. Only two quotations were received of which the one submitted by Mr. Ferreira was the higher and this was accepted. The tenancy would originally have been for one year only after which tenders for further leasing would have been called for. Mr. Ferreira requested that the farm be let to him for at least 3 years as it was very neglected and to derive any benefit at all from the farming operations extensive preparation and capital expenditure would be necessary. An Extension Officer of the Department of Agricultural Technical Services verified this position. In view of this, the Community Development Board decided to let the farm to Mr. Ferreira for a further period of 2 years.</li>

<li>(b) R190-65 per year.</li>

</ol></li>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_transport" to="#raw">

<from>The MINISTER OF TRANSPORT:</from>

<ol>

<li>(a)

<ol>

<li>(i) Both married and single servants are paid the difference between their civil emoluments and the pay they receive from the Department of Defence.</li>

<li><span class="col_683-684" refersTo="page_0441"/>(ii) Single servants with more than two years&#x2019; railway service, married servants and all apprentices are paid the difference between their civil emoluments and the pay they receive from the Department of Defence. </ol>
