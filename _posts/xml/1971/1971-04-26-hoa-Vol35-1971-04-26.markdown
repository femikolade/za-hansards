<heading>Coloured education: Shortage of schools, classrooms, teachers, lecturers</heading>

<question by="#wood" to="#minister_of_coloured_affairs">

<num>468.</num>

<from>Mr. L. F. WOOD</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Coloured Affairs:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) What is the estimated or actual shortage of (a) schools, (b) classrooms, (c) White and non-White teachers respectively and (d) White and non-White lecturers respectively in respect of Coloured education in the Republic;</li>

<li>(2) what is the earliest date at which it is expected that these shortages will be overcome.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_coloured_affairs" to="#wood">

<from>The MINISTER OF COLOURED AFFAIRS:</from>

<p>Education for Coloured persons is a matter falling under the control of the Coloured Persons Representative Council and administered country-wide by its Executive. I have, however, ascertained that the position is as follows:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) (a) and (b) It is practically impossible to reply categorically to these questions and consequently the following particulars are furnished as a broad indication of the position:<br/>

1 181 Double shift classes were introduced at 386 schools so that all pupils enrolled this year, could be accommodated.<br/>

(c) White: None. Coloured: 1150.<br/>

(d) White: None. Coloured: 82.</li>

<li>(2) The need for schools and classrooms will be met as progress is made with the building programme of the Administration of Coloured Affairs, which for the period 1971-&#x2019;76 relates to building services <i>inter alia</i> for 55 schools with 1 391 classrooms as well as the promotion of building schemes thereafter for 189 schools with 3 881 classrooms.</li>

</ol>

<p>Overcoming the shortage of Coloured teachers would depend on the number of candidates who make themselves available to the teaching profession. The same applies in respect of Coloured lecturers.</p>

</answer>

<heading>Culling of seals at Mossel Bay</heading>

<question by="#wiley" to="#minister_of_economic_affairs">

<num>558.</num>

<from>Mr. J. W. E. WILEY</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Economic Affairs:</p>

<p>Whether seals are culled at Mossel Bay; if so, (a) how many (i) bulls and (ii) pups per annum, (b) by whom is the culling done, (c) when was the concession awarded, (d) how many applicants were there and (e) what are the terms of the concession.</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_economic_affairs" to="#wiley">

<from>The MINISTER OF ECONOMIC AFFAIRS:</from>

<p>Yes.</p>

<ol>

<li>(a)

<ol>

<li>(i) 400 bulls during the concession period of five years.</li>

<li>(ii) Unlimited.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(b) Mr. W. J. Muller, Welkom Butchery, Willowmore.</li>

<li><span class="col_789-790" refersTo="page_0494"/>(c) 9th November, 1970.</li>

<li>(d) Two.</li>

<li>(e) The main provisions are:<br/>

An annual fee of R1211 for five years is payable.<br/>

An unlimited number of pups may be culled.<br/>

At least 400 bulls must be taken during the concession period.<br/>

Methods for the culling of seals are prescribed.<br/>

The removal and processing of carcasses in consultation with the Mossel Bay Municipality are prescribed.<br/>

Annual written returns of the number of seals taken must be submitted.<br/>

A forfeitable deposit of R100 as a guarantee for the compliance with the conditions of the concession must be paid.<br/>

The State is being indemnified against any claims for loss or damage that may arise from the concession holder&#x2019;s activities.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
