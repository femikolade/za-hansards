<heading>Universities for Bantu: Signing of declaration of loyalty</heading>

<question by="#suzman" to="#minister_of_bantu_education">

<num>*2.</num>

<from>Mrs. H. SUZMAN</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Bantu Education:</p>

<p>Whether students applying for admission to any of the universities for Bantu are required to sign a declaration of loyalty as a prerequisite to admission; if so, (a) at which universities does this requisite obtain, (b) when was it introduced, (c) for what reasons and (d) what is the import of the declaration.</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_bantu_education" to="#suzman">

<from>The MINISTER OF BANTU EDUCATION:</from>

<p>Yes.</p>

<ol>

<li>(a) The University of the North.</li>

<li>(b) 1st January, 1971.</li>

<li>(c) To make students conversant with the aims of the University and with <span class="col_483-484" refersTo="page_0341"/>certain rights and responsibilities of students as members of the University community.</li>

<li>(d) The import of the declaration is as follows:<br/>

A University besides being an institution for higher education and research is also a community consisting of three groups of persons: the teaching staff, the administrative staff and the students. If one or more of these groups refuse to recognize the University and to partake in the normal activities of the University, this community is assailed in its very nature. The Council cannot therefore allow students to join this community who are not prepared to associate themselves with the University and who participate in activities implying a rejection of the University. If it should appear that a student does not fulfil this requirement he will have to leave the campus immediately.<br/>

The University declares in the statement its willingness to support and protect with all the means at its disposal, those students who associate themselves unreservedly with the University; who want to study in peace; and who wish to respect the confidence placed in them by their parents or guardians.<br/>

The student declares that he wishes to be accepted as a student and that he has read this statement and understands it fully, and that he undertakes to abide by it.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<question by="#suzman" to="#minister">

<from>Mrs. H. SUZMAN:</from>

<p>Arising from the Minister&#x2019;s reply, can he tell us why the University of the North has been singled out for this particularly juvenile treatment?</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister" to="#suzman">

<from>The MINISTER:</from>

<p>The University of the North has not been singled out by anybody, but has decided on its own to do this.</p>

</answer>

<question by="#suzman" to="#minister">

<from>Mrs. H. SUZMAN:</from>

<p>Arising further from the hon. the Minister&#x2019;s reply, can the Minister tell us whether the students too of their own accord asked for this treatment?</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister" to="#suzman">

<from>The MINISTER:</from>

<p>Those students who were prepared to abide by the regulations signed the declaration.</p>

</answer>

<p>Whether consideration has been given to the introduction of a wider rail gauge on certain routes; if not, what plans are being made to improve speed and carrying capacity on the present track width.</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_transport" to="#timoney">

<heading>Reply:</heading>

<p>No. The following are, however, details of some of the more important measures introduced to increase general operating speed over existing lines and the carrying capacity of such lines:</p>

<ol>

<li>(i) Easing of gradients.</li>

<li>(ii) Elimination of sharp curves.</li>

<li>(iii) Relaying of track with heavier material.</li>

<li>(iv) Introduction of more powerful traction power, including electrification and dieselization.</li>

<li>(v) Introduction of modern signalling techniques.</li>

<li>(vi) Multiplication of tracks.</li>

<li>(vii) Use of special-type trucks equipped with air brakes.</li>

<li>(viii) Block-load and unit-train working.</li>

<li>(ix) Elimination of the short truck.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>New Table Bay Dock scheme</heading>

<question by="#malan" to="#minister_of_transport">

<num>337.</num>

<from>Mr. E. G. Malan</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Transport:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) (a) What were the original details of the new dock scheme for <span class="col_497-498" refersTo="page_0348"/>Table Bay, (b) on what date were these details decided upon and (c) what was the original estimated cost;</li>

<li>(2) whether changes have been made in the original plans; if so, how will these changes affect (a) the width of the dock basin, (b) the seaward wall, (c) the outer mole and its length and (d) other parts of the existing docks;</li>

<li>(3) what is the estimated cost of the amended scheme;</li>

<li>(4) whether any expenses had been incurred in regard to the (a) planning and (b) execution of the original plan; if so, (i) what expenses and (ii) what amount of these expenses represents expense incurred in respect of parts of the original plan which had to be scrapped or altered;</li>

<li>(5) whether it is expected that Parliamentary authority for the new scheme will be asked for during the current Session;</li>

<li>(6) when is it expected that the new scheme will be completed.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_transport" to="#malan">

<from>The MINISTER OF TRANSPORT:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1)

<ol>

<li>(a) The Scheme provides for the construction outside Duncan Dock of a new harbour basin containing eight deepwater berths and 1 600 lineal feet of berthage for non-working vessels near the entrance, as well as the addition in time to come of a second basin with fourteen deepwater berths.</li>

<li>(b) 26th March, 1969.</li>

<li>(c) R42.7 million in respect of the first outer basin and R56.9 million for the second basin.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(2) Yes.

<ol>

<li>(a) The basin will be 300 ft. wider than originally planned.</li>

<li>(b) The seawall section parallel to the long quay is now to be constructed 300 ft. further seawards with its length reduced by 114 ft. The length of the long quaywall will remain unchanged at 4 500 ft. but the cross-berth will be 300 ft. longer.</li>

<li>(c) The outer mole and the seawall are one and the same structure. The caisson section of the seawall will, however, be lengthened by 146 ft. and the berthing length for non-working vessels reduced by 41 ft. owing to the changed curved section of the caisson seawall.</li>

<li>(d) None.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(3) Approximately R45 million for the first outer basin.</li>

<li>(4) (a) and (b) Yes.

<ol>

<li>(i) R4 192 205.</li>

<li>(ii) An estimated amount of R17 000.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(5) No.</li>

<li>(6) The first and second stages of the construction of the first basin are expected to be completed by 1976. No indication can, however, be given at this juncture as to when the further stages of the project will be completed.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
