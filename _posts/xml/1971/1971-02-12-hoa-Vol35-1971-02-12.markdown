<heading>Drug staff section of S.A. Police</heading>

<question by="#wood" to="#minister_of_police">

<num>*3.</num>

<from>Mr. L. F. WOOD</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Police:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) What is the (a) White and (b) non-White (i) authorized and (ii) actual establishment of officers and other ranks, respectively, of the drug staff section of the South African Police;</li>

<li>(2) what is the rank of the commanding officer of this section;</li>

<li>(3) whether members of this section undergo special training; if so, what is (a) the nature of the training and (b) the duration of the course;</li>

<li>(4) (a) how many and (b) what types of vehicles are at the disposal of the section;</li>

<li>(5) whether special equipment relating to drug detection and identification is provided; if so, what type of equipment;</li>

<li><span class="col_143-144" refersTo="page_0171"/>(6) what is the (a) authorized and (b) actual establishment for the section responsible for (i) the identification and (ii) the analysis of drugs;</li>

<li>(7) how many sniffer dogs (a) are employed by the drug detection section and (b) are being trained for this work.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#deputy_minister_of_finance" to="#wood">

<from>The DEPUTY MINISTER OF FINANCE (for the Minister of Police):</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) and (2) There is no detachment of the South African Police on a country wide basis with a specified establishment charged with dealing with the drug menace, but in certain large cities and towns special units under the command of local officers have been established to pay special attention to combating this evil. These police activities are being co-ordinated from South African Police Headquarters, Pretoria.</li>

<li>(3), (4) and (5) Already one training course for members of these units was held in Johannesburg during 1970. It is, however, not the Department&#x2019;s policy to divulge the details, nature, scope and methods of police training in the combating of crime of whatever nature, or to furnish particulars of vehicles or equipment employed therein, as this may be of assistance to offenders.</li>

<li>(6) None. Such identification and analysis are at present still being undertaken by laboratory staffs and pharmacists not attached to the South African Police.</li>

<li>(7) (a) and (b) None. Experiments with various crossbreeds are still being continued.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
