<heading>Lilani Hot Springs</heading>

<question by="#mitchell" to="#minister_of_bantu_administration_and_development">

<num>*1.</num>

<from>Mr. D. E. MITCHELL</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Bantu Administration and Development:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether the Lilani Hot Springs are situated in a scheduled Bantu area;</li>

<li>(2) (a) on what conditions did the previous owner have a title or rights to occupy the ground and develop the springs and (b) what rights in property or other rights were acquired by his Department from the owner;</li>

<li>(3) whether public money was used for the purchase of the property;</li>

<li>(4) whether White persons still have access to the springs; if so, on what conditions.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#deputy_minister_of_bantu_development" to="#mitchell">

<from>The DEPUTY MINISTER OF BANTU DEVELOPMENT:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) Yes.</li>

<li>(2)

<ol>

<li>(a) Permission to occupy issued on behalf of the South African Bantu Trust.</li>

<li>(b) The occupier&#x2019;s rights and interest including the improvements effected by him.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(3) South African Bantu Trust funds were utilized.</li>

<li>(4) Not at present as the property is not being run as a holiday resort.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<question by="#mitchell" to="#deputy_minister">

<from>Mr. D. E. MITCHELL:</from>

<p>Arising from the reply, could the hon. the Deputy Minister tell us whether it is anticipated that White persons will in future be permitted to visit these springs?</p>

</question>

<answer by="#deputy_minister" to="#mitchell">

<from>The DEPUTY MINISTER:</from>

<p>The matter is still being investigated.</p>

</answer>
