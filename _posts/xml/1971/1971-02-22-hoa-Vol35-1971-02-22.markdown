<heading>International requirements i.r.o. air-mail letters</heading>

<question by="#malan" to="#minister_of_posts_and_telegraphs">

<num>206.</num>

<from>Mr. E. G. MALAN</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Posts and Telegraphs:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether his Department has received information in regard to a resolution by the World Postal Union that certain South African air-mail letters did not comply with requirements; if so, (a) (i) on what date, (ii) where and (iii) on what occasion was the resolution adopted, (b) what were the contents thereof and (c) what reasons were given by the Postal Union for its resolution;</li>

<li>(2) for what reasons were the requirements not met;</li>

<li>(3) what steps have been taken in this regard;</li>

<li>(4) whether air-mail letters which meet the requirements are now available; if not, (a) why not and (b) when will they be available.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_posts_and_telegraphs" to="#malan">

<from>The MINISTER OF POSTS AND TELEGRAPHS:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) and (2) Arising from a decision by the congress of the Universal Postal Union in Tokyo during October/ November, 1969, viz., that the dimensions of certain postal articles, including aerogramme forms, should not be less than 90 X 140 mm. with a tolerance of 2 mm., the present South African aerogramme forms, in common with those of many other postal administrations, will not meet international requirements as from 1st July, 1971. This decision was taken on the strength of the findings and recommendations of the Universal Postal Union&#x2019;s Consultative Council for Postal Studies, which inquired into the rationalization of letterhandling methods and the standardization of envelope sizes.</li>

<li>(3) and (4) Steps have been, and are being, taken in consultation with the Government Printing Works to make available aerogramme forms which will meet the new requirements on 1st July, 1971.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
