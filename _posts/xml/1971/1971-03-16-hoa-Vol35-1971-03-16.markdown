<heading>Spreading of foot-and-mouth disease through production of elephant biltong in Kruger National Park</heading>

<question by="#thompson" to="#minister_of_agriculture">

<num>*6.</num>

<from>Mr. J. O. N. THOMPSON (for Mr. W. H. D. Deacon)</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Agriculture:</p>

<p>Whether any measures have been taken to prevent the spread of foot-and-mouth disease through the sale of elephant biltong produced in the Kruger National Park; if so, what measures; if not, why not.</p>

</question>

<answer by="#deputy_minister_of_agriculture" to="#thompson">

<from>The DEPUTY MINISTER OF AGRICULTURE:</from>

<p>Yes. Although elephants are not considered symptomatic carriers of the disease, the following preventive measures are, however, taken:</p>

<ol>

<li>(a) All animals utilized for biltong production are examined by a veterinary official;</li>

<li>(b) only carcasses with a pH of 6 and under are used, since the foot-and-month virus is not resisted against a low pH degree;</li>

<li>(c) biltong is produced only from muscle tissue free from blood vessels and lymph glands;</li>

<li>(d) the production of biltong is done in an isolated part of the abattoir;</li>

<li>(e) a salt mixture containing vinegar is used to bring the pH down to between 3 and 4.</li>

<li>(f) the biltong is kept in the mixture for 24 hours and then dried in isolation to a moisture content of below 30 per cent;</li>

<li>(g) the packing of the biltong is done on a premises away from the abattoir to obviate any possible contamination of the wrapping materials.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>Detention of Bishop of Zululand</heading>

<question by="#von_keyserlingk" to="#minister_of_bantu_administration_and_development">

<num>*16.</num>

<from>Brig. C. C. VON KEYSERLINGK</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Bantu Administration and Development:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether his attention has been drawn to reports of the arrest of certain Bantu prelates by municipal officials on alleged contraventions of pass laws;</li>

<li>(2) whether he will make a statement in regard to the matter.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#deputy_minister_of_bantu_administration_and_education" to="#von_keyserlingk">

<from>The DEPUTY MINISTER OF BANTU ADMINISTRATION AND EDUCATION:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) Yes, but I must point out that all pass laws have already been withdrawn in 1952.</li>

<li>(2) Yes. I am informed that on the 11th March, 1971 at 4.15 a.m. an inspection was carried out by Bantu and <span class="col_513-514" refersTo="page_0356"/>White Inspectors of the Municipality of Roodepoort on various premises near a White residential area from where continuous serious complaints have been received from the public as a result of the illegal presence of Bantu on these premises and the nuisance they created.</li>

</ol>

<p>In the residential quarters of one of these sites nineteen Bantu were found. As the European in charge of this site, a Dr. Marcus Braun, did not have the necessary permit he was summoned to appear in Court on 19th March, 1971.<br/>Whilst the Inspectors were busy checking the identity documents Dr. Braun called a Bantu from a separate rondavel. This Bantu was dressed in a safari suit with an open neck shirt and not clad in the usual ecclesiastical habit. At a later stage he was requested to produce his reference book. He replied that he did not have it with him but that it was in Eshowe. He also stated that he had no other documents to identify him. but he opened his pocket book and gave a number which he said was his national identity number.<br/>He was informed that he should have his reference book with him and was requested to come to the Registration offices of the Municipality of Roodepoort together with other Bantu who were found elsewhere and also detained. He got into the Kombi which left the premises at 5.30 a.m. On being interrogated at the office he said he was A. H. Zulu from Eshowe. Neither he nor Dr. Braun up to this stage identified him as the Bishop of Zululand.<br/>Shortly before 8 a.m. a Mr. White came to the offices with Dr. Braun. Mr. White then disclosed to the office that the person concerned was the Bishop of Zululand. The Bishop was then no longer detained but served with a summons to appear in Court on 19th March, 1971. The summons specified an admission of guilt to the amount of five rand.<br/>My Department then immediately administratively investigated the matter and the Attorney-General agreed to the withdrawal of the charge in the absence of Bishop Zulu It was further arranged that Bishop Zulu would produce his reference book at Eshowe. which he did.<br/>I wish to emphasize that it is my Department&#x2019;s policy that the laws appertaining to identity documents should be applied with discretion, consideration and understanding. Whites who know that by their conduct, as Dr. Braun did, they might be causing discomfort and embarrassment to Bantu, are to be blamed for unhappy occurrences as this one which is regretted on all sides.<br/>If Dr. Braun had only taken the most elementary steps, namely to obtain the necessary licence and identify the Bishop as one would have expected from any polite and courteous host with respect to his guest. Bishop Zulu and all concerned would have been spared the inconvenience and embarrassment of this unfortunate incident.</p>

</answer>
