<heading>Refrigeration trucks for transport of sheep and beef carcases</heading>

<question by="#kingwill" to="#minister_of_transport">

<num>526.</num>

<from>Mr. W. G. KINGWILL</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Transport:</p>

<p>How many refrigeration trucks are available for the transport of sheep and beef carcases on the South African railways.</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_transport" to="#kingwill">

<from>The MINISTER OF TRANSPORT:</from>

<p>Altogether 920 refrigerator trucks are in service for the conveyance of highly perishable commodities. No specific number of trucks is allocated exclusively to the conveyance of meat, and this type of truck is normally supplied according to demand and availability. Approximately 90 ice-cooled refrigerator trucks are at present utilized weekly for the conveyance of beef, mutton and other meat products in the Republic, South-West Africa and Botswana.</p>

</answer>

<heading>Installation of telephones at the Rotunda, Johannesburg, i.r.o. taxi service</heading>

<question by="#malan" to="#minister_of_transport">

<num>550.</num>

<from>Mr. E. G. MALAN</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Transport:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) On what conditions was permission given for the installation of telephones at the Rotunda, Johannesburg, by means of which a taxi company could be dialled directly to order a taxi;</li>

<li>(2) whether these telephones have been removed; if so, for what reasons;</li>

<li>(3) whether the same conditions will apply to future applications for the installation of similar telephones.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_transport" to="#malan">

<from>The MINISTER OF TRANSPORT:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) The conditions governing the installation of such telephones in the Rotunda are as follows:

<ol>

<li>(a) The Administration reserves the right to grant a similar concession to any other taxi company.</li>

<li>(b) In order that there shall be no possibility of differentiation, South African Airways staff will not be permitted to summon taxis for the public.</li>

<li>(c) Members of the public must themselves contact the particular taxi they wish to engage.</li>

<li>(d) All expenses in connection with the maintenance of the telephone are to be borne by the taxi company.</li>

<li><span class="col_799-800" refersTo="page_0499"/>(e) The rental for the retention of the telephone service is R30 per annum, which is payable to the Administration apart from the normal charges payable by the company to the Post Office.</li>

<li>(f) The Administration&#x2019;s rental is payable in advance and will become due on the first day of January of each year.</li>

<li>(g) The rental is to be paid to the Local Accountant, South African Railways, Johannesburg, within 14 days of the first day of January of each year during which the agreement remains in force.</li>

<li>(h) Cancellation of the agreement may be sought by either party subject to one month&#x2019;s written notice. The rental charges will in such instances be adjusted on a pro-rata basis in respect of the period the facility was actually used.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(2) Two taxi companies had telephones installed in the Rotunda, one of which cancelled the agreement and removed the telephone of its own accord. The other company has been requested to remove its telephone for the following reasons:

<ol>

<li>(a) The company did not respond to all telephone calls; after 6 p.m. did not respond to any call.</li>

<li>(b) It did not respond to calls for the conveyance of nightstop passengers in possession of S.A.A. taxi vouchers, resulting in alternative arrangements having to be made and causing unnecessary delays and inconvenience to passengers.</li>

<li>(c) The rental payable to the Administration was overdue on 4th March, 1971, the date of notification of the cancellation of the agreement.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(3) Yes.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>Compensation i.r.o. dependants and injured passengers of aircraft and train accidents</heading>

<question by="#winchester" to="#minister_of_transport">

<num>552.</num>

<from>Mr. L. E. D. WINCHESTER</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Transport:</p>

<p>What amounts (a) have been paid out in compensation to dependants and injured passengers, respectively, and (b) remain to be paid in respect of (i) the Rietbok air crash, (ii) the Windhoek air crash, (iii) the Effingham rail crash and (iv) other rail crashes.</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_transport" to="#winchester">

<from>The MINISTER OF TRANSPORT:</from>

<ol>

<li>(a)

<ol>

<li>(i) Dependants: R97 307.<br/>Injured passengers: None (there were no survivors).</li>

<li>(ii) Dependants: R207 513. Injured passengers: R18 317.</li>

<li>(iii) Dependants: R170 261.<br/>Injured passengers: R84 868.</li>

<li>(iv) Dependants and injured passengers: R359 750.<br/>The amount reflected represents compensation paid in respect of passenger train accidents subsequent to the Effingham accident on 4th October, 1965. Separate details of payments to dependants and injured passengers, respectively, are not readily available in respect of these accidents.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(b)

<ol>

<li>(i) None.</li>

<li>(ii) Dependants: R21 000. Injured passengers: R9 000.</li>

<li>(iii) None.</li>

<li>(iv) Compensation in respect of claims not yet settled still requires to be assessed in most instances.</li>

</ol></li>

</ol>

</answer>
