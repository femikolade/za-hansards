<heading>Commercial gathering of oysters on Alexandria coast</heading>

<question by="#deacon" to="#minister_of_economic_affairs">

<num>173.</num>

<from>Mr. W. H. D. DEACON</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Economic Affairs:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether any licences were granted during the period 1st January, 1969, to 31st December, 1970, for the commercial gathering of oysters on the Alexandria coast; if so, (a) to which persons or companies were licences issued, (b) by whom were the licences issued, (c) what type of licence was issued, (d) to which areas did the licences apply and (e) who were (i) the directors and (ii) the shareholders of the companies concerned;</li>

<ol>

</ol></li>

<heading>Stop-orders on salaries of S.A. Navy personnel</heading>

<question by="#wiley" to="#minister_of_defence">

<num>223.</num>

<from>Mr. J. W. E. WILEY</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Defence:</p>

<p>Whether all ranks of the South African Navy are permitted to make out stop-orders on their salaries in favour of a bank or building society; if not, (a) what ranks are not so permitted, (b) for what reason and (c) when was such a decision made.</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_defence" to="#wiley">

<from>The MINISTER OF DEFENCE:</from>

<p>No.</p>

<ol>

<li>(a) All ranks below warrant officer class 2.</li>

<li>(b) Because unit debts and overpayments, which occur mainly within the lower ranks, cannot easily be recovered if their units have no control over their salaries. Another reason is that the depositing of members&#x2019; salaries in their personal bank or building society accounts places a considerable administrative burden on the office of the Chief Paymaster. It is, therefore, preferable to pay the salaries of the lower ranks, which form the biggest group in the Defence Force, to them in cash at their units.</li>

<li>(c) Approximately ten years ago.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>Language requirements i.r.o. Postal employees</heading>

<question by="#malan" to="#minister_of_posts_and_telegraphs">

<num>226.</num>

<from>Mr. E. G. MALAN</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Posts and Telegraphs:</p>

<p>Whether changes have been made in the language requirements for (a) persons in the employ of his Department and (b) persons applying for employment in his Department; if so, (i) what were the requirements before the changes and (ii) what changes have been made.</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_posts_and_telegraphs" to="#malan">

<from>The MINISTER OF POSTS AND TELEGRAPHS:</from>

<p>(a) and (b) No.</p>

<p>As far as (a) is concerned, there has been a change in the method of determining an officer&#x2019;s language proficiency for advancement to the next higher grade. Prior to 1st October, 1970, a formal oral/written language test was imposed. At present the only requirement is a certificate by the candidate&#x2019;s controlling officer(s) to the effect that his proficiency in both languages is sufficient for advancement to the next higher grade.</p>

</answer>
