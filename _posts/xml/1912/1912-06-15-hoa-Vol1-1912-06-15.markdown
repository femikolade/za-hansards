<debateSection name="#administration_of_justice_bill_third_reading">

<heading>ADMINISTRATION OF JUSTICE BILL.<br/>THIRD READING.</heading>

<p>In clause 14,</p>

<speech by="#juta">

<from>Sir <person refersTo="hansard_za">H. H. JUTA</person> (Cape Town, Harbour)</from>

<p>moved to omit all the words in lines 23 and 24, and to substitute: &#x201C;Provided that such division shall hear and determine such cause proceedings or matter in accordance with the practice governing the division in which the proceedings were instituted and with the law according to which such last-mentioned division would have heard and determined such cause proceedings or matter.&#x201D; The mover said he would give a case in point. In the Free State there were the same principles of consideration as in the Cape, while those in the Transvaal were different. Supposing a case of this sort were removed from Bloemfontein to Pretoria, it was only fair that the same law should apply to it as if it were heard at Bloemfontein. The amendment was perfectly right and fair, and the Minister&#x2019;s objections were removed by the new words for safeguarding all those questions on which he had a difficulty.</p>

</speech>

<speech by="#brown">

<from>Mr. <person refersTo="hansard_za">D. M. BROWN</person> (Three Rivers)</from>

<p>seconded the amendment.</p>

</speech>

<speech by="#minister_of_justice">

<from>The <person refersTo="hansard_za">MINISTER OF JUSTICE</person></from>

<p>said he would point out a few reasons why the amendment should not be insisted upon. Originally he had something equivalent to what the hon. member now proposed, but the Chief Justice said there would be objections to it, so he (General Hertzog) had it struck out. So far as the law was concerned, the hon. member had worded it so that hardly any objection could be taken against it. To decide which law was applicable to a particular case was a most difficult question. When a case was removed from, say, Bloemfontein to Cape Town, the question as to which law was applicable would remain open. It was the general rule that the judge must decide it according to the law governing the transaction. Concluding, the hon. Minister said he would accept the amendment.</p>

<p>The amendment was agreed to, and the Bill read a third time.</p>

</speech>

He would support the amendment because in his opinion this agreement, which was adopted without the consent of Parliament, was not clearly defined. He believed that if Parliament had had an opportunity of discussing this contract it would not have been entered into. Some of the clauses in the contract were very loosely drawn. Articles supposed to be manufactured in South Africa were simply imported and a wrapper put round them stating that they were manufactured in South Africa. It would be quite possible for these people to import goods and put them through some process, and say that they were manufactured in South Africa. That was a very important point, and one that might lead them into infinite trouble. It would be very difficult for the Government to define their legal <span class="col_3629-3630" refersTo="page_1821"/>position in this matter.
