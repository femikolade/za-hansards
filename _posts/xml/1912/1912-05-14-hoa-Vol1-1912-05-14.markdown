<debateSection name="#injured_railway_shunter">

<heading>INJURED RAILWAY SHUNTER.</heading>

<speech by="#griffin">

<from>Mr. <person refersTo="hansard_za">W. H. GRIFFIN</person> (Pietermaritzburg, South)</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Railways and Harbours: &#x201C;(1) Whether his attention has been drawn to the fact that the services of a man named Roberts, who lost a leg and an arm while engaged in shunting operations in Durban some time back, have been dispensed with, although the accident happened through no fault of his own; (2) that out of an allowance of &#x00A3;540, &#x00A3;310 has been deducted for hospital charges and for an artificial leg: (3) whether he will supply full details of the amount of &#x00A3;310 which was deducted from the allowance; (4) whether it is customary or in cordance with Law or regulation that hospital charges are deducted from such allowances; and, if so, (5) whether he will take into favourable consideration this unfortunate man&#x2019;s case, he having a wife and two children to provide for, and give him some suitable employment to enable him to provide for his family?&#x201D; The hon. member added that he had received a telegram to the effect that Roberts had four children and not two as stated in his question.</p>

</speech>

<speech by="#minister_of_railways_and_harbours">

<from>The <person refersTo="hansard_za">MINISTER OF RAILWAYS AND HARBOURS</person></from>

<p>In regard to (1) the answer is in the affirmative, but the accident happened at Pinetown. As regards (2) the answer is also in the affirmative, but &#x00A3;236 of this amount represents injury-on duty pay. With regard to (3) the details of the &#x00A3;310 deducted from the compensation paid to Roberts are as follows: Injury-on-duty pay from date of accident, namely, November 11, 1910, to February 29, 1912, inclusive, &#x00A3;236; fees paid by Administration to doctor for medical attendance, &#x00A3;19; Addington hospital fees, &#x00A3;19; artificial leg, &#x00A3;18; artificial arm, &#x00A3;18. The answer to (4) is in the affirmative. As regards (5)This has been done; but there is no suitable position vacant to which Roberts could usefully be appointed. I would like to add that the Administration was not responsible for the mishap, and Roberts was not legally entitled to any compensation at all a sum of &#x00A3;540 having been paid by the Administration purely as an act of grace and in view of circumstances of case.</p>

</speech>

<from>The <person refersTo="hansard_za">PRIME MINISTER</person></from>

<p>replied: (1) The answer is in the affirmative. (3), (4) and (5) The answer is in the negative. (6) The Government, as at present advised, see no reason why the High Commissioner should not be a member of the London Chamber of Commerce.
