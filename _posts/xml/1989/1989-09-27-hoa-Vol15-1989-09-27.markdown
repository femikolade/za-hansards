<p>Parliament has been summoned to meet here today in order to constitute the President&#x2019;s Council. Sixty members must be designated to serve on this council. For many of the 60 members who are going to be elected to the President&#x2019;s Council it is very important to know what the future holds for them, particularly now that they have had to take note of a press statement by the hon the State President about the future of the President&#x2019;s Council. <p>In 1988 the President&#x2019;s Council instituted an investigation into its own composition, after the previous State President had suggested that Black people be included in the President&#x2019;s Council. The President&#x2019;s Council recommended that its membership be increased to 65 to make provision for Black people as well. That was at the beginning of last year.</p>

<p>The report had hardly been published when the previous State President did a complete about-face and issued a new instruction to the President&#x2019;s Council, which I should like to repeat. On 2 July 1988 the State President requested the President&#x2019;s Council, in terms of section 78(1) of the Republic of South Africa Constitution Act, 1983, to institute an investigation into and advise him on the composition of the President&#x2019;s Council in respect of the rationalisation of the advisory function rendered by the council in terms of the Constitution in order to make provision for the re-organisation of the present President&#x2019;s Council, converting it into a part-time council consisting of skilled and experienced persons. The State President also issued other instructions.</p>

<p>On 29 April, after an <i>ad-hoc</i> committee of the President&#x2019;s Council under the chairmanship of Mr Pen Kotz&#x00E9; had investigated this matter, the President&#x2019;s Council met. <p>I should now like to quote what the President&#x2019;s Council recommended. It must be borne in mind that this was its second recommendation in one year in regard to its own composition. The President&#x2019;s Council recommended on page 19, paragraph 5.1, that there should be no drastic change, at that stage, to the full-time nature of the council, but that the membership of the council should be reduced. Furthermore the Council proposed that reducing the membership was justified from a cost-saving point of view, as well as from the point of view of improved <span class="col_93-94" refersTo="page_0074"/>functionality. <p>This report of the President&#x2019;s Council was still hot off the press&#x2014; the printer&#x2019;s ink on its pages was hardly dry&#x2014;when the present hon State President made another announcement. [Interjections.] He said that apart from the four members, all the other members of the President&#x2019;s Council would function on a part-time basis. He said there would be only two committees, not three.</p>

<p>Is the NP&#x2019;s prevarication about the composition and functioning of the President&#x2019;s Council not the absolute pinnacle of inconsistency? Is this not the pinnacle of political bungling? Is this inconsistent and contradictory approach not a manifestation of the total uncertainty in which the NP finds itself? [Interjections.]</p>

<p>I think it is the Government&#x2019;s duty, before members are elected to the President&#x2019;s Council, to spell out to aspiring members of the President&#x2019;s Council and political parties in Parliament who have to elect these members, what is in store for them during their term of office. [Interjections.] I think Parliament owes it to the members of the President&#x2019;s Council to spell out to them what is in store for them in the coming year. [Interjections.]</p>

<p>The CP is, in principle, opposed to the policy of political power-sharing. [Interjections]</p>
