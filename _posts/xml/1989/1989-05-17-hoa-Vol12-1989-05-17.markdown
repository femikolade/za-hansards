<from>*Mr <person refersTo="hansard_za">F J LE ROUX</person>:</from>

<p>Mr Speaker, on a point of order: Rule 167 (1) of the Standing Rules of Parliament stipulates:</p>

<block name="quote">When the schedule to an appropriation Bill has been agreed to by each House, the Chief Whip of Parliament shall place the Bill on the Order Paper of each House for Second Reading.</block>

<p>The heading to this rule indicates that it is applicable to a money Bill.</p>

<p>It is a well-known rule in the interpretation of a piece of legislation that words are to be interpreted according to their normal meaning. Vote No 3&#x2014;&#x201C;Development Planning&#x201D;&#x2014;as contained in Schedule 1 on page 4 of the Appropriation Bill and as introduced in Parliament by the hon the Minister of Finance on 15 March, was rejected by the House of Representatives. It concerns an amount of R1 062 935 000, and the result of that is that the schedule, as introduced, was not agreed to by each House.</p>

<p>Any amended approval in terms of which no provision has been made for the allocation of the said amount, is not an approval of the schedule. This Rule is not amenable to any other interpretation.</p>

<p>Standing Rule 143 is not applicable either, as Mr Speaker can only send the message referred to here, to the hon the State President once a House agrees to a version of a Bill which differs from the version passed by the other House or Houses. That can only happen after the Second Reading.</p>

<p>In terms of the stipulations&#x2014;I think it is section 32&#x2014;of Act No 110 of 1983, the Bill may not be referred to the President&#x2019;s Council either as that can only happen after the Second Reading. Therefore it is not possible to place it on the Order Paper for the Second Reading in terms of Rule 167 (1).</p>

<p>The vacuum cannot be filled in terms of Rule 2 (1) either, because Rule 167 (1) makes specific provision for this kind of event. The legislature specifically stipulated that each House&#x2014;stressing the words &#x201C;each House&#x201D;&#x2014;had to agree to the schedule as a whole. If this does not happen, the next stage of the Bill may not be proceeded with. I therefore ask you, Mr Speaker, to rule that Parliament may not proceed with this debate.</p>

</speech>
