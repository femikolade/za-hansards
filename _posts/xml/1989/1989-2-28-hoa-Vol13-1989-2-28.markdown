<from>*The <person refersTo="hansard_za">MINISTER OF FOREIGN AFFAIRS</person>:</from>

<p>Mr Chairman, in replying to this question I assume that under diplomatic staff the hon member for Pietersburg includes consular staff. <p>With reference to consular staff, the Transkei has consulates in Bloemfontein, Durban, Johannesburg, East London and Port Elizabeth. Venda has consulates in Johannesburg and Pietersburg, and the Ciskei has consulates in Bloemfontein, Durban, Johannesburg, Cape Town, Newcastle and Port Elizabeth.</p>

<heading>SADF pensioners: medical arrangements</heading>

<question by="#malcomess" to="#minister_of_defence">

<num>*3.</num>

<from>Mr D J N MALCOMESS</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Defence:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) What medical arrangements are made for South African Defence Force pensioners who require operations;</li>

<li>(2) whether such pensioners are obliged to have operations at military hospitals; if not, what are the relevant details?</li>

</ol>

<p>B139E</p>

</question>

<answer by="#deputy_minister_of_defence" to="#malcomess">

<from>The DEPUTY MINISTER OF DEFENCE:</from>

<p>(1) and (2) The medical expenses of members of the Permanent Force who retire with pension, is borne by the Permanent Force Medical Continuation Fund. These members must, where possible, make use of the facilities of Military Hospitals. If this is not possible, for example in areas where no Military Hospital exists, Provincial Hospitals may be used and the cost will be borne by the Permanent Force Medical Continuation Fund.</p>

<p>Military pensioners who receive a pension in terms of the War Pension Act, receive their medical treatment at the expense of the Department of National Health and Population Development at their nearest Provincial or Military Hospital.</p>

</answer>

</question>

<heading>SAP vehicles: differing numeber plates displayed</heading>

<question by="#ellis" to="#minister_of_law_and_order">

<num>*6.</num>

<from>Mr M J ELLIS</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Law and Order:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether the South African Police own a vehicle with the number plates NP 47417 and NP 81816 and another with the number plates NP 6062 and NP 71125; if so, (a) why are two differing number plates displayed on each vehicle and (b) who gave permission for two differing number plates to be displayed; if not, who owns the vehicles concerned;</li>

<li>(2) whether any action has been taken against the owners and/or drivers of these vehicles as a result of the differing number plates; if not, why not; if so, (a) what action, (b) against whom and (c) when?</li>

</ol>

<p>B213E</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_law_and_order" to="#ellis">

<from>&#x2020;The MINISTER OF LAW AND ORDER:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) and (2)</li>

</ol>

<p>In combating crime, during clandestine police actions, it is often necessary for the South African Police to make use of vehicles which cannot be identified by means of their number plates or otherwise. It is also often necessary in high risk areas because identifiable police vehicles are maliciously damaged by hostile elements, especially when they are parked unguarded.</p>

<p>However, instructions in respect of this matter, which I am not prepared to make known here, are being applied.</p>

<p>The honourable member has apparently put this question to me as a result of a letter concerning the matter which was published in the <i>Natal Witness</i> recently. In the newspaper concerned, the liaison officer of the South African Police in Pietermaritzburg addressed an invitation to the correspondent to substantiate the allegations which he made in his letter <span class="col_153-154" refersTo="page_0116"/>by means of an affidavit, so that they could be investigated.</p>

<p>Up until now no reaction in this regard has been received from the correspondent or from any other person. On the contrary, his letter evoked extremely favourable comments from the public regarding the excellent service rendered by the South African Police, Pietermaritzburg. I would therefore like to make an appeal to the hon member &#x2014; we know the hon member and we know he is a good friend of the South African Police &#x2014; not to always regard the South African Police with suspicion, but to rather support them positively in the difficult task which they perform.</p>

</answer>

<li>(2) whether any action arising out of these reports is being considered; if so, (a) what action, (b) against whom and (c) when;</li>

<li>(3) whether any further investigations arising out of these reports are being considered; if so, (a) what is the nature of each such investigation and (b) when is it anticipated that each will be completed?</li>

</ol>

<p>B215E</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_home_affairs_and_of_communications" to="#dalling">

<from>The MINISTER OF HOME AFFAIRS AND OF COMMUNICATIONS (for the Minister of Justice):</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) At this stage a white paper on the first two reports is not indicated, since the recommendations in those two reports are clear and well defined and have already been given effect to. Suffice it to say that the Government&#x2019;s initiatives to expose curruption and unethical business practices support the State President&#x2019;s commitment to clean administration. The Government will therefore continue to pursue the ideals of clean administration in spirit and in deed.</li>

I have subsequently requested the South African Law Commission to consider including the matter in its programme.<br/>

The hon member is furthermore referred to my oral answer on 14 February 1989.</li>
