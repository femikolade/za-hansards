<heading>Presidency: cost of extensions</heading>

<question by="#soal" to="#minister_of_public_works_and_land_affairs">

<num>90.</num>

<from>Mr P G SOAL</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Public Works and Land Affairs:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether any extensions have been made to the Presidency in Pretoria; if so, (a) at what total cost, (b) (i) when and (ii) in what manner were tenders invited, (c) what were the (i) highest and (ii) lowest tenders received and (d) (i) which tenders were accepted and (ii) why;</li>

<li>(2) whether a survey of existing facilities in Pretoria was made before work on the Presidency began; if not, why not; if so, (a) when and (b) what were the results of the survey;</li>

<li>(3) (a) how many persons can the Presidency accommodate at present and (b) how many State banquets were held there in 1988?</li>

</ol>

<p>B174E</p>

</question>

<answer by="#acting_minister_of_public_works_and_land_affairs" to="#soal">

<from>The ACTING MINISTER OF PUBLIC WORKS AND LAND AFFAIRS:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) No. Extensions are presently being made to the Presidency in Pretoria.

<ol>

<li>(a) The total cost is not yet available.</li>

<li>(b)

<ol>

<li>(i) November 1988</li>

<li>(ii) Tenders were called for from selected contractors</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(c)

<ol>

<li>(i) R3 007 000</li>

<li>(ii) R2 350 000</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(d)

<ol>

<li>(i) The tender for R2 350 000</li>

<li>(ii) As it was the lowest and most favourable tender for the State.</li>

</ol></li>

</ol></li>

<li>(2) No. The executive of the Central Government is seated in Pretoria where the State has a need of an own multi-purpose conference facility which can accommodate 600 persons within a secured area. Facilities for functions should also be available. The basic facilities are available at the Presidency. By adding a conference hall, which can also be used for functions for large groups, the utilitarian value and functional utilization of this historic national asset are enhanced.</li>

<li>(3)

<ol>

<li>(a) A maximum of 180-210 persons can be accommodated in the main sittingroom for meetings/conferences, by placing chairs after the style of a theatre.</li>

<li>(b) None. The existing facility does not lend itself to the presenting of State banquets.</li>

</ol></li>

</ol>

</answer>

</writtenStatements>

<heading>Cape Town: old training college</heading>

<question by="#burrows" to="#minister_of_public_works_and_land_affairs">

<num>189.</num>

<from>Mr R M BURROWS</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Public Works and Land Affairs:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether the building known as the old training college in Queen Victoria Street in Cape Town falls under the control of his Department; if not, under which Government Department does it fall; if so,</li>

<li>(3) whether the building is in use at present; if so, what are the relevant details?</li>

</ol>

<p>B436E</p>

</question>

<answer by="#acting_minister_of_public_works_and_land_affairs" to="#burrows">

<from>The ACTING MINISTER OF PUBLIC WORKS AND LAND AFFAIRS:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) Yes.</li>

<li>(2) No. Planning is in progress for the transformation and refurbishing of the building for the Department of Justice (State Attorney). The provisional estimate is R1,5 million.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading><span class="col_855-856" refersTo="page_0467"/>South African prisons: average population</heading>

<question by="#dalling" to="#minister_of_justice">

<num>239.</num>

<from>Mr D J DALLING</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Justice:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) (a) How many prisoners can be accommodated in South African prisons at present, (b) what was the current daily average prison population as at the latest specified date for which information is available and (c) how many unsentenced prisoners were there in prison in the Republic on that date;</li>

<li>(2) whether any prisons were over-populated in 1988; if so, (a) which prisons and (b) what was the average rate of over-population in each case?</li>

</ol>

<p>B544E</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_justice" to="#dalling">

<from>The MINISTER OF JUSTICE:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1)

<ol>

<li>(a) According to the norm mentioned below, 83 895.</li>

<li>(b) The daily average for December 1988 is 109 924 (see Annexures A and B for analysis of sentences and crimes).</li>

<li>(c) On 31 December 1988 there were 19 997 unsentenced prisoners in South African prisons.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(2) Yes, in the sense that the norm was exceeded. However, as mentioned on various occasions in the past, over-populated prisons is a relative concept. The accommodation figure for South African prisons is determined against a broad norm and the highest standards of hygiene and health are maintained. Everything possible is done to keep prisoners productively occupied and this gives rise to the fact that a large number of prisoners mainly work outside the prison during the day.<br/>Prisoners also have access to spacious courtyards and ample opportunity is given for outdoor recreation. In view thereof, overcrowding as such is not an unmanageable phenomenon. However, attention is given to overcrowding in South African prisons on a continual basis. The situation at prisons with an above average influx of admission due to the temporary circumstances is alleviated by the transfer of prisoners to prisons with a lower occupancy level. The application of this deconcentration policy has resulted in a more even distribution of the prison population and therefore considerable relief has been effected with regard to occupancy levels in general. A further method for utilizing available accommodation more efficiently, is by implementing stacked beds to such an extent that hygiene and health standards are still complied with.

</ol></li>

</ol>

<num>304.</num>

<from>Mr D J DALLING</from>

<p>B644E</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_justice" to="#dalling">

<from>The MINISTER OF JUSTICE:</from>

<p>(a) and (b) Yes. Shortages are experienced in the following vocational groups:</p>

<p>&#x2014; Work study officer</p>

<p>&#x2014; Integrated information system (computer personnel)</p>

<p>&#x2014; Agriculturist</p>

<p>&#x2014; Agricultural technician</p>

<p>&#x2014; Tradesmen</p>

<p>&#x2014; Finance personnel (Accounts clerk)</p>

<p>&#x2014; Logistics personnel (Material supporting clerk)</p>

<p>&#x2014; Musician</p>

<p>&#x2014; Nurse</p>

<p>&#x2014; Pharmacist</p>

<p>&#x2014; Psychologist</p>

<p>&#x2014; Typist</p>

<p>Due to the general under-supply on the labour market, these vacancies simply cannot be filled.</p>

<p>There were no vacancies on the fixed establishment of the disciplinary occupational group, on 1 April 1989. However, the existing fixed establishment as on 1 April 1989 does not reflect the shortage of 5 306 posts which, due to financial realities, are being attended to on a planned and continuous basis by means of the provision of funds on the South African Prisons Service&#x2019;s budget.</p>
