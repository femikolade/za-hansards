<heading>SADF: immovable equipment in South West Africa</heading>

<question by="#langley" to="#minister_of_defence">

<num>1.</num>

<from>Mr T LANGLEY</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Defence:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) What is the total value of immovable defence equipment, including buildings and roads, which has been erected by or on behalf of the South African Defence Force in South West Africa;</li>

<li>(2) what is to be done with it when South Africa withdraws from that territory?</li>

</ol>

</question>

</writtenStatements>

<speech by="#deputy_minister_of_defence">

<from>*The <person refersTo="hansard_za">DEPUTY MINISTER OF DEFENCE</person>:</from>

<p>Mr Speaker, the immovable defence equipment and property referred to by the hon member are regarded as all the fixed assets which were developed, purchased or installed in terms of the capital account and which consequently became part of a specific geographic locality on a relatively permanent basis, including buildings, earthworks, sewerage services, water reticulation, electricity distribution, roadworks, runways, fuel installations and storm-water services. The total value of these structures in South West Africa is estimated at approximately R480 million.</p>

<p>Gen Prem Chand, commander of the UN forces in South West Africa, has just arrived in the area, and is at present visiting all the bases. Initially the UN indicated that they wanted to make use of the SA Defence Force&#x2019;s existing bases. Because of reduced numbers of troops, a re-appraisal is now being made to determine the final needs. Some of these facilities can then be made available to Untag for a consideration.</p>

<p>It is envisaged that airfields and associated services will be leased to Untag. Certain hangars, store-rooms and buildings can be disassembled for reutilization in the Republic of South Africa. A detailed study is being made at present to determine whether the disassembling charges plus the transport costs will make it cost-effective to recover the buildings for reutilization in the Republic of South Africa or whether they should be disposed of locally by means of auctions, sales or transfer.</p>

<p>The development of the infrastructure in the operational area of SWA by the SA Defence Force was essential to enable efficient operations to be carried out. Our soldiers had to have bases that were as convenient as possible. Tarred roads and hard gravel roads were necessary to combat the danger of mines. Runways were of course essential for operations to be carried out. To cling to them simply because they cost money is therefore illogical.</p>

<p>In addition to operational benefits, this infrastructure had many economic and social advantages. It also promoted the creation of additional infrastructure such as schools, hospitals and clinics. In this way the general standard of living of everyone in the area, including the local population, was promoted. How on earth can anyone calculate the price and the value of a road? What is at issue here is the development of opportunities in the area in question. In any case, the SA Defence Force cannot acquire ownership of a road in SWA, but they utilized the full right to use that road. [Time expired.]</p>

</speech>

<p>B313E</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_justice" to="#dalling">

<from>The MINISTER OF JUSTICE:</from>

<p>Last year the hon member for Sandton also asked me about the possible abolition of Latin as a prerequisite for persons wishing to practise as advocates and attorneys. On 8 March 1988 I pointed out in this House that before I exercise my power in terms of section 1 of the Admission of Advocates Amendment Act, 1987 (Act 17 of 1987), to determine a date on which the concession granted in terms of that Act is to cease to apply, I require the viewpoint of the advocature. According to a majority resolution of the General Council of the Bar of South Africa it is proposed that Latin be abolished as a requirement. The various Bars were, however, not unanimous in this regard, and several representations on behalf of individual Bar Councils were once again received advocating the retention of Latin as a requirement for admission as an advocate.</p>

<p>I have, in the meanwhile, had the benefit of a wide range of views, in the course of which the following factors have emerged as most relevant &#x2014;</p>

<ol>

<li>(a) certain provincial division of the Supreme Court of South Africa have held that a special university course in Latin is sufficient <span class="col_255-256" refersTo="page_0167"/>to satisfy the requisites prescribed by law for admission as an advocate;</li>

<li>(b) as a result of the said decisions several large universities have successfully adapted their syllabuses to meet the needs of the legal profession;</li>

<li>(c) a large number of students have planned their curricula accordingly; and</li>

<li>(d) it is undesirable to make Latin I, which for the past 7 years has not been required by some universities and provincial divisions, a requirement once again.</li>

</ol>

<p>A certain standard of Latin is, however, indicated as a requirement for the study of law for the following reasons &#x2014;</p>

<ol>

<li>(a) many of our legal rules are expressed as Latin maxims;</li>

<li>(b) certain standard ideas are succinctly expressed in Latin; and</li>

<li>(c) Latin expressions are part of every-day court language.</li>

</ol>

<p>It has been suggested to me authoritatively that either of the following options, or a combination thereof, is viable &#x2014;</p>

<ol>

<li>(a) Latin at matriculation level as prescribed by the Joint Matriculation Board; or</li>

<li>(b) a special university course in Latin which will entitle a student to proceed to Latin I; or</li>

<li>(c) a special university course in Latin comprising general principles of grammar, legal phrases and expressions, and a Roman legal and cultural overview.</li>

</ol>

<p>The implication is that I am contemplating a recommendation to the Government that Latin I as a compulsory course for admission as an advocate ought not to be re-instated, subject to what I have said above.</p>

<p>I am therefore considering introducing legislation this year, with the above guidelines in mind, if time so permits. Naturally this will be done after interested parties have had the opportunity to study my proposal.</p>

</answer>

<heading>Public servants encouraged to improve qualifications</heading>

<p>2. Mr J V IYMAN asked the Minister of Local Government and Agriculture:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether public servants employed in his Department are encouraged to improve their qualifications; if so, what incentives are given;</li>

<li>(2) whether such public servants who go on study leave receive their normal remuneration?</li>

</ol>

<speech by="#minister_of_local_government_and_agriculture">

<from>The <person refersTo="hansard_za">MINISTER OF LOCAL GOVERNMENT AND AGRICULTURE</person>:</from>

<p>Mr Chairman, the reply to the first part of the question is as follows: Yes, in the case of studies at technikons, registration, tuition and examination fees are paid from State funds. In the case of university studies, Public Service bursaries are offered by the Commission for Administration.</p>

<p>Study leave is granted on a 50-50 basis, for example public servants are entitled to one day&#x2019;s special leave for every day vacation leave approved. In addition, officers studying on a part-time basis are granted a day&#x2019;s special leave for each day on which they write an examination plus a day&#x2019;s special leave to prepare for each such examination.</p>

<p>On successful completion of his course of study the officer will qualify for a cash reward of either one or more salary notches which is equivalent to his next salary increment and which is payable in accordance with the personnel administration standard for the relevant occupational class.</p>

<p>Secondly, the payment of full or part-time remuneration is dependent on the number of days of vacation leave the officer has to his credit, for which purpose study leave is granted on the basis referred to above. If he has no vacation leave to his credit he can obviously not benefit from the 50-50 basis.</p>

</speech>
