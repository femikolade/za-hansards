<heading>Conference/symposium on ozone-depleting chemicals: Montreal</heading>

<question by="#soal" to="#minister_of_foreign_affairs">

<num>*5.</num>

<from>Mr P G SOAL</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Foreign Affairs:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether any representatives of the South African Government attended a conference or symposium on ozone-depleting chemicals held in Montreal in or about September 1987; if not, why not; if so, who were they;</li>

<li>(2) whether South Africa signed a treaty limiting the production and consumption of such chemicals at the time; if not, why not;</li>

<li>(3) whether he will make a statement on the matter?</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_foreign_affairs" to="#soal">

<from>The MINISTER OF FOREIGN AFFAIRS:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) No. South Africa was not invited to attend the conference held in Montreal under the auspices of the United Nationals Environmental Programme (UNEP) since in terms of a UNEP decision of 1980 all forms of co-operation between South Africa and UNEP were suspended.</li>

<li>(2) No. It is assumed that by &#x201C;treaty&#x201D; is meant the Montreal Protocol on Chlorofluorocarbons to the Vienna Convention for the Protection of the Ozone Layer (1985).<br/>In terms of the above-mentioned Convention a state may not become a party to a protocol unless it is or becomes at the same time a party to the Convention. South Africa did not accede to the Vienna Convention and therefore could not sign the Montreal Protocol.</li>

<li>(3) The desirability of assession to the treaties is a matter for the consideration of my colleague the hon the Minister of Environment Affairs.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
