<question by="#soal" to="#minister_of_law_and_order">

<num>*10.</num>

<from>Mr P G SOAL</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Law and Order:</p>

<p>Whether the public relations division of the South African Police issued any warnings and/ or put forward any suggestions to any editors or news editors on or about 7 June 1988 in regard to the publication of information regarding stay-aways, intimidation or any other specified topic; if so, (a) when, (b) why, (c) what was the purport of such warnings or suggestions, (d)(i) in what ways, (ii) on whose instruction and (iii) to which editors or news editors were they issued or put forward and (e) what was the response to each such warning or suggestion?</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_law_and_order" to="#soal">

<from>&#x2020;The MINISTER OF LAW AND ORDER:</from>

<p>(a) to (e)</p>

<p>I wish to point out to the hon member that an excellent relationship of trust exists between the South African Police and the vast majority of the media.</p>

<p>On 6 June 1988 the commander of the Public Relations Division of the South African Police sent a confidential note by telex message through SAPA to all editors and news editors in which media regulations relating to the publication of certain information, was brought to their attention.</p>

<p>In view of an agreement between the South African Police and the Newspaper Press Union, as well as a meeting between these two parties which took place on 25 June 1987, it was decided to advise editors and news editors when it appeared necessary, with regard to reporting that could possibly constitute an offence of the media regulations. This step was welcomed by the media. This advice would, however, not be binding and the final decision to accept or ignore it, would still rest with the editors and news editors.</p>

<p>Up until now no complaints have been received from the media regarding this matter.
