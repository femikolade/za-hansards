<heading>Detainees: access to prison shops</heading>

<question by="#suzman" to="#minister_of_justice">

<num>1319.</num>

<from>Mrs H SUZMAN</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Justice:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether detainees held in prison cells under emergency regulations have access to prison shops; if not, whynot; if so, (a) how frequently, (b) with what products are such shops stocked and (c) who is responsible for setting the prices of the goods sold;</li>

<li>(2) whether goods sold in prison shops are <span class="col_2237-2238" refersTo="page_1188"/>sold for profit; if so, what is the current profit margin;</li>

<li>(3) whether access to prison shops differs with regard to (a) race, (b) (i) age and (ii) health and (c) other specified criteria; if so, (aa) why and (bb) what are the differences in each case;</li>

<li>(4) whether St Albans Prison, Port Elizabeth, complies with the above provisions regarding detainees held under emergency regulations; if not, (a) why not and (b) in what ways do they differ?</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_justice" to="#suzman">

<from>The MINISTER OF JUSTICE:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) Yes.

<ol>

<li>(a) As regularly as possible, within the limits of an orderly prison routine and also taking into account the provisions of Regulation 3 of the Prison Emergency Regulations and the local circumstances at each prison.</li>

<li>(b) It is unfortunately not possible to supply a complete list within the scope of this reply of all items kept in stock. These shops were implemented as tuck shops which stock <i>inter alia</i> canned food, smoking requisites and toiletries and the type of stock is made up of items for which there is a demand and further taking into account, factors such as perishability and availability in general.</li>

<li>(c) As at (2) below.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(2) There are basically two ways in which this service is provided for in prisons, namely:

<ul>

<li>&#x2014; The items ordered by prisoners are bought for the prisoners directly from retail dealers. The amount paid by the prisoners in this instance is the price charged by the retailer. No profit other than that of the retailer is therefore in question.<br/>This procedure is normally followed at those prisons where no Prisons Service Canteens are available.</li>

<li>&#x2014; At those prisons with Prisons Service Canteens, tuck shops are normally run in the prisons as outlets of the Prison Service Canteens. The profit margin for Prison Service Canteens is fixed countrywide at 5&#x0025;, being provision for handling costs and prisoners pay the same prices as the personnel of the South African Prisons Service.</li>

</ul></li>

<li>(3) (a), (b) (i) and (ii) as well as (c).<br/>No.<br/>(aa) and (bb) fall away.</li>

<li>(4) Yes.<br/>(a) and (b) fall away.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<question by="#suzman" to="#minister_of_justice">

<num>1320.</num>

<from>Mrs H SUZMAN</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Justice:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether he has received any representations regarding the conditions under which detainees held under emergency regulations are held in St Albans Prison, Port Elizabeth; if so, (a) how many and (b) (i) from whom (ii) when, and (iii) what was the purport of these representations, in each case;</li>

<li>(2) whether he responded to any of these representations; if not, why not; if so, (a) to whom, (b) when, and (c) what was the purport of his response, in each case;</li>

<li>(3) whether he has conducted any inquiry into the conditions under which such detainees are held at St Albans Prison; if not, why not; if so, (a) when, (b) why and (c) what were the findings?</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_justice" to="#suzman">

<from>The MINISTER OF JUSTICE:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) and (2)<br/>From time to time representations are received from emergency regulation detainees, their legal representatives and/or other persons acting on their behalf. Each such representation receives thorough attention and the person who made the representation is suitably informed on the result of his representation.<br/>Unfortunately the honourable member&#x2019;s question does not mention for which specific period the information is required. However, should she have a specific case in mind, she is welcome to furnish me with the details and I will then have the matter investigated.</li>

<li>(3) Apart from the Judges of the Supreme Court of South Africa who visit emergency regulation detainees, inspection visits <span class="col_2239-2240" refersTo="page_1189"/>by inspectors of prisons, health inspectors, commanding officers, etc. are regularly carried out at all prisons and all matters which have to be attended to are thoroughly investigated and disposed of. <heading>Detainees: minimum area of cell space</heading>

<question by="#suzman" to="#minister_of_justice">

<num>1321.</num>

<from>Mrs H SUZMAN</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Justice:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) What is the minimum area of cell space allocated to each detainee held in prison cells under emergency regulations;</li>

<li>(2) whether such detainees are provided with (a) bedding and (b) mattresses; if not, why not; if so, (i) what bedding and/or mattresses and (ii) what are the dimensions of the mattresses provided;</li>

<li>(3) whether such detainees are provided with (a) warm clothing, (b) eating utensils, (c) hot water and (d) heating where necessary; if not, why not;</li>

<li>(4) whether the provision of such items differs with regard to (a) race, (b) (i) age and (ii) health, (c) detainees held in prison cells as opposed to police cells and (d) other specified criteria; if so, (aa) why and (bb) what are the differences in each case;</li>

<li>(5) whether St Albans Prison, Port Elizabeth, complies with the above provisions regarding detainees held under emergency regulations; if not, (a) why not and (b) in what ways do they differ?</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_justice" to="#suzman">

<from>The MINISTER OF JUSTICE:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) The accommodation norm which is applied in South African prisons is not based on surface space per prisoner alone, but also on the total cubic space per cell taking into account factors such as the age, structure and design of the building and climate concerned. The allocation of detainees per communal cell therefore depends on the said factors and also whether such cells are furnished with conventional beds, stacked beds or sleeping mats with bedding.</li>

<li>(2) (a) and (b), (i) and (ii)<br/>All prisoners, including emergency regulation detainees, are provided with separate bedding which is warm enough and conforms to hygienic requirements.<br/>Items of bedding are as follows:

<ul>

<li>&#x2014; Beds</li>

<li>&#x2014; Mattresses (Sponge): 2 m X 700 mm X 100 mm</li>

<li>&#x2014; Pillows</li>

<li>&#x2014; Sheets (two per prisoner who sleeps in a bed)</li>

<li>&#x2014; Pillow cases</li>

<li>&#x2014; Blankets</li>

<li>&#x2014; Bedspreads</li>

<li>&#x2014; Sisal mats*</li>

<li>&#x2014; Felt mats*<br/>* These items are supplied where beds are not provided.</li>

</ul></li>

<li>(3)

<ol>

<li>(a) As is the case with unsentenced prisoners, emergency regulation detainees wear their own clothes and their families and friends may give the detainees additional clothing during regular visits according to the needs of the season. However, if a detainee&#x2019;s clothes are insufficient, prison clothes which conform to certain requirements are provided on request.</li>

<li>(b) Yes.</li>

<li>(c) Yes, where available.</li>

<li>(d) Taking into account the general climate of the Republic of South Africa as well as the type of clothing which is available to prisoners, additional artificial heating is not provided. The number of blankets provided to prisoners is however varied according to climatic conditions.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(4)

<ol>

<li>(a) No.</li>

<li>(b)

<ol>

<li>(i) No.</li>

<li>(ii) Physicians&#x2019; prescriptions are strictly adhered to in all cases.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(c) The honourable member is referred to the reply to question 1315 put to the Minister of Law and Order and which should be read in conjunction with the details furnished in this reply.</li>

<li><span class="col_2241-2242" refersTo="page_1190"/>(d) All emergency regulation detainees entrusted to the care of the South African Prisons Service, are treated in a uniform and humane manner strictly in accordance with the stipulations of the Prisons Act, 1959 (Act No 8 of 1959) and the applicable Regulations.

<ol>

<li>(aa) and (bb) fall away.</li>

</ol></li>

</ol></li>

<li>(5) Yes

<ol>

<li>(a) and (b) fall away.</li>

</ol></li>

</ol>

</answer>
