<heading>Foreign Affairs: role interpreters</heading>

<question by="#derby-lewis" to="#minister_of_foreign_affairs">

<num>1031.</num>

<from>Mr C J DERBY-LEWIS</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Foreign Affairs:&#x2020;</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) (a) What are the duties of the person holding the post of role interpreter in his Department, (b) (i) how many persons have been appointed to this post and (ii) for what period are they appointed and (c) what qualifications are required for the post;</li>

<li>(2) whether role interpreters receive any fringe benefits; if so, in what respects do such fringe benefits differ from those of their colleagues in his Department?</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_foreign_affairs" to="#derby-lewis">

<from>The MINISTER OF FOREIGN AFFAIRS:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) (a) Role interpreters perform duties of an auxiliary nature in the fields of information and representation at a number of overseas missions. Professionally qualified and experienced persons are appointed in these posts.

<ol>

<li>(b) (i) Seven.

<ol>

<li>(ii) The period for which role interpreters are appointed varies from case to case and is influenced by the nature of the duties attached to each post. Contracts apply as a rule for three or four years but can be extended when this is deemed necessary.</li>

</ol>

</li>

<li>(c) Qualifications required depend on the functions and needs of each post. In general terms, professional qualifications and/or appropriate experience and expert knowledge in a particular field or fields are required.</li>

</ol>

</li>

<li>(2) Role interpreters receive the same fringe benefits as other officials of equivalent rank who serve in South African missions abroad.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
