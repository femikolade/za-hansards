<heading>REPORTS OF STANDING SELECT COMMITTEE</heading>

<p>Mr D P A SCHUTTE, as Chairman, presented the Third Report of the Standing Select Committee on Justice, dated 16 March 1988, as follows:</p>

<block name="quote">The Standing Committee on Justice having considered the subject of the South African Law Commission&#x2019;s Report on Committal to Prison in respect of Debt, referred to it, your Committee begs to report as follows:</block>

<ol>

<li>(a) The recommendation of the South African Law Commission in paragraph 4.1 of its Report that the principle that non-compliance with an order for payment of a sum of money or instalment orders constitutes contempt of court, be done away with, is not supported <i>in toto</i> by your Committee.</li>

<li>(b) Your Committee supports the South African Law Commission&#x2019;s recommendations&#x2014;

<ol>

<li>(i) in paragraph 4.2 of its Report that provision continue to be made for a financial enquiry in order to enable the creditor to ascertain the judgment debtor&#x2019;s assets, that it be compulsory for the judgment debtor to attend this enquiry and that he should be brought before the court by means of a warrant if he fails to appear;</li>

<li>(ii) in paragraph 4.3 of its Report that provision continue to be made for an instalment order arising from a financial enquiry and that the judgment debtor may be called upon anew to attend a financial enquiry in the event of non-compliance with the order; and</li>

<li>(iii) in paragraph 4.4 of its Report that non-appearance by a debtor if he is called upon to appear at any financial enquiry or any postponement thereof constitute a criminal offence and be made punishable.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(c) Your Committee further recommends that the existing procedure be so adapted as to make provision that committal to prison on account of contempt of court for failure to comply with an order for payment of a sum of money be limited to those cases in which there was intentional contempt of court whilst the judgment debtor was financially in a position to pay his debt and that such committal to prison only be made in the presence of the judgment debtor.</li>

<li>(d) Your Committee further recommends that the effect of the decision in <i>Quentin&#x0027;s</i> v <i>Komane</i> 1983(2) SA 775(T), in terms of which a judgment debtor may only be referred for committal to prison once, be reviewed.</li>

<li>(e) Your Committee further recommends that the procedure prescribed by section 65 of the Magistrates&#x2019; Courts Act, 1944 (Act No 32 of 1944), be revised with a view to reducing the costs connected thereto.</li>

<li>(f) In conclusion your Committee recommends that amendments to legislation be prepared in order to implement the abovementioned proposals.</li>

</ol>

<p>Report to be considered.</p>

<p>Mr D P A SCHUTTE, as Chairman, presented the Fourth Report of the Standing Select Committee on Justice, dated 17 March 1988, as follows:</p>

<block name="quote">The Standing Committee on Justice having considered the subject of the <i>Law of Evidence Amendment Bill</i> [B 33&#x2014;88 (GA)], referred to it, your Committee begs to report the Bill with amendments [B 33A&#x2014;88 (GA)].<br/>Your Committee further recommends that the Minister of Justice be requested to refer clause 3, which was negatived by the Standing Committee, to the South African Law Commission for further enquiry.</block>
