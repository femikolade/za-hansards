<heading>Decentralization incentive benefits removed/ reduced</heading>

<question by="#hulley" to="#minister_of_constitutional_development_and_planning">

<num>223.</num>

<from>Mr R R HULLEY</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Constitutional Development and Planning:</p>

<p>Whether any industries that have been awarded decentralization incentive benefits (a) have had these incentive benefits (i) removed and (ii) reduced, (b) were place-bound industries and (c) have since ceased to operate; if so, (aa) how many in each case and (bb) in respect of what date is this information furnished?</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_constitutional_development_and_planning" to="#hulley">

<from>The MINISTER OF CONSTITUTIONAL DEVELOPMENT AND PLANNING:</from>

<ol>

<li>(a)

<ol>

<li>(i) In the case of 110 firms, the incentives which they enjoyed were phased out over a period of two years ended on 31 August 1987 and, in the case of one company, on 30 June 1987. In the case of a number of firms falling under the sawmilling industry the incentives were withdrawn only in respect of a part of their production processes, namely wet sawmilling, which does no longer qualify for incentives while certain secondary stages of processing do qualify.</li>

<li>(ii) In the case of a further 130 firms, certain components of the incentive <span class="col_393-394" refersTo="page_0276"/>package granted to them were, or will be phased out. In the case of producers of certain cement products and tombstone manufacturers the transport rebates were withdrawn on 31 August 1987 and in the case of industries established at Coloured and Indian preference employment points, the long term incentives will be withdrawn on 29 February 1988.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(b) The withdrawal of the incentives of 92 firms mentioned in (a) (i) above and of 38 of the firms mentioned in (a) (ii) above, in other words, a total of 130 firms, were effected in terms of multilateral guidelines on incentives for raw material orientated and locality-bound industries, as well as on the basis of a decision by the Government in 1984 to phase out the incentives of existing industries falling in the two categories.</li>

<li>(c)

<ol>

<li>(aa) The Decentralisation Board is not aware of any firm closing down its operations as a direct result of having to forfeit part or all of its decentralization incentives.</li>

<li>(bb) from 1 July 1984 to 19 February 1988.</li>

</ol></li>

</ol>

</answer>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_constitutional_development_and_planning" to="#walsh">
