<heading>Sites B/C in Khayelitsha: self-help facilities</heading>

<question by="#walsh" to="#minister_of_constitutional_development_and_planning">

<num>224.</num>

<from>Mr J J WALSH</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Constitutional Development and Planning:</p>

<p>Whether any self-help facilities aimed at improving properties in Sites B and C in Khayelitsha are being planned or in operation; if so, what is the nature of these facilities?</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_constitutional_development_and_planning" to="#walsh">

<from>The MINISTER OF CONSTITUTIONAL DEVELOPMENT AND PLANNING:</from>

<p>Yes.</p>

<p>A request was directed to the National Housing Commission for R3,5 million for the 1988/89 financial year for the building of 500 houses by means of a self-help programme.</p>

<p>In the 1987/88 financial year an amount of R750 000 was made available for self-help in Khayelitsha. This amount, however, was transferred to Old Cross Roads because of the greater need there. From this source, loans of R7 000 each at 6&#x0025; interest for 30 years will be made available to inhabitants of Old Cross Roads to either build or have their homes built in the upgraded phases.</p>

</answer>

<heading>South African prisons: prisoners</heading>

<question by="#dalling" to="#minister_of_justice">

<num>331.</num>

<from>Mr D J DALLING</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Justice:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1)

<ol>

<li>(a) How many prisoners can be accommodated in South African prisons at present, (b) what was the current daily average prison population as at the latest specified date for which information is available and (c) how many unsentenced prisoners were there in prison in the Republic on that date;</li>

</ol>

</li>

<li>(2) whether any prisons were over-populated in 1987; if so, (a) which prisons and (b) what was the average rate of over-population in each case?</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_justice" to="#dalling">

<from>The MINISTER OF JUSTICE:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1)

<ol>

<li>(a) According to the norm mentioned below, 84 802.</li>

<li>(b) The daily average for December 1987 is 103 200 (see annexures A and B for analysis of sentences and crimes).</li>

<li>(c) On 31 December 1987 there were <span class="col_523-524" refersTo="page_0341"/>21 674 unsentenced prisoners in South African prisons.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(2) Yes, in the sense that the norm was exceeded. However, as mentioned in my replies to questions No 12 of 6 March 1984, No 103 of 15 April 1986 and No 246 of 4 March 1987 over-populated prisons is a relative concept. The accommodation figure for South African prisons is determined against a broad norm and the highest standards of hygiene and health are maintained. Everything possible is done to keep prisoners productively occupied and this gives rise to the fact that a large number of prisoners mainly work outside the prison during the day. Prisoners also have access to spacious courtyards and ample opportunity is given for outdoor recreation. In view thereof, overcrowding as such is not an unmanageable phenomenon. However, attention is given to overcrowding in South African prisons on a continual basis. The situation at prisons with an above average influx of admissions due to temporary circumstances is alleviated by the transfer of prisoners to prisons with a lower occupancy level. The application of this deconcentration policy has resulted in a more even distribution of the prison population and therefore considerable relief has been effected with regard to occupancy levels in general. A further method for utilizing available accommodation more efficiently, is by implementing stacked beds to such an extent that hygiene and health standards are still complied with.

</ol></li>

</ol>

<li>(3) The following criteria are taken into account when the subsidisation of commuters is considered:<br/>whether the need exists for workers to be transported from their place of residence to their place of employment;<br/>the unemployment situation prevailing in the area where employment opportunities exist;<br/>whether the place where workers are to be transported from is a resettlement area, township or squatter camp;<br/>whether rail facilities exist for commuting purposes and, if not, what the possibilities are for the provision of such facilities in the near future;<br/>the financial position of the bus company which intends providing the service; and<br/>whether the bus company is capable of providing and maintaining a reliable and efficient service by availing itself of the necessary servicing and repair facilities and infrastructure.<br/>After determining what portion of the economic fare the commuter can afford to pay from his wages, the subsidy, being the difference between the economic fare and the portion thereof which is affordable by the commuter, is calculated.</li>
