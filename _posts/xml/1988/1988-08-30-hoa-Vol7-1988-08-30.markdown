<heading>International sporting events: tax rebates for sponsors</heading>

<question by="#andrew" to="#minister_of_national_education">

<num>*1.</num>

<from>Mr K M ANDREW</from>

<p>asked the Minister of National Education:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether any changes were effected or are to be effected in respect of tax rebates for sponsors of international sporting events; if so, (a) what changes, (b) when and (c) why;</li>

<li>(2) what are or were the criteria for, according international status to, a sporting event and so enabling the sponsor of that event to qualify for a tax rebate;</li>

<li>(3) who makes the decision as to whether a particular event qualifies for a tax rebate for its sponsor;</li>

<li>(4) (a) how many sporting events qualified for such rebates over the latest specified five-year period for which informaton is available and (b) what total amount was allowed in such tax rebates over this period?</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_national_education" to="#andrew">

<from>&#x2020;The MINISTER OF NATIONAL EDUCATION:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) Yes.

<ol>

<li>(a) Section 18B of the Income Tax Act, Act 58 of 1962, is not to be amended. The policy in accordance with which the provisions of section 18B are applied has, however, been amended.</li>

<li>(b) 1 April 1988.</li>

<li>(c) The policy has been amended to ensure that the loss of revenue by the <span class="col_2193-2194" refersTo="page_1166"/>Government as a result of section 18B will remain realistic.</li>

</ol></li>

<heading>Beer: evasion of GST</heading>

<question by="#derby-lewis" to="#minister_of_finance">

<num>*10.</num>

<from>Mr C J DERBY-LEWIS</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Finance:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether he or his Department has been informed of and/or received any complaints about alleged general sales tax (GST) evasions involving beer purchased GST-free in Rosslyn near Pretoria for business undertakings in Bophuthatswana and subsequently transported to South Africa for sale at reduced prices; if so,</li>

<li>(2) whether he has taken any steps in this regard; if not, why not; if so, (a) what steps and (b) when;</li>

<li>(3) whether he will make a statement on the matter?</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_finance" to="#derby-lewis">

<from>The MINISTER OF FINANCE:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) The Inland Revenue Branch of the Department of Finance is aware of such malpractices.</li>

<li>(2) In terms of regulations made in July 1987 under the Sales Tax Act, 1978, as amended with effect from 1 August 1987, liquor traders from neighbouring states may no longer take possession of liquor in South Africa free of sales tax. It is only where liquor sold in South Africa is delivered or consigned <i>by the seller</i> to the purchaser&#x2019;s address outside South Africa and proof of such delivery or consignment is kept by the seller, that the sale of such liquor is exempt from sales tax. Whenever reports of malpractices in that regard have been received by Inland Revenue, an investigation has been launched, resulting to date in payment of well over R1 million in sales tax and penalty being claimed <span class="col_2201-2202" refersTo="page_1170"/>from the liquor traders concerned. Further investigations are still in progress.</li>

<li>(3) In regard to liquor which is exported from South Africa and then finds its way back into South Africa, there is a clear charge to sales tax in respect of the importation into South Africa of such liquor. However, the lack of clearly defined borders and border control measures such as between some of South Africa&#x2019;s neighbouring states makes effective enforcement of the provisions of the Sales Tax Act extremely difficult. This is a problem which is receiving consideration in the drafting of legislation with a view to introducing the value-added tax recently announced by the Government.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
