<heading>INDIANS EDUCATION BILL</heading>

<p>First Order read: Committee Stage,&#x2014;Indians Education Bill.</p>

<speech by="#steenkamp">

<from>*Dr. <person refersTo="hansard_za">STEENKAMP</person>:</from>

<p>I move&#x2014;</p>

<block name="quote">That the Committee of the Whole House on the Indians Education Bill have leave to consider the advisability of extending the provisions of the Bill to any person who in fact is or is generally accepted as a member of the race or class known as Zanzibari Arabs (also known as Zanzibari or Kiwas) and any person who in fact is, or is generally accepted as, a member of a race or tribe whose national home is in any country or area in Asia other than China, India or Pakistan.</block>

<p>This motion is in connection with the legislation before us, the Indians Education Bill, and it further relates to the definition of &#x201C;Indian&#x201D;. The definition of &#x201C;Indian&#x201D; in this Bill reads as follows: &#x201C; &#x2018;Indian&#x2019; means a person classified in terms of the Population Registration Act, 1950 (Act No. 30 of 1950) as a member of the Indian group&#x201D;. After that legislation followed, in terms of Section 5 of that Act, announcements in the <i>Government Gazette</i> in regard to the definition of groups of, as that Act stated, Coloured persons, to wit in <i>Government Gazette</i> No. 6191 of 1959, and a republication of it in <i>Government Gazette</i> No. 67) of 1963, and also in the last-mentioned year a republication of <i>Gazette</i> No. 27 of 1961 in connection with the definition of Zanzibar Arabs and other Asiatic groups. In terms of these publications in the <i>Government Gazettes</i> the Indian group is described as follows. It is paragraph 5 of that announcement in the <i>Government Gazette</i>&#x2014;</p>

<block name="quote">&#x201C;Indian group&#x201D; includes any person who is in fact a member of a race or tribe whose national home is in India or Pakistan, or who usually passes for such.</block>

<p>It therefore appears to me that the term &#x201C;Indian&#x201D; in this Indians Education Bill may be interpreted as referring only to those Indians or Asiatics coming from or who have as their national homes India and/or Pakistan. In South Africa there are, however, related groups of persons who do not fit in with the definition of &#x201C;Indian&#x201D; or &#x201C;Indian group&#x201D; which I have just read, as coming from India or from Pakistan. In that regard I may just refer to groups of persons who are in this country, coming for example from Ceylon, Burma, Afghanistan, Nepal, Thailand, Iran and Iraq. The State also realized this difficulty. It realized that there are or may be other related groups of persons in South Africa, and it in fact provided for them in the following paragraph, paragraph 6 of the aforementioned <i>Government Gazette</i> of 1959 and again later in 1963, under the heading &#x201C;Group of Other Asiatics&#x201D;. Under this heading &#x201C;Group of Other Asiatics&#x201D; the position becomes clearer to us in the definition which reads as follows&#x2014;</p>

<block name="quote">The group &#x201C;other Asiatics&#x201D; includes any person who is in fact a member of a race or tribe whose national home is in any country or territory in Asia other than China, or Pakistan, or who usually passes for such.</block>

<p>In other words, it provides for the other Asiatics who do not come from India, Pakistan or China, for which special provision is made. The same extension of the definition was also made, or was republished in 1963, in the paragraph affecting the Zanzibari Arabs, which was published in 1961. It is, with respect, quite clear in my opinion that the word &#x201C;Indian&#x201D; as used in this Indians Education Bill is too limited. It seems to me that the schools under the new set-up may be restricted to only those originating from Pakistan or India, or who had their homes in Pakistan or India. In other words, no provision is being made for the children of the group or groups of &#x201C;other Asiatics&#x201D;, to which I have already referred, except of course the Chinese for whom separate provision was made in the aforementioned <i>Government Gazette.</i> Therefore, in order to provide for all Indians, from wherever they might have come, and for all other Asiatics who do not only come from India or Pakistan, or who do not regard only India or Pakistan as their homes, I feel that we should extend the definition of &#x201C;Indian&#x201D; or of &#x201C;Indian group&#x201D;, as in fact was also indicated in the definition of the &#x201C;group of other Asiatics&#x201D; which I have already read, and as asked for by me in this instruction, and also in my proposed amendment to Clause 1&#x2014;</p>

<block name="quote">In line 6, page 4, after &#x201C;group&#x201D;, to add &#x201C;and the other Asiatic Group&#x201D;.</block>

<p>If we do that I feel it will be wide enough to include in this legislation all those groups which are known or were known to us as Indians. Therefore I move.</p>

</speech>

<speech by="#minister_of_indian_affairs">

<from>*The <person refersTo="hansard_za">MINISTER OF INDIAN AFFAIRS</person>:</from>

<p>The position is as stated by the hon. member for Hillbrow (Dr. Steenkamp), namely, that a distinction is made in the Population Registration Act between Indians and Indian groups and other Asiatics etc. I do not want to read the provision because he has already done so. He has quoted it quite correctly. The position is, however, that there are two reasons why I cannot accept this amendment.</p>

<p>The first is that this Bill deals with the education of the Indian group and the intention is ultimately, in conformity with Government policy, to place the control over the education of this group in the hands of the Indian Council which will be established in due course. If provision were made in this Bill that other <span class="col_4929-4930" refersTo="page_1077"/>groups which are really not Indians could be brought in under Indian education, you would ultimately have another minority group under the control of the Indian Council which I do not think would be fair. I do not think the Indian Council should eventually have power over groups of people who are not really Indians. That was also the reason why, at the time the Department of Indian Affairs was established, it was stated specifically that the Department of Indian Affairs would concern itself with the interests of the Indian communities in South Africa.</p>

</speech>

It is also similar to the section in the Coloured Education Act, and which remained there after the Minister of Coloured Affairs at the time investigated the matter properly with his law advisers and found that it was in fact necessary to put it in this form in order to provide for all the possibilities.</p>
