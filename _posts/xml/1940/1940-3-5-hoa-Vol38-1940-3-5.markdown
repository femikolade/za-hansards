<heading>&#x201C;Sprinz&#x201D; Estate.</heading>

<question by="#derbyshire" to="#minister_of_lands">

<num>I.</num>

<from>Mr. DERBYSHIRE</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Lands:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether the purchasers in 1933 of the farm &#x201C;Langespruit&#x201D; (subsequently known as the &#x201C;Sprinz Estate&#x201D;), situated in the Stanger district, Natal, included any persons who were at that time nationals of the German Reich;</li>

<li>(2) what are the names of the persons who acquired the farm &#x201C;Langespruit&#x201D; from the estate of the late J. F. Thring in 1933;</li>

<li>(3) whether any of the owners who were German nationals have disposed of their interests in the &#x201C;Sprinz Estate&#x201D; since 1933; if so,</li>

<li>(4) whether any change as to the registered owners has been recorded in the Deeds Registry Office, Natal; and</li>

<li>(5) what are the names of the present registered owners of the &#x201C;Sprinz Estate.&#x201D;</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_lands" to="#derbyshire">

<from><span class="col_2749-2750" refersTo="page_0047"/>The MINISTER OF LANDS:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1)</li>

<li>(2) and.</li>

<li>(3) Remainder of Lange-spruit, in extent 5,742 acres, was transferred in 1933 from Estate J. S. Thring to African German Estate and Investment Corporation, Limited, the Directors of which were F. Brorson, H. D. Bowker, J. M. Gordon, P. Smit, A. F. A. Merensky, T. E. Lawlor and J. Telford. Portion of Langespruit, called Mayfield, in extent 500 acres, was transferred in 1933 to James F. Thring. Sub-division (1) of Mayfield, in extent 3 acres, was transferred in 1935 to M. A. Kajee. I have no information at my disposal as to whether the purchasers of the 5,742 acres were at that time nationals of the German Reich.</li>

<li>(4) The land was transferred in 1934 from the African German Estate and Investment Corporation, Limited, to Doornkop Sugar Estates, Limited.</li>

<li>(5) Doornkop Sugar Estates, Limited.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>S.A. Broadcasting Corporation: Contract with African Theatres Trust.</heading>

<p>The MINISTER OF POSTS AND TELEGRAPHS replied to Question VII by Mr. Marwick, standing over from 27th February.</p>

<question by="#marwick" to="#minister_of_posts_and_telegraphs">

<heading>Question:</heading>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether his attention has been directed to the extent to which the funds provided by the listeners of the South African Broadcasting Corporation are being used to provide free orchestral service to the African Theatres Trust;</li>

<li>(2) whether the Director of Broadcasting, Johannesburg, has entered into an agreement whereby the services of the Broadcasting Orchestra were rendered available to the African Theatres for a series of Sunday evening concerts; if so, when were those concerts to take place;</li>

<li>(3) whether the services of the Broadcasting Orchestra of approximately 60 persons were by such agreement to be provided gratis for such Sunday evening concerts;</li>

<li>(4) (a) what sum was the African Theatres entitled to charge for a seat at these Sunday evening concerts, (b) whether they collected such charges for their own benefit in respect of the concerts which have already taken place, and (c) what was the total amount collected;</li>

<li>(5) whether any proportion of the admission charges accrued to the African Broadcasting Corporation; if so, what proportion; and</li>

<li>(6) whether he will lay upon the Table the contract between the African Theatres and the Director of Broadcasting on the subject mentioned.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_posts_and_telegraphs" to="#marwick">

<heading>Reply:</heading>

<ol>

<li>(1), (2) and (3) A co-operative arrangement was made at the request of Dr. Van der Byl, the Chairman of the Johannesburg Orchestral Society intended to organise orchestral performances on a national basis. It makes available an orchestra of 78 professional players, of which the Broadcasting Corporation supplies 50, and the African Consolidated Theatres 28. The Corporation is allowed to broadcast these performances, including any artists of note who appear, without charge.</li>

<li>(4) The charges for admission to the concerts ranged from 1s. to 5s. 6d. This was to cover the cost of advertising, hire of hall, lights, attendance, etc. There was a substantial loss on the first two concerts.</li>

<li>(5) No.</li>

<li>(6) There is no contract.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
