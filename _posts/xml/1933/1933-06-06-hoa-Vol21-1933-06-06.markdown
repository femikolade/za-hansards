<heading>Land Bank: Arrear Interest.</heading>

<question by="#van_coller" to="#minister_of_finance">

<num>I.</num>

<from>Mr. VAN COLLER</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Finance:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) What was the amount of arrear interest (more than six months in arrear) on mortgages to the Land Bank as at the 31st December last;</li>

<li>(2) how many farms were held by the Land Bank on the 31st December last due to the failure of the owners to meet their commitments to the said bank and surrendered by such owners;</li>

<li>(3) what amount of capital in the way of loans is represented by the above holdings;</li>

<li>(4) how many farms mortgaged to the bank were sold by the bank prior to the 31st December last; and</li>

<li>(5) what loss of capital was suffered by the bank as a result of such safes, if any?</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#acting_minister_of_finance" to="#van_coller">

<from><span class="col_345-346" refersTo="page_0184"/>The ACTING MINISTER OF FINANCE:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) The total amount of arrear interest as at 31st December last was &#x00A3;265,422. It cannot be ascertained in the time available what portion of this amount is more than six months in arrear.</li>

<li>(2) 140 (including insolvencies).</li>

<li>(3) &#x00A3;175,624 (including interest and costs).</li>

<li>(4) 773 since the inception of the bank in 1912.</li>

<li>(5) The separate account kept by the bank of all properties bought in shows a profit up to the present.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>Fruit Subsidy.</heading>

<question by="#du_toit" to="#minister_of_agriculture">

<num>II.</num>

<from>Mr. R. J. DU TOIT</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Agriculture:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) What is the total amount of levy funds paid to (a) the S.A. Co-operative Citrus Exchange, (b) the S.A. Co-operative Deciduous Fruit Exchange during the past five years;</li>

<li>(2) what is the total amount from the same source during the same period paid to the Perishable Products Control Board;</li>

<li>(3) what is the reason for fruit subsidy payments being altered from an f.o.b. basis to a measurement basis;</li>

<li>(4) what provision has been made in the Estimates for research work in regard to the following problems, viz., (a) the ascertaining of the optimum soil moisture content of different soil types for different crops, with particular reference to fruit varieties, (b) the ascertaining of the correct stage of ripeness for the picking of different varieties of fruit for export purposes, (c) the ascertaining of the carrying quality of grapes grown (i) without irrigation, (ii) under irrigation;</li>

<li>(5) what steps are being taken by the Department of Agriculture with regard to reducing the costs of production; and</li>

<li>(6) whether the advisability of the adoption of the American Farm Bureau system has been considered?</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_agriculture" to="#du_toit">

<from>The MINISTER OF AGRICULTURE:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) The following amounts were paid out during the period 1st April, 1928, to the 31st March, 1933: (a) &#x00A3;98,600; (b) &#x00A3;37,341 out of the ordinary levy, and &#x00A3;39,666 out of the special levy.</li>

<li>(2) &#x00A3;10,350 out of the citrus levy and &#x00A3;6,845 out of the deciduous levy.</li>

<li>(3) The only change was a percentage alteration of the amount of the subsidy.</li>

<li>(4) No special provision has been made for research work in connection with any one of the three problems mentioned but work in this direction is being carried out by the Department of Agriculture in the ordinary course of its activities. The problems mentioned will form the subject of discussion at the forthcoming conference between the research committee of the Deciduous Fruit Exchange and the Department of Agriculture.</li>

<li>(5) Costs of production are determined by a variety of factors such as wages, rent, interest, size of farm, size of crop, etc., over which the Department has no control. The Department of Agriculture also investigates the business side of farming and furnishes expert advice by means of its publications, lectures and its extension service in order to make farming more efficient.</li>

<li>(6) A similar system in effect also exists in South Africa inasmuch as South African farmers&#x2019; associations and unions correspond to a large extent with the American farm bureau, and the South African extension officers with the American county agents.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
