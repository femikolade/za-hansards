<heading>Mealie Industry Control Board.</heading>

<question by="#egeland" to="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry">

<num>I.</num>

<from>Mr. EGELAND</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Agriculture and Forestry:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) How many members at present constitute the Mealie Industry Control Board;</li>

<li>(2) what interests do such members respectively represent;</li>

<li>(3) on how many occasions and on what dates has the full Board been called together during the period from 1st July to 31st December, 1942;</li>

<li>(4) how many members of the staff of the Board have been seconded to deal with the issue of maize permits under the existing regulations; and</li>

<li>(5) what is the total cost of the administration including salaries of all matters entrusted to the Board.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry" to="#egeland">

<from>The MINISTER OF AGRICULTURE AND FORESTRY:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) 19 excluding advisory members.</li>

<li>(2) There are six representatives each of co-operative and non-co-operative producers, one representative each of merchants, brokers, exporters, millers, stockfeeders and consumers, and one officer of the Department of Agriculture and Forestry.</li>

<li>(3) The Board met once only during this period, but several meetings were held by a special committee appointed to deal with urgent questions arising during the present abnormal conditions.</li>

<li>(4) 71.</li>

<li>(5) &#x00A3;42,702 for the period 1st May, 1941, to 30th April, 1942. These figures have, however, not yet been finally audited.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>Workmen&#x2019;s Compensation Act: Prisoners Of War.</heading>

<p>The MINISTER OF LABOUR replied to Question LXIII by Mr. Sonnenberg standing over from 22nd January:</p>

<question by="#sonnenberg" to="#minister_of_labour">

<heading>Question:</heading>

<p>Whether he will state the policy his Department intends adopting in regard to the inclusion under the Workmen&#x2019;s Compensation Act, 1941, of prisoners of war (a) who are employed by agriculturists and others under a contract of employment with the Government, and (b) who while employed as (i) agricultural workers and (ii) workers in industrial undertakings, come in contact with power-driven machinery such as tractors, lorries, threshing machines, etc.</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_labour" to="#sonnenberg">

<heading>Reply:</heading>

<p>Prisoners of war are not workmen within the meaning of the Workmen&#x2019;s Compensation Act and I do not consider that the question of compensation for injuries sustained by prisoners of war while working could appropriately be dealt with under that Act. The treatment of prisoners of war is dealt with by the International Prisoners of War Convention of 1929, in terms of which prisoners are entitled to food, clothing, accommodation and medical treatment. This applies equally to prisoners who are injured while performing work for any person in terms of a contract between such person and the Government.</p>

</answer>
