<heading>Railways and Harbours : Voluntary Provident Fund</heading>

<question by="#marwick" to="#minister_of_transport">

<num>I.</num>

<from>Mr. MARWICK</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Transport :</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether the South African Railways and Harbours (Natal Province) <span class="col_7435-7436" refersTo="page_0066"/>Employees&#x2019; Voluntary Provident Fund is under the control of the Railway Administration; if so,</li>

<li>(2) whether the Administration charges a commission for the collection of the funds; if so, at what rate;</li>

<li>(3) whether the Administration charges a sum of &#x00A3;100 per annum against the Fund; if so, on what grounds;</li>

<li>(4) whether the services of a secretary and a typist together with the monthly rental of an office are debited against the Fund; if so, what are the amounts debited;</li>

<li>(5) whether contributions made by members of the Fund to the Children&#x2019;s Home are collected through the paysheets; if so, at what rate of commission ;</li>

<li>(6) whether any commission is charged by the Administration for the collection of funds from railwaymen contributed towards (a) the Governor-General&#x2019;s Fund or (b) the Railway Mission Fund; and</li>

<li>(7) whether the Minister will consider the desirability of the discontinuance of charges for commission upon all amounts collected through the paysheets or the reduction of the rate?</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_transport" to="#marwick">

<from>The MINISTER OF TRANSPORT:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) No, except that the General Manager appoints the Chairman of the Committee in which the control of the Fund is vested.</li>

<li>(2) Yes, in common with all other benefit funds a commission of 2&#x00BD; per cent. is charged when subscriptions are collected through the medium of paysheets.</li>

<li>(3) No.</li>

<li>(4) Disbursements from the Fund are made on instructions from the Secretary. The Administration prepares the cheques in favour of beneficiaries and for working expenses, the latter, I understand, including rental and the services of the Fund&#x2019;s staff.</li>

<li>(5) Yes, if the contributors are in the employ of the Administration, but no commission is charged.</li>

<li>(6) No.</li>

<li>(7) No, in view of the considerable accounting work caused by the collection of subscriptions through the medium of paysheets.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>Percentage Profit Allowed on Agricultural Products</heading>

<p>The MINISTER OF AGRICULTURE AND FORESTRY replied to Question No. XVI by Mr. Tothill standing over from 25th April :</p>

<question by="#tothill" to="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry">

<heading>Question:</heading>

<p>What percentage profit is allowed (a) the producer or manufacturer, (b) the agent and (c) the retailer on (i) the various grades of mutton, beef and pork, (ii) deciduous fruit, (iii) citrus fruit, (iv) potatoes and onions, (v) other vegetables, (vi) wheat, (vii) maize, (viii) jams and canned fruits, (ix) cheese, (x) milk and (xi) cold storage and fresh eggs.</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry" to="#tothill">

<heading>Reply:</heading>

<ol>

<li>(a) No producers&#x2019; prices have been fixed in respect of (ii), (iii), (iv), (v), (x) and (xi). For (vi) a percentage profit of 7 per cent. is allowed to the producer, and for (i) and (vii) no profit has been assessed.<br/>In respect of (viii) the manufacturer is allowed a gross profit of 23&#x00BD; per cent. and 22&#x00BD; per cent. by the Price Controller and for (ix) an average of 4 per cent. on cost.</li>

<li>(b)

<ol>

<li>(i) 2&#x00BD; per cent. commission.</li>

<li>(ii) Uncontrolled but normally 5 per cent. to 10 per cent. commission.</li>

<li>(iii) Uncontrolled but average commission of 5&#x2153; per cent.</li>

<li>(iv) and (v) Uncontrolled.</li>

<li>(vi) Gross agency fee of 9d. per bag plus 4d. per bag per month storage.</li>

<li>(vii) Gross agency fee of 5d. per bag plus 2d. per bag per month storage where stored more than two months.</li>

<li>(viii) Nil.</li>

<li>(ix) 4 &#x00BD; per cent. commission.</li>

<li>(x) Nil.</li>

<li>(xi) Nil.</li>

</ol>

</li>

<li><span class="col_7439-7440" refersTo="page_0068"/>(c)

<li>(ii) Uncontrolled except that maximum retail price for grapes has been fixed for Cape Peninsula.</li>

<li>(iv) Maximum gross margin of 5s. per bag on potatoes; onions are uncontrolled.</li>

<li>(v) Uncontrolled.</li>

<li>(vi) Bakers are allowed a profit of 7 &#x00BD; per cent. on capital in addition to other costs.</li>

<li>(ix) 3d. per lb.</li>

<li>(x) Prices vary for different centres but for the Witwatersrand the gross distributor&#x2019;s margin for retail quantities delivered is 14d. per gallon on a producer&#x2019;s price of 20d. per gallon delivered.</li>

<heading>Railways : Junior Railworker Drowned in Railway Well, Bloemfontein</heading>

<p>The MINISTER OF TRANSPORT replied to Question No. I by Mr. Haywood standing over from 5th May:</p>

<question by="#haywood" to="#minister_of_transport">

<heading>Question:</heading>

<ol>

<li>(1) (a) What position in the railway service was held by the railway servant who was drowned on 5th September in Bloemfontein in a railway well and (b) what were his conditions of employment;</li>

<li>(2) what was his period of railway service in Bloemfontein immediately before his death;</li>

<li>(3) what was the nature of his work when he was drowned;</li>

<li>(4) (a) what is the circumference of the well, (b) what is its depth, (c) what was the number of lights at the well, (d) what was the strength of the lights and (e) what distance were they from the well;</li>

<li>(5) whether any alterations have been made at the well since the drowning of the railway servant in respect of lights and whether anything has been done to make the well safer; if so, (a) what alterations and (b) why was it considered necessary to make such alterations;</li>

<li>(6) what amount of compensation is being offered to his father;</li>

<li>(7) whether his father is in the service of the Railway Administration; if so, what are his conditions of service; and</li>

<li>(8) whether he is prepared to have an impartial enquiry made into the drowning of the railway servant with a view to determining if there was any negligence <span class="col_7441-7442" refersTo="page_0069"/>on the part of the Administration or anybody else in connection with his death.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_transport" to="#haywood">

<heading>Reply:</heading>

<ol>

<li>(1)

<ol>

<li>(a) Junior railworker.</li>

<li>(b) His conditions of service were those usually applicable to members of the temporary staff and his wage was 3s. 2d. per day, plus cost of living allowance at the rate of 11s. 8d. per month. His net income for the period of his service on the temporary staff averaged &#x00A3;6 11s. 7d. per month.</li>

</ol>

</li>

<li>(2) Fourteen days.</li>

<li>(3) He was helping a passed fireman in attending to locomotives.</li>

<li>(4)

<ol>

<li>(a) Length 184 feet; width 19&#x00BC; feet.</li>

<li>(b) 12&#x00BD; feet.</li>

<li>(c) Three.</li>

<li>(d) and (e) 100 watts, 8 feet away. 300 watts, 50 feet away. 300 watts, 305 feet away.</li>

</ol>

</li>

<li>(5) (a) and (b) The track on which the gantry-crane moves was extended by 40 feet, two lights of 200 watts each were placed on the crane and the 100 watt light which was 8 feet from the pit was moved about 40 feet farther away to make clearance for the crane.<br/>These alterations were made so that the crane could be left in a specified position at night in order to minimise the possibility of a further mishap.</li>

<li>(6) &#x00A3;9 14s. 1d. computed in accordance with the provisions of Section 49 (1) (e) of the Workmen&#x2019;s Compensation Act, 1934, but without admission of responsibility for the payment of compensation. As a matter of grace, the amount has been increased to &#x00A3;25.</li>

<li>(7) Yes, as a railworker in a casual capacity under the usual conditions of service applicable to the casual staff. His wage is at present 9s. per day, plus temporary extra responsibility allowance at 5d. per day and cost of living allowance at &#x00A3;4 6s. 8d. per month.</li>

<li>(8) The circumstances under which the accident occurred were investigated at the time by the Railway Police and subsequently an inquest was held. A further enquiry into the mishap is therefore not justified.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>Discussions on Post-War Monetry Problems</heading>

<p>The MINISTER OF FINANCE replied to question No. IX by Mr. Marwick standing over from 12th May:</p>

<question by="#marwick" to="#minister_of_finance">

<heading>Question:</heading>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether he will give the House the latest information in his possession concerning the progress made, if any, in discussions in the United States of America between the monetary authorities on the subject of the postwar monetary problems, and especially in their relation to the future of gold;</li>

<li>(2) whether he has received any official or semi-official report concerning any suggested compromise between the authors of what are known as Keynes and White monetary schemes; and</li>

<li>(3) whether the Prime Minister has been able to report to his colleagues upon the extent to which his collaboration with persons in control of the Bank of England has enabled him to watch the development of the future of gold in relation to currency?</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_finance" to="#marwick">

<heading>Reply:</heading>

<ol>

<li>(1) and (2) Discussions have taken place over a considerable period between technical experts of the United and Associated Nations on post-war monetary co-operation. These arose from two sets of proposals issued as a basis of discussion by the Treasuries of the United Kingdom and the U.S.A., generally known as the Keynes and White plans. These two plans have now been dropped, and a &#x201C;Joint Statement by Experts on the establishment of an International Monetary Fund&#x201D; was issued simultaneously in London and Washington on April 21st, 1944.<br/>Extracts from this document appeared in the South African Press on April 22nd. A full copy of the statement is available in my office, where the hon. member can consult it if he so desires.<br/>Up to the present the discussions have been carried on on the expert level, and no commitments have been entered into with regard to them by any Government.</li>

<li>(3) No.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
