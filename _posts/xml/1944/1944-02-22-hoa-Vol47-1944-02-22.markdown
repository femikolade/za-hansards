<heading>Gold Mines: European and Native Workers.</heading>

<question by="#cilliers" to="#minister_of_mines">

<num>I.</num>

<from>Mr. H. J. CILLIERS</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Mines:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) What was the ratio between native and European workers on the Witwatersrand gold mines during each of the years 1939 and 1943;</li>

<li>(2) how many (a) mine workers who belong to the Mine Workers&#x2019; Union, (b) managers, (c) assistant managers, (d) section managers, (e) underground managers, (f) mine overseers, (g) shift foremen, (h) fitters, (i) electricians, (j) rope splicers, (k) lift operators, and (l) natives working below shaft level, were employed in those mines during each of the years 1939 and 1943;</li>

<li>(3) what was the percentage of certified mine workers as against uncertified mine workers during each of the years 1939 and 1943;</li>

<li>(4) what was the accident rate per 1,000 during each of the years 1939 and 1943;</li>

<li>(5) what tonnage was produced during each of the years 1939, 1940, 1941, 1942 and 1943;</li>

<li>(6) how many mine workers have on account of internment been absent from the mines for a longer period than two years and how many of them were unable to pass the medical test again; and</li>

<li>(7) whether special relief is provided for such persons; if not, why not.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_mines" to="#cilliers">

<from>The MINISTER OF MINES:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) 7.5 natives to 1 European in 1939.<br/>

8.0 natives to 1 European in 1943.<br/>

If the number of European mine employees on military service is included the ratios are:&#x2014;<br/>

1939&#x2014;7.5 to 1.<br/>

1943&#x2014;6.9 to 1.</li>

<li>(2)

<ol>

<li>(a) No information available.</li>

<li>(b) 1939&#x2014;60.<br/>

1943&#x2014;55.</li>

<li>(c), (d) and (e) The total number of assistant, section and underground managers employed was: 1939&#x2014;120, 1943&#x2014;141.<br/>

Figures are not kept separately showing the numbers employed under each of these heads.</li>

<li><span class="col_1685-1686" refersTo="page_0855"/>(f) 1939&#x2014;360.<br/>

1943&#x2014;388.</li>

<li>(g) 1939&#x2014;1,687.<br/>

1943&#x2014;1,625.</li>

<li>(h) 1939&#x2014;580.<br/>

1943&#x2014;408.</li>

<li>(i) 1939&#x2014;526.<br/>

1943&#x2014;433.</li>

<li>(j) 1939&#x2014;237.<br/>

1943&#x2014;211.</li>

<li>(k) 1939&#x2014;406.<br/>

1943&#x2014;408.</li>

<li>(l) 1939&#x2014;243,936.<br/>

1943&#x2014;225,580.</li>

</ol>

</li>

<li>(3) The Department&#x2019;s statistics are not framed to show the percentage of certificated as against uncertificated employees as there are some thousands of employees in the mining industry who may follow occupations without holding any special certificate.</li>

<li>(4) 1939&#x2014;42.85 per 1,000.<br/>

1943&#x2014;63.55 per 1,000.</li>

<li>(5) 1939&#x2014;59,101,042.<br/>

1940&#x2014;65,504,105.<br/>

1941&#x2014;68,330,890.<br/>

1942&#x2014;68,144,509.<br/>

1943&#x2014;61,053,620.</li>

<li>(6) No information available. There is no record of any miner having been unable to pass the medical examination of the Miners&#x2019; Phthisis Medical Bureau after release from internment.</li>

<li>(7) No. In view of my answer to the previous question it will be appreciated that the problem has not arisen.</li>

</ol>

<p>All the foregoing data refer to the large gold mines of the Witwatersrand and Extensions to the East and West, with the exception of the accident rates given in No. (4) which embrace all mining concerns, large and small, on the Witwatersrand and Extensions.</p>

</answer>

<heading>Free Higher and Technical Education.</heading>

<question by="#van_onselen" to="#minister_of_education">

<num>II.</num>

<from>Mr. VAN ONSELEN (for Mr. Tighy)</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Education:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether (a) higher education and (b) technical and commercial training in the Transvaal is free; if not, why not; and</li>

<li>(2) whether he will consider the advisability of making it free.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_education" to="#van_onselen">

<from>The MINISTER OF EDUCATION:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1)

<ol>

<li>(a) No.</li>

<li>(b) As regards technical and commercial training at technical colleges, no, but bursaries are being granted annually to needy pupils. Technical and commercial training at departmental schools are also not free in the true sense of the word, but pupils whose parents are in need of it are to a considerable extent admitted to departmental schools free of charge or at a reduced rate.<br/>

Higher education at university institutions and technical and commercial training at technical colleges are entrusted to statutory bodies which are subsidised by the State and it is for those bodies to decide whether they will provide free education or not.</li>

</ol>

</li>

<li>(2) As regards training at university institutions and technical colleges, see reply under 1 above. As far as training at departmental schools is concerned, I am of opinion that in view of the very liberal amount of assistance given, the present position may be regarded as satisfactory.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
