<heading>Grapes: Prices fixed by Deciduous Fruit Board.</heading>

<question by="#tothill" to="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry">

<num>I.</num>

<from>Mr. TOTHILL</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Agriculture and Forestry:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) What are the prices fixed by the Deciduous Fruit Board for first, second and third grade grapes, respectively, for (a) the grower and (b) the consumer; and</li>

<li>(2) who receives the difference between the prices received by the grower and paid by the consumer.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry" to="#tothill">

<from>The MINISTER OF AGRICULTURE AND FORESTRY:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1)<ol>

<li>(a) <i>Advance to growers.</i>

<ol>

<li>(i) <i>Selected</i> in 10 lb. trays: 2/9 per tray (including 8d. for tray).</li>

<li>(ii) <i>Choice</i> in 40 lb. boxes: 5/10 per box or 1&#x00BE;d. per lb. (box returnable).</li>

<li>(iii) <i>Standard</i> in 40 lb. boxes: 3/9 per box or 1&#x215B;d. per lb. (box returnable).</li>

</ol>

</li>

<li>(b) <i>Board&#x2019;s wholesale selling prices.</i>

<ol>

<li>(i) <i>Selected</i> in trays: 3/6 per tray.</li>

<li>(ii) <i>Choice</i> in 40 lb. boxes: 8/- per box for 100 boxes or more and 8/6 per box for less than 100 boxes.</li>

<li>(iii) <i>Standard</i> in 40 lb. boxes: 5/6 per box for 100 boxes or more and 5/9 per box for less than 100 boxes.</li>

</ol>

<span class="col_233-234" refersTo="page_0129"/>No consumers&#x2019; prices have been fixed by regulation but the Board sells to consumers at its depots (Cape Town and Salt River markets) at the following prices:

<ol>

<li>(i) Selected at 4/3 per tray.</li>

<li>(ii) Choice at 3&#x00BD; lb. for 1/-.</li>

<li>(iii) Standard at 5 lb. for 1/-.</li>

</ol>

</li>

</ol>

</li>

<li>(2) The difference between the advance paid to producers at the time of delivery and the Board&#x2019;s wholesale selling price is, together with the Government grant, intended to cover the costs of transportation, inspection, handling, storage, delivery and sale of the fruit and to pay an agterskot to producers so as to bring the producers&#x2019; total returns to more or less the level of last year&#x2019;s intake prices.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<p>If the hon. member for Jeppes had devised some procedure or other under which there would not have been so many applications to court, so that the power could have been given to some other party &#x2014; which could not have been a good principle &#x2014; we could still have considered the proposition, but we find at the moment that the position is such that in every instance where difficulty arises and the parent is anxious about the welfare of the child, the court is there for the parent to appeal to. There the law is no different from what it is here. Legislation was introduced but even there the court is the supreme guardian of the child.
