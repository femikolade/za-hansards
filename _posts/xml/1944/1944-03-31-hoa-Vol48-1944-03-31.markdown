<heading>Production Cost of Maize.</heading>

<p>The MINISTER OF AGRICULTURE AND FORESTRY replied to Question XII by Mrs. Ballinger standing over from 21st March:</p>

<question by="#ballinger" to="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry">

<heading>QUESTION:</heading>

<ol>

<li>(1) What costs of production are taken into consideration by the Maize Control Board in fixing the price of maize to the producer; and</li>

<li>(2) what were these costs in each of the years 1940, 1941, 1942 and 1943, and what is their estimated level in 1944.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry" to="#ballinger">

<heading>REPLY:</heading>

<ol>

<li>(1) All direct costs incurred in connection with the crop such as fertilisers, labour, fuel en draught power, threshing costs, bags and twine, seed and transport; depreciation and repairs of implements, machinery and farm buildings; interest on capital and remuneration to the farmer for his own labour and management.</li>

<li>(2) Actual costs of production are dependent upon the yield per morgen which again is dependent upon climatic conditions during the growing season and the various vicissitudes of farming. In view of the uncertain rainfall in the main maize producing areas there is a very wide fluctuation in maize yields from season to season, from area to area and even from farm to farm and it is consequently practically impossible to arrive at an average cost of production figure for maize which will cover all the important producing areas in any one season.<br/>

Notwithstanding these limitations, cost of production surveys covering a limited number of farms in selected areas have, however, from time to time been undertaken by the Division of Economics and Markets, the latest being an investigation conducted in 1940 and 1941 in respect of the crop year 1939-&#x2019;40. The results of these investigations can, however, be accepted as no more than an indication of costs and are intended only as a general guide in price fixation.<br/>

Based on the 1939-&#x2019;40 investigation, the Division has estimated the costs over the war years and, depending upon the crop, has found that the average costs of production vary from 7s. 8d. per bag in one year to 14s. 3d. per bag in another year, excluding any allowance to the farmer for his own labour and management and the labour of his family.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
