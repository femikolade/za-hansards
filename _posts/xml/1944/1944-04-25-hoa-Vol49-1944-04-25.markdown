<heading>National Supplies Control Board</heading>

<question by="#butters" to="#minister_of_economic_development">

<num>I.</num>

<from>Capt. BUTTERS</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Economic Development :</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Who are the members of the National Supplies Control Board and who is Chairman;</li>

<li>(2) what salaries are paid to (a) the Chairman, (b) the secretary or manager and (c) the accountant;</li>

<li>(3) what was the total cost of administration during 1943 and what is the total cost to date;</li>

<li>(4) what is the value of goods imported by the Board during 1943 for (a) war supplies, (b) industry and (c) commerce;</li>

<li>(5) what is the nature of the goods imported for (b) and (c), respectively;</li>

<li>(6) what is the rate of commission charged by the Board to (a) war supplies, (b) industry and (c) commerce;</li>

<li>(7) what amount has been collected in commission to date from commerce and industry; and</li>

<li>(8) what amount has been debited to commerce and industry for contingencies.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_economic_development" to="#butters">

<from>The MINISTER OF ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) The Secretary for Commerce and Industries (Chairman), Messrs. E. P. <span class="col_5731-5732" refersTo="page_0294"/>Smith, A. B. McDonald, E. J. Crean, G. H. Starck and F. P. Spooner;</li>

<li>(2) (a), (b) and (c) The Chairman, Secretary and Accountant are permanent public servants, who are in receipt of salaries payable to the incumbents of the posts ordinarily occupied by them in the public service. There is no manager;</li>

<li>(3) It is not possible to determine the cost of administration since the officials employed are public servants, the rents for the offices occupied by the Board, stationery and other office requisites, etc., as well as the services of the High Commissioner in London and Trade Commissioner in Bombay, which are utilised in this connection are paid by the Government and no separate accounts are kept of these costs;</li>

<li>(4) (a), (b) and (c) It is not possible to indicate separately the value of the goods imported by the Board during 1943 for war supplies, industry or commerce, as the goods are imported in bulk and it is not known for what purpose they are used after sale to merchants and manufacturers in the Union. The total value of goods imported by the Board during 1943 amounts to approximately &#x00A3;2,650,000;</li>

<li>(5) Goods which are so far being imported by the Board are rubber (raw), latex, groundnuts, grainbags, sisal, timber, shellac, tin and tin concentrates, salt for industrial purposes and linseed;</li>

<li>(6) (a), (b) and (c) When an indemnity against losses is given by the buying firm, the rate of commission is 1 per cent. When no such indemnity is given, the rate of commission is 2&#x00BD; per cent.;</li>

<li>(7) In view of the fact that it is not possible to ascertain the value of the goods which go to war supplies, industry or commerce, the amount collected in commission from commerce and industry cannot be determined. The total amount collected in commission up to the 31st March, 1944, in respect of all goods imported by the Board is &#x00A3;6,936. Further sums, the amounts of which have not yet been finally determined, however, remain to be collected on sales to date;</li>

<li>(8) No separate amount is charged for contingent liabilities, as any provision for loss or damage is included in the 2&#x00BD; per cent. commission.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>Campaign Against Nagana</heading>

<question by="#ueckermann" to="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry">

<num>II.</num>

<from>Maj. UECKERMANN</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Agriculture and Forestry:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) How many head of game have been shot in Zululand under Government authority since the beginning of the present campaign against Nagana;</li>

<li>(2) whether there is any diminution of Nagana as a result;</li>

<li>(3) whether protests have been received from the Royal Society of South Africa and other societies against the destruction of game;</li>

<li>(4) whether it is the policy of the Government to continue the destruction of game in order to combat Nagana;</li>

<li>(5) whether no other and more effective means to control the tsetse-fly have been devised;</li>

<li>(6) whether stringent action has been taken to keep settlers&#x2019; and natives&#x2019; cattle away from the boundaries of the Zululand game reserves; and</li>

<li>(7) whether the Government will institute a thorough scientific investigation of the Nagana problem, and in the meantime discontinue the policy of shooting the game.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry" to="#ueckermann">

<from>The MINISTER OF AGRICULTURE AND FORESTRY:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) I must refer the hon. member to the reply given to the second part of Question XIII on the 10th March, 1944.</li>

<li>(2) Yes.</li>

<li>(3) Yes.</li>

<li>(4) Yes.</li>

<li>(5) Destruction of game as carriers and source of food and clearing of bush serving as shelter and breeding ground of the tsetse-fly are the only effective measures yet discovered. Trapping, burning, etc., cannot exterminate the flies.</li>

<li>(6) Yes.</li>

<li>(7) Such an investigation has already been carried out and other means of solving the problem have been fully explored. The results obtained in this country and in all other African tsetse-fly areas, have shown that the present policy is the only really effective one, and in the circumstances I regret that I cannot <span class="col_5733-5734" refersTo="page_0295"/>accede to the request of the hon. member.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
