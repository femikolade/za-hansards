<heading>QUESTIONS.</heading>

<p>Servitudes on Crown Grants to Discharged Servicemen.</p>

<question by="#marwick" to="#minister_of_lands">

<num>I.</num>

<from>Mr. MARWICK</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Lands:</p>

<p>Whether he will consider introducing legislation providing for the issue of Crown Grants to discharged servicemen on the lines of the Southern Rhodesia Act, No. 17 of 1944, whereby servitudes, such as are imposed by the Land Settlement Act, 1944, will not be imposed on such men and which will make provision for the adoption of special schemes applying only to them and will enable the Government to grant preferential treatment to exvolunteers who desire to settle on the land.</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_lands" to="#marwick">

<from>The MINISTER OF LANDS:</from>

<p>I am not prepared to introduce legislation amending the Land Settlement Acts in this respect. Land advertised for allotment after the cessation of hostilities will be alloted strictly in accordance with the provisions of the laws of the Union pertaining to land settlement. I should like to inform the hon. member that the provisions of Sections (2) and (3) of Act No. 42 of 1944 were chiefly intended for the preservation of the land for future &#x2022; generations and to prevent the land falling into the hands of persons for whom it was never intended.</p>

</answer>

<question by="#marwick" to="#minister_of_lands">

<from>&#x2020;Mr. MARWICK:</from>

<p>Will the Minister deal with my question as to whether he is prepared to introduce a special scheme relating to discharged soldiers or returned soldiers, so that they can benefit preferentially?</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_lands" to="#marwick">

<from>The MINISTER OF LANDS:</from>

<p>My answer is very clear on that point.</p>

</answer>

<heading>Citrus Growers: Subsidy for Export Fruit.</heading>

<question by="#marwick" to="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry">

<num>V.</num>

<from>Mr. MARWICK</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Agriculture and Forestry:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether the subsidy granted to citrus growers on the 1943 crop was intended for both growers of export fruit and those growing for sale in the Union;</li>

<li>(2) whether the distribution of the subsidy resulted in an increased price to the growers of 4d. per pocket;</li>

<li>(3) whether this subsidy was paid to growers at the time when they received their share of the pool distribution from the Citrus Board; and</li>

<li>(4) what was the number of pockets on which the subsidy was paid in respect of the 1943 citrus crop.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry" to="#marwick">

<from>The MINISTER OF AGRICULTURE AND FORESTRY:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) No, only export growers received the subsidy.</li>

<li>(2) No. The subsidy did not result in the suggested increase in price since growers producing less than the equivalent of 25,000 cases of export quality fruit each received 7&#x00BD;d. per case for grapefruit and 9d. per case for other citrus fruit; those producing from 25,001 to 50,000 cases were paid at the same rates on their first 25,000 cases, and those producing more than 50,000 cases could claim up to a maximum of 4d. per case depending on their costs of production in relation to the payout from the pool. Only one large producer qualified for a subsidy payment of 4d. per case.</li>

<li>(3) Growers received pool payments periodically throughout the season from about June, 1943, and subsidy was paid to most growers during August, 1944.</li>

<li>(4) To date the subsidy has been paid on the equivalent of 7,511,310 pockets of export quality fruit.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<question by="#marwick" to="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry">

<from>&#x2020;Mr. MARWICK:</from>

<p>Did I understand that the fruit was actually exported, or was the payment made merely because it was export quality?</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry" to="#marwick">

<from>The MINISTER OF AGRICULTURE AND FORESTRY:</from>

<p>The reply is quite clear on that point.</p>

<heading>Sale of Defence Force Vehicles.</heading>

<question by="#latimer" to="#minister_of_economic_development">

<num>XXXV.</num>

<from>Mr. LATIMER (for Mr. H. J. Cilliers)</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Economic Development:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) How many (a) lorries, (b) motor cars and (c) tractors are to be made available from war supplies for distribution and sale to the public when no longer required for military purposes and what is their estimated value;</li>

<li>(2) what is the estimated value of (a) building material, (b) clothing and (c) agricultural implements and equipment which are to be made available;</li>

<li>(3) through which respective channels will the sale of the articles mentioned in (1) and (2) be effected;</li>

<li>(4) what are the respective percentages of profit which will be allowed distributors, wholesalers and retailers; and</li>

<li>(5) whether, in order to give employment to ex-volunteers, he will consider establishing dep&#x00F4;ts in different parts of the country for distribution and sale at reasonable prices by the Department of Defence direct to the public requiring army material and equipment.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_economic_development" to="#latimer">

<from>The MINISTER OF ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) It is not possible at this stage to indicate the total number of vehciles which will ultimately be available but so far the disposal of the following lorries and motor cars from surplus war stores to the public is under consideration:

<ol>

<li>(a) 798; estimated value &#x00A3;194,200.</li>

<li>(b) 64; estimated value &#x00A3;6,400.</li>

<li>(c) No tractors have as yet been declared surplus but 186 vehicles of a military type known as gun tractors at an estimated value of &#x00A3;55,800 have been declared surplus. It is a wheeled vehicle with a short wheelbase of 101 inches and can be used for towing purposes but it is not what is normally termed a tractor.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(2) In order to determine what surplus war stores will eventually become available for disposal a survey is being undertaken by the Department of Defence and until such time as that survey has been completed it will not be possible to give the desired information.</li>

<li>(3) The policy is to use when and where possible the normal channels of trade for the distribution of surplus war stores under such conditions as the War Stores Disposal Board may lay down for each particular commodity.</li>

<li>(4) This has not yet been determined, as large quantities of surplus war stores have not yet been made available through the trade. I may add that the small quantities so far made available have been sold at current market prices, but subject to maximum prices gazetted by the Price Controller for individual commodities.</li>

<li>(5) No, it is not considered a practicable proposition. By establishing its own depots for the distribution of surplus war stores the Government would only create false avenues of temporary employment and would, in effect, establish a large selling organisation which could only operate in direct conflict to organised trade. As surplus war stores will be sold at current market prices, the purchaser, even under such a scheme, would not obtain any advantages. The Government considers that by making these goods available through the trade the trade will be re-stocked and will be able to provide more permanent avenues of general employment.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

I quite agree that emotionalism or sensationalism should play no part in arriving at a decision on these matters, and that rather any conclusion we arrive at should be based on reason and the logic of facts with the general background of the interests of South African society as a whole. <block name="quote">Three factors combine to cause criminal actions, as indeed any actions of any kind. <p>He ends by saying&#x2014;</p>

<block name="quote">My answer to the question: &#x201C;Is the criminal to blame, or society?&#x201D; would be sometimes one, sometimes the other, but usually both.</block>

<p>I quote this because I think it gives us broadly the background for consideration of penal reform. In regard to the essential of the enforcement of the law and the punishment of offenders, I cannot do better than quote from a speech of the Home Secretary and Minister of Home Security, Mr. Herbert Morrison at Birmingham in March, 1944&#x2014;</p>

<block name="quote">Amid all the work of social reconstruction a home must be found at an appropriate time for improvements of our methods of enforcing the law and of dealing with offenders. In this and other directions the possibilities are opening of large changes in our traditional methods of dealing with law-breakers. Unless our <span class="col_3309-3310" refersTo="page_0307"/>penal methods are reviewed in the light of modern knowledge, to the next generation our present-day methods may seem as crude, clumsy and barbarous as the treadmill and the crank seem to us. The primary duty of the court is usually to make the punishment fit the offender. The first principle in prison reform is to keep as many offenders as possible out of prison &#x2026;. In the 1860&#x2019;s it was laid down in plain terms that the sole object of imprisonment was punitive deterrence, with the emphasis on the punitive. Just that! And so for a generation we had the most strictly deterrent penal system ever devised &#x2026;. The belief in the efficacy of severe punishment is always cropping up, and without the devastating failure of this experiment we would never have known better. The failure was so complete that a departure to fresh principle becomes essential &#x2026;. The main deterrent force of, the punishment lies in the shame of going to prison and in the loss of personal liberty. But once inside prison the treatment must not be punitive repression but positive training. The offender is sent to prison as a punishment, not for punishment.</block>

<block name="quote">Although imprisonment must of its nature be deterrent it should yet be devised so as to maintain, stimulate or awaken the higher susceptibilities of prisoners and turn them out of prison better men and women, both physically and morally, than when they came in.</block>

The preamble of the Bill reads&#x2014;</p>

<block name="quote">To amend the law relating to the probation of persons by probation officers and the functions of probation officers, to provide new methods and to reform existing methods of dealing with offenders and persons liable to imprisonment; to amend the law relating to the management of prisons and other institutions and the treatment of offenders after sentence and of persons committed to custody.</block>

South Africa has a population of between 10,000,000 and 11,000,000; in 1943, 207,000 persons were committed to penal institutions.
