<heading>Seizure of Vichy Government Ships.</heading>

<question by="#klopper" to="#minister_of_economic_development">

<num>III.</num>

<from>Mr. KLOPPER</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Economic Development:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) How many ships and other craft which belonged to the Vichy Government have been seized by the Union Government during the war;</li>

<li>(2) whether such seizure is temporary or permanent;</li>

<li>(3) what is the value of such ships and craft;</li>

<li>(4) whether they are being used; if so, (a) by whom and (b) on what conditions; and</li>

<li>(5) what is the Government&#x2019;s policy with regard to possessions of the Vichy Government which have come under the control of the Union Government.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_economic_development" to="#klopper">

<from>The MINISTER OF ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) No ships or other craft belonging to the Vichy Government have been seized by the Union Government during the war. Since March, 1941, however, the Union Government, under War Measure No. 7 of 1941, have requisitioned fourteen vessels which, at the time of their requisitioning, belonged to or were operated by or on behalf of persons or firms in territory under the control of the Vichy Government.</li>

<li>(2) There was no seizure but the requisitioning is intended to be of temporary duration.</li>

<li>(3) As these ships were not seized by the Union Government no value was acquired by the Government.</li>

<li>(4). Yes, (a) they are being used by the Union&#x2019;s Allies, (b) reasonable compensation will be paid to the French owners.</li>

<li>(5) No possessions of the Vichy Government have come under the Union Government&#x2019;s control, but the Union Government have obtained control over certain cargoes which were found on board the requisitioned Vichy-French <span class="col_3575-3576" refersTo="page_0440"/>vessels. These cargoes have been disposed of in terms of Regulations 3 and 5 of War Measure No. 7 of 1941.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>IMPORTATION OF RACING DOGS.</heading>

<p>The MINISTER OF ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT replied to Question No. XXVI by Mr. Sullivan standing over from 2nd March:</p>

<question by="#sullivan" to="#minister">

<heading>Question:</heading>

<ol>

<li>(1) What is the name of the controller who issued the essentiality certificate for the importation of a consignment of racing dogs which arrived from Australia recently;</li>

<li>(2)

<ol>

<li>(a) when and to whom was the certificate issued,</li>

<li>(b) on what grounds did the controller issue it and</li>

<li>(c) whether the Minister&#x2019;s consent was obtained;</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(3) whether Union nationals accompanied the dogs from Australia; if so, (a) how many and (b) when did they leave the Union for this purpose; if not, whether Australian or other nationals accompanied the dogs; if so, how many;</li>

<li>(4) whether, when the essentiality certificate was issued, consideration was given to the fact that the Dog Racing Commission had not yet reported;</li>

<li>(5) whether any further essentiality certificates for the same or similar purposes have been issued; if so, when and for what purpose;</li>

<li>(6) (a) how many dogs were imported and (b) what is their value;</li>

<li>(7) how much shipping space did they occupy;</li>

<li>(8) why was the shipping space not used for the import of essential foodstuffs;</li>

<li>(9) why was a Union Government vessel used for this purpose; and</li>

<li>(10) whether he will institute an immediate equiry into the issue of this particular essentiality certificate and at the same time review the efficiency of the import control system to prevent the issue of certificates for unessential imports.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister" to="#sullivan">

<heading>Reply:</heading>

<ol>

<li>(1) Certificates of essentiality are issued by the Commodity Supply Directorate, which functions under the Board of Supply. These certificates are issued on the recommendation of the controller concerned in the case of controlled commodities, but as racing dogs do not fall in this category no such recommendation was necessary.</li>

<li>(2)

<ol>

<li>(a) Two certificates of essentiality, each for forty dogs, were issued to Messrs. African Greyhound Racing Association (Pty.) Ltd. on the 4th April, 1944, and 11th January, 1945, respectively. Both certificates of essentiality bore priority rating Eight; shipping term &#x201C;When Offering&#x201D; and were endorsed &#x201C;this certificate is issued on condition <span class="col_3581-3582" refersTo="page_0443"/>that shipment of dogs does not displace any cargo covered by a higher essentiality rating&#x201D;.</li>

<li>(b) On formal application by importers in order to enable them to carry on an established, legitimate and licensed business.</li>

<li>(c) No, it is not usual to obtain the Minister&#x2019;s consent for the issue of particular certificates of essentiality.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(3) No Union, Australian or any other nationals accompanied the dogs.</li>

<li>(4) No.</li>

<li>(5) No.</li>

<li>(6) In terms of Section 6 of the Customs Act, 1944 (Act No. 35 of 1944), this information may not be disclosed.</li>

<li>(7) 640 square feet.</li>

<li>(8) The dogs were carried on the square of the hatch where other cargo could not have been stowed. The carriage of food on deck other than live animals is not permitted.</li>

<li>(9) The importation of the dogs was covered by a certificate of essentiality and as their shipment on deck would not displace any cargo they were accepted on a Union Government vessel which was on the Australian coast at the time.</li>

<li>(10) There does not appear to be any necessity for an enquiry into the matter.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<question by="#louw" to="#minister">

<from>Mr. LOUW:</from>

<p>Arising out of this question, will the Minister please give us a definition of essentiality?</p>

</question>

<heading>Constitution of S.A. Theatre and Cinema Employees&#x2019; Uniof.</heading>

<p>The MINISTER OF JUSTICE replied to Question No. XI by Mr. Marwick standing over from 9th March:</p>

<question by="#marwick" to="#minister_of_justice">

<heading>Question:</heading>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether he has examined the terms of the constitution of the South African Theatre and Cinema Employees&#x2019; Union recently registered under the Industrial Conciliation Act;</li>

<li>(2) whether he will ascertain if employees of the Schlesinger group of cinema companies were called during 1944 to the managerial offices of their employers at the different centres and required to sign an application form for membership; if so, what were the terms embodied in such form to which they were required to signify their agreement;</li>

<li>(3) whether the constitution since registered lays down that certain persons specified in such form shall be the general executive of the Union and makes exceptional provision for their continuance in office;</li>

<li>(4) whether the customary right of the rank and file in a trade union to elect their own leaders is safeguarded to them in the constitution in question;</li>

<li>(5) whether the executives of this trade union are under Clause 6 of the constitution entrenched as members of the executive;</li>

<li>(6) what provision is made for the regular retirement and election of members of the executive;</li>

<li>(7) whether the exercise of a free vote by the rank and file is protected by the method of postal voting and unsupervised custody of the voting papers; and</li>

<heading>Reply:</heading>

<ol>
