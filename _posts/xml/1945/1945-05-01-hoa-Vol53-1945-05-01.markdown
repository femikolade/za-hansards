<heading>Kraal Manure: Wellington Companies.</heading>

<question by="#humphreys" to="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry">

<num>III.</num>

<from>Mr. HUMPHREYS</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Agriculture and Forestry:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) What concessions are being given a certain Wellington company dealing in kraal manure in respect of (a) transport from farms to the railhead and (b) railage from the railhead to Wellington per ton on kraal manure;</li>

<li>(2) what price per ton does the farmer receive; and</li>

<li>(3) at what price per ton is the manure sold to farmers.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry" to="#humphreys">

<from>The MINISTER OF AGRICULTURE AND FORESTRY:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) (a) and (b) There are two companies at Wellington dealing in kraal manure.<br/>

Both these companies hold temporary permits issued by the Controller of Fertilisers, pending the submission of full data by the companies in order to enable the Controller to consider the issue of permits in terms of Government Notice 59 of 1945. The temporary permits have been issued in order not to delay the despatch of manure to farmers.<br/>

My information is that sufficient petrol allowances have been made to them for the cartage of kraal manure from dumps on farms to the nearest railheads. Defence lorries have also been made available through the Railways Administration for the conveyance of manure.<br/>

As regards the railage rebate of 90 per cent. I have to point out that this concession is only allowed on manure consigned to farmers direct, and that no special facilities are granted on the railage from the railheads to Wellington in respect of manure consigned to the companies themselves.</li>

<li>(2) This information will not be available until the full data to be submitted by the companies are received.</li>

<li>(3) In the case of the one company 42s. 6d. per ton for milled kraal manure; in the case of the other 41s. to 45s. per ton for milled kraal manure and 13s. 9d. per ton for crude manure.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>San Francisco Conference: Informationin Regard to Discussions.</heading>

<question by="#van_nierop" to="#acting_prime_minister">

<num>V.</num>

<from>Dr. VAN NIEROP</from>

<p>asked the Acting Prime Minister:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether the Government has knowledge of the matters which will or may be discussed at the San Francisco Conference; if so, on what matters;</li>

<li>(2) whether the Government is in touch with its representatives at the conference in connection with (a) subjects for possible discussion and (b) the actual discussions there;</li>

<li>(3) whether the Government has given any instructions to its representatives on the attitude to be adopted by them; if so, what instructions;</li>

<li>(4) whether the Government will keep the House informed of (a) the discussions, (b) the matters under discussion and (c) the attitude adopted by the Union representatives;</li>

<li>(5) whether discussions took place or resolutions were taken in London in consultation with Commonwealth representatives; if so, what were the discussions or resolutions taken; and</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#acting_prime_minister" to="#van_nierop">

<from>The ACTING PRIME MINISTER:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) The purpose of the conference is to prepare a charter for a general international organisation for the maintenance of international peace and security;</li>

<li>(2) (a) and (b). Yes.</li>

<li>(3) As the Union delegation at San Francisco is led by the Rt. Hon. the Prime Minister and Minister of External Affairs and having regard to the contact between the Union delegation and the Union Government, no specific directive was necessary;</li>

<li>(6)

<heading>Report on German Concentration Camps.</heading>

<question by="#pocock" to="#acting_prime_minister">

<num>XVI.</num>

<from>Mr. POCOCK</from>

<p>asked the Acting Prime Minister:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether, in view of the practical difficulties in sending a Parliamentary delegation to inspect conditions in the German concentration camps the Government has taken steps to ensure that the report to be submitted from overseas is obtained from independent South African official sources instructed to obtain, at first hand as far as possible and from personal knowledge, information regarding conditions existing in the camps;</li>

<li>(2) whether the instructions being issued will lay down that a fair, impartial and judicial assessment of all the facts shall be made available; and</li>

<li>(3) what other steps the Government proposes to take to ensure that the South African public receives full information about the atrocities.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#acting_prime_minister" to="#pocock">

<from>The ACTING PRIME MINISTER:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) The High Commissioner has been requested to take steps for the preparation of a fully documented report. He has detailed two officers, one of whom speaks German fluently, to prepare the report. It is hoped to secure approval from SHAEF to visit some of the camps, one of which might be the camp at Dachau after it is liberated. The officers would naturally give such information as they have ascertained first hand, but it is contemplated that the report would include information obtainable from other reliable sources. The High Commissioner has been instructed to ascertain whether any Union Nationals are involved. So far as the Government is at present aware there are no such cases.</li>

<li>(2) Yes.</li>

<li>(3) Steps will be taken to make the report as widely available as possible. It is also proposed to arrange for one of the officers to broadcast to South Africa on their return to the United Kingdom, and for the speedy distribution to South Africa of newsreels of the camps.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
