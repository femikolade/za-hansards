<heading>QUESTIONS.</heading>

<p>Agricultural Courses for Returned Soldiers.</p>

<question by="#brink" to="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry">

<num>I.</num>

<from>Mr. BRINK</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Agriculture and Forestry:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) (a) How many returned soldiers followed courses of study at agricultural colleges in the Union after the termination of the last world war, and (b) at which colleges were they enrolled;</li>

<li>(2) (a) how many of them completed and did not complete such courses, respectively, and (b) what were the reasons for non-completion of the courses;</li>

<li>(3) whether returned soldiers of the present war are being given an opportunity of following courses of study at agricultural colleges; so, (a) for what purpose, and (b) how many have availed themselves of such opportunity; and</li>

<li>(4) whether such opportunities are being extended to coloured and native soldiers.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry" to="#brink">

<from>&#x2020;The MINISTER OF AGRICULTURE AND FORESTRY:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1)

<ol>

<li>(a) 543.</li>

<li>(b) Elsenburg; Potchefstroom; Cedara; Glen; Grootfontein.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(2)

<ol>

<li>(a) 492 and 51 respectively.</li>

<li>(b) A small number relinquished the course owing to illness, a few were discharged and the majority took up posts before completion of the course.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(3) Yes.

<ol>

<li>(a) For the purpose of taking up farming, receiving training as instructors on settlements and as foremen for soil erosion works, or to be absorbed in other suitable posts in the Civil Service.</li>

<li>(b) Up to the present a total of 147 students have enrolled at the colleges. I must, however, point out to the hon. member that general demobilisation has not yet commenced and that in any case the colleges were only re-opened on 28th February last. A considerable number of additional enrolments can therefore be expected as the present students complete the relatively short courses and demobilisation takes place on a greater scale.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(4) No.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>Stabbing of Indian at Durban.</heading>

<question by="#marwick" to="#minister_of_justice">

<num>XIV.</num>

<from>Mr. MARWICK</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Justice:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether a young Indian was stabbed to death about 20 months ago on the pavement in front of the Avalon Theatre, Durban, in the presence of a number of witnesses; if so,</li>

<li>(2) what was his name;</li>

<li>(3) whether evidence was taken from the witnesses who were present when the stabbing took place;</li>

<li>(4) whether there was any evidence to the effect that before the stabbing took place a certain person waiting at the Avalon Theatre drew a knife from his pocket and stated his intention of stabbing the deceased; if so, what was his name;</li>

<li>(5) whether the deceased actually expired after his admission to hospital; if so, when;</li>

<li>(6) what was the cause of death stated in the hospital medical report;</li>

<li>(7) whether the accused personally surrendered himself to the police;</li>

<li>(8) whether the accused made any appearance before the magistrate at Durban; if so, when;</li>

<li>(9) (a) what charge was brought against him, (b) what was his name, and (c) how was the case disposed of;</li>

<li>(10) whether any evidence was led before the magistrate;</li>

<li>(11) whether the accused was allowed to sail for India; if so, (a) why, and (b) whether the magistrate and police were aware of the accused&#x2019;s impending departure;</li>

<li>(12) whether the accused has any relatives in Durban;</li>

<li>(13) what is his father&#x2019;s name, address and occupation;</li>

<li>(14) what action does the Minister propose to take in this case; and</li>

<li><span class="col_4037-4038" refersTo="page_0671"/>(15) whether he will lay upon the Table all statements on oath collected in this case.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_justice" to="#marwick">

<from>&#x2020;The MINISTER OF JUSTICE:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) At about 10.40 p.m. on the 7th December, 1942, during the black-out, a young Indian was stabbed in a crowd, but there were no eye-witnesses to the stabbing.</li>

<li>(2) Goolman Hoosen Dawood Shaikh.</li>

<li>(3) Statements were taken from a number of persons who were in the vicinity when the stabbing took place.</li>

<li>(4) No.</li>

<li>(5) Yes&#x2014;at 7.30 a.m. on the 8th December, 1942.</li>

<li>(6) According to the post-mortem death was due to stab wound and haemorrhage, the stab wound piercing lower lobe of left lung, diaphragm and spleen and severing spleen artery, filling the pleural and peritoneal cavities with blood.</li>

<li>(7) On the 8th December, 1942, Advocate Bloom came to the police station with an Indian and stated that he understood this Indian was wanted in connection with this matter. On being asked what he knew about the matter the Indian replied &#x201C;Nothing&#x201D; and said that he was making no further statement. He was detained as a suspect.</li>

<li>(8) Yes, he appeared in &#x201C;E&#x201D; Court, Durban, on the 11th and 29th December, 1942, and the 5th January, 1943.</li>

<li>(9)

<ol>

<li>(a) Murder.</li>

<li>(b) As the suspect was at the time stated to be under the age of 18 years it is not in the public interest to disclose his name.</li>

<li>(c) On the 5th January, 1943, the Senior Public Prosecutor instructed that the charge be withdrawn and investigations proceed,</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(10) No, but an inquest was held on the 19th July, 1943, when a verdict was returned that death was due to a stab wound in the back inflicted by some person or persons unknown at night.</li>

<li>(11) Yes.

<ol>

<li>(a) Because the police advised the Immigration authorities that they had no objection to the issue of an exit permit.</li>

<li>(b) The police were aware of impending departure.</li>

</ol></li>

<li>(12) and (13) See reply to (9) (b) above.</li>

<li>(14) No further action.</li>

<li>(15) No; but I am obtaining the statements from Durban and, when they arrive, the hon. member may inspect them in my office.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
