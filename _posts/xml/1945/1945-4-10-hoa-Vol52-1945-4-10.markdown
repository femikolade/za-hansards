<heading>QUESTIONS.</heading>

<p>Unemployment as Result of Closing down of Factories.</p>

<question by="#de_wet" to="#minister_of_economic_development">

<num>I.</num>

<from>Mr. H. C. DE WET</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Economic Development:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether any factories are expected to close down after the cessation of <span class="col_5021-5022" refersTo="page_1162"/>hostilities; if so, whether he is in a position to state (a) how many and (b) the number of employees involved; and</li>

<li>(2) whether arrangements are being made to provide employment for those who will be unemployed; if so, what arrangements.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_economic_development" to="#de_wet">

<from>The MINISTER OF ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) I regret that I cannot reply specifically to the question of the hon. member. There are undoubtedly certain factories, which are mainly concerned with the production of war material, which will cease such activities. The question of the extent to which these factories could be converted to the production of consumers&#x2019; articles enjoys the serious attention of my Department and it is expected that in most cases satisfactory arrangements will be made to prevent the complete closing down of such factories.</li>

<li>(2) It can be stated that in consultation and co-operation with the Department of Labour everything possible will be done to place those who may become temporarily unemployed in other suitable permanent employment as soon as possible. It is understood that the matter is enjoying the serious attention of the Minister of Labour.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>Southern Rhodesia: Civic Rights of Union Nationals.</heading>

<question by="#van_nierop" to="#acting_minister_of_external_affairs">

<num>III.</num>

<from>Dr. VAN NIEROP</from>

<p>asked the Acting Minister of External Affairs:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether any Union nationals have been deprived of certain civic rights in Southern Rhodesia; if so, (a) how many, (b) what rights and (c) what reasons did the Southern Rhodesia Government give for such action; and</li>

<li>(2) whether the Union Government has taken or intends taking any steps in the matter; if so, what steps; if not, why not.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#acting_minister_of_external_affairs" to="#van_nierop">

<from>The ACTING MINISTER OF EXTERNAL AFFAIRS:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) (a), (b) and (c) My information is that during 1944 forty-one Rhodesian soldiers were charged before a Southern Rhodesian General Court Martial for refusing to obey a lawful order. Orders of civil disabilities have since been obtained against all these men under Act No. 12 of 1942 of Southern Rhodesia, to which I would refer the hon. member.</li>

<heading>Defence Force: Bilharzia.</heading>

<question by="#van_nierop" to="#acting_minister_of_defence">

<num>V.</num>

<from>Dr. VAN NIEROP</from>

<p>asked the Acting Minister of Defence:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether his attention has been drawn to a report in a local Afrikaans newspaper of 2nd April, regarding the incidence if bilharzia amongst the population of Egypt; if so,</li>

<li>(2) whether any members of the Union forces in Egypt are suffering from the disease; if so, to what extent; and</li>

<li>(3) whether he will make a statement to the House on the steps taken to prevent such disease being carried to the Union either by air or by sea.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#acting_minister_of_defence" to="#van_nierop">

<from>The ACTING MINISTER OF DEFENCE:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1), (2) and (3) The existence of bilharzia in Egypt is well known to the Union military medical authorities and precautions have been taken which have been very effective in preventing infection of our troops. Bilharzia is also rife in cer tain areas of the Union, particularly in the Transvaal, where a special committee exists for combating it. There is no record of any Union troops having been infected with this disease in Egypt, but if it did so occur it must have been in quite exceptional cases. Any such cases would have received the necessary treatment and thus become non-infectious to others.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>South African Prisoners-of-War in Germany.</heading>

<question by="#butters" to="#acting_minister_of_defence">

<num>IX.</num>

<from>Capt. BUTTERS</from>

<p>asked the Acting Minister of Defence:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) How many South African prisoners-of-war are held in Germany;</li>

<li>(2) which camps have been overrun by the Allies during their recent advance;</li>

<li>(3) how many South Africans have been freed as a result;</li>

<li>(4) which camps have been moved by the enemy from their original location;</li>

<li>(5) how many South African prisoners-of-war have (a) escaped and (b) been released and returned to the Union since Italy capitulated; and</li>

<li>(6) whether he will undertake to publish immediately any information received by the Defence authorities regarding the release of further South African prisoners-of-war and particularly the overruning of any further camps in which South Africans are held.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#acting_minister_of_defence" to="#butters">

<from>The ACTING MINISTER OF DEFENCE:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) 10,130 as at 29th March, 1945.</li>

<li>(2) and (3). No further official information is available other than that released for publication by the United Kingdom authorities on 13th February, 1945, to the effect that Stalags XXA, XXB, VIIIA, VIIIB, VIIIC, 344 and Stalag Lufts III and IV, being camps which housed U.D.F. prisoners-of-war, had either been overrun by or were in the path of the Soviet forces. The numbers freed, according to names received to date, are 97 Europeans and 12 non-Europeans.<br/>

The Department has had no confirmation of the reports regarding further releases which appeared in the <span class="col_5027-5028" refersTo="page_1165"/>Press recently, but everything possible is being done to obtain full information, which will be made available to the public without delay.</li>

<li>(4) Apparently no camps have actually been moved. The only official information received to date, however, indicates that prisoners-of-war have been transferred away from camps mentioned in (2) above.</li>

<li>(5) Separate figures of escapes and releases are not available. The total number of U.D.F. prisoners-of-war who have escaped and have been released since Italy capitulated, is 3,435 as at 29th March, 1945. The number of U.D.F. prisoners-of-war repatriated as sick and wounded or protected personnel since Italy capitulated is 242.</li>

<li>(6) Yes.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
