<heading>FIRST REPORT OF S.C. ON RAILWAYS AND HARBOURS</heading>

<p>Mr. DOLLEY as Chairman, brought up the First Report of the Select Committee on Railways and Harbours, as follows:</p>

<block name="quote">Unauthorised Expenditure&#x2014;Railways and Harbours, 1943-&#x2019;44.</block>

<p>Your Committee begs to report that sums amounting to &#x00A3;302,266 2s. 5d. (&#x00A3;283,290 19s. 7d. of which is apportioned to Revenue Services and the balance of &#x00A3;18,975 2s. 10d. to Capital and Betterment Services), are shown in paragraph 2, on page 2, of the Report of the Controller and Auditor-General on the Accounts of the South African Railways and Harbours Administration for the financial year 1943-&#x2019;44 as unauthorised expenditure.</p>

<p>Your Committee, having made enquiry into the circumstances, recommends the above sums for specific appropriation by Parliament.</p>

<p>GORDON DOLLEY, Chairman.</p>

<p>Report to be considered on 12th February.</p>

<heading><span class="col_1047-1048" refersTo="page_0534"/>Shortage of Fertiliser</heading>

<question by="#de_wet" to="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry">

<num>XV.</num>

<from>Mr. H. C. DE WET</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Agriculture and Forestry:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) (a) Where are our present supplies of fertiliser obtained and (b) what quantity is obtained from each source;</li>

<li>(2) whether all the sources in the Union are being fully investigated and developed;</li>

<li>(3) what is the main cause of the shortage of fertiliser.</li>

<li>(4) whether he will endeavour to have supplies of fertiliser increased as soon as possible; and</li>

<li>(5) whether he is in a position to give an assurance that the prospects for obtaining fertiliser for the 1946 season are more favourable.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry" to="#de_wet">

<from>The MINISTER OF AGRICULTURE AND FORESTRY:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) (a) and (b) There is at present a general shortage of fertiliser supplies in the world.<br/>The Union receives an annual allocation of 100,000 tons of rock phosphate from Morocco and 95,000 tons from Egypt. This is the maximum quantity of rock which can be converted into superphosphate by the local factories. Endeavours have been made to obtain prepared double superphosphate from America but only a limited quantity has been granted. The Union&#x2019;s yearly allocation of nitrates is about 22,000 tons from America and Canada, and of potash 6,400 tons from Palestine. Furthermore advantage is taken of every opportunity to obtain rock guano from the Seychelle Islands.</li>

<li>(2) When war commenced immediate steps were taken to explore all possible sources in the Union but only those at Langebaan have proved suitable for exploitation. Rock obtained from that source is already been converted at the factory at Bellville.</li>

<li>(3) The principal reasons are the shortage of shipping space for transport of the quantities allocated to the Union and the great increase in the local demand.</li>

<li>(4) Continuous efforts have been and are being made to obtain more shipping facilities in order to ensure greater and more regular consignments.</li>

<li>(5) As the hon. member will appreciate, it is most difficult to give an assurance in connection with a matter of this nature, since it is most closely related to war conditions. I will, however, continue to do everything possible to obtain greater supplies.</li>

</ol>

</answer>

<heading>Railways : Boreholes at Hutchinson</heading>

<question by="#louw" to="#minister_of_transport">

<num>XVI.</num>

<from>Mr. LOUW</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Transport :</p>

<block name="quote">Whether the capacity of the boreholes of the Railway Administration at Hutchinson has been tested; and, if so, (a) when and (b) what were the results for each borehole.</block>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_transport" to="#louw">

<from>The MINISTER OF TRANSPORT:</from>

<p>The capacity of the three new boreholes was tested fifteen years ago. Two yielded 2,800 gallons each per hour and the third 2,700 gallons per hour.</p>

<p>The tests were carried out on one borehole at a time, and it is estimated that if the three holes were pumped simultaneously they would give an aggregate yield of not more than 5,000 gallons per hour. As experience has proved that it is not advisable to pump from boreholes for a longer period than twelve hours per day, it is not anticipated that the yield from these three boreholes would exceed 60,000 gallons per day.</p>

<p>There is no record of the remaining boreholes having been tested, but the yield was many years ago estimated at 2,000 gallons per hour each, or about 25,000 gallons per pumping period of twelve hours per day.</p>

<p>These estimates are based on the assumption that natural changes have not taken place since the tests were made, which would affect the yield.</p>

</answer>
