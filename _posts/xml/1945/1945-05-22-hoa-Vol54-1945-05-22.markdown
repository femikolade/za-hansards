<heading>Retention of Military Ranks in CivilLife.</heading>

<question by="#van_nierop" to="#acting_minister_of_defence">

<num>VI.</num>

<from>Dr. VAN NIEROP</from>

<p>asked the Acting Minister of Defence:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether all officers will on demobilisation be allowed to retain their military ranks, titles or designations in civil life; if not,</li>

<li>(2) what categories will be allowed to retain their military ranks, titles or designations in civil life after demobilisation;</li>

<li>(3) whether officers who (a) performed clerical work in South Africa and (b) served in auxiliary and part-time units, will also be allowed so to retain their ranks, titles or designations; and</li>

<li>(4) whether the same conditions will apply to women officers; if not, in what respect will there be any difference.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#acting_minister_of_defence" to="#van_nierop">

<from>The ACTING MINISTER OF DEFENCE:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1), (2) and (3) Substantive, war substantive or temporary rank (whichever is the higher) may be retained by officers on release from full-time or part-time service, but not acting rank. This applies to all male officers at date of release.<br/>

As soon as the Union ceases to be at war, officers&#x2014;

<ol>

<li>(a) will either be absorbed in new post-war Citizen Force Units,*</li>

<li>(b) or transferred to Reserve of Officers.</li>

<li>(c) or they will resign their commissions.</li>

</ol>

Those who resign will not retain any rank in the service.<br/>

Officers who reach the age limit for service on the Active List or Reserve of Officers or are declared permanently medically unfit for service are placed on the Retired List with permission to retain their rank.</li>

<li>(4) No, for the reason that women do not hold commissions in the Union Defence Forces.</li>

</ol>

<heading>Combating Italian Beetle.</heading>

<question by="#de_wet" to="#minister_of_public_works">

<num>XVII.</num>

<from>Mr. H. C. DE WET</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Public Works:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether his attention has been directed to the destruction caused by the Italian beetle in the framework of buildings in the Cape Peninsula;</li>

<li>(2) whether he is in a position to state to what extent the destruction has developed in residential areas;</li>

<li>(3) whether the beetle has been found outside the Cape Peninsula;</li>

<li>(4) what steps are being taken for combating the pest;</li>

<li>(5) what results have been obtained in combating it; and</li>

<li>(6) whether all possible steps are; taken for combating the pest.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry" to="#de_wet">

<from>The MINISTER OF AGRICULTURE AND FORESTRY:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) Yes.</li>

<li>(2) Without a systematic survey it is impossible to state with any degree of certainty what damage has already been done to residential areas. According to voluntary reports received, 473 buildings have so far been involved.</li>

<li>(3) Yes.</li>

<li>(4) The Senior Entomologist of the Department at Rosebank furnishes advice to all interested parties in regard to the best known methods of controlling this pest. Consideration is also being given to the promulgation of regulations for the compulsory treatment of susceptible timber and the destruction of the beetle itself.</li>

<li>(5) In view of the fact that the problem is a relatively new one and the long life-cycle of the beetle, it is impossible at this stage to assess the effectiveness of the steps taken so far.</li>

<li>(6) Yes, within the limits of the available materials, facilities etc. and knowledge we have concerning the habits and life-cycle of the beetle.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
