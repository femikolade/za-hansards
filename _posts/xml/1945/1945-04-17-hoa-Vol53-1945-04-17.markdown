<li>(2) whether the area is a predominantly European area;</li>

<li>(2) Yes.</li>

<li>(4) Yes,

<ol>

<li>(a) the estimated cost is &#x00A3;10,000.</li>

<li>(b) I am not aware of the specific conditions of the notice. It will be necessary for the tenant to vacate the house when the demolition permit is issued not less than nine months after the commencement of the erection of the block of flats. The rental of the house has meanwhile been reduced by the owner.</li></ol></li>

<heading>Prices of Wheat, Rye and Barley.</heading>

<question by="#de_wet" to="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry">

<num>V.</num>

<from>Mr. H. C. DE WET</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Agriculture and Forestry:</p>

<p>Whether prices for wheat, oats, barley and rye have been fixed for the next season; if so, what prices; and, if not, when will such prices be published.</p>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_agriculture_and_forestry" to="#de_wet">

<from>The MINISTER OF AGRICULTURE AND FORESTRY:</from>

<p>The following prices will be fixed:</p>

<p>Wheat:</p>

<p>Class A grade I: 38s. per bag of 200 lb.</p>

<p>Class B grade I: 37s. 6d. per bag of 200 lb.</p>

<p>The prices for the other classes and grades will be brought into line.</p>

<p>Rye:</p>

<p>Grade I: 25s. per bag of 200 lb.</p>

<p>The prices for the other grades will be in line with this price.</p>

<p>Barley:</p>

<p>The same as the ruling prices in the case of Classes A, B and D.</p>

<p>A statement on these price fixations will be issued to the Press today.</p>

<p>As regards feed barley (Class C) and oats (Classes A and B), the prices will be announced shortly as soon as the price of mealies for the coming season has been determined since, on account of their close relationship from the point of view of stock feeding purposes, the prices must be brought into line.</p>

</answer>

<heading>Railways: Staff Representatives on Boards.</heading>

<question by="#van_nierop" to="#minister_of_transport">

<num>VII.</num>

<from>Dr. VAN NIEROP</from>

<p>asked the Minister of Transport:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether the members of boards such as the Railway Sick Fund, Joint Pension and Disciplinary Appeal Boards previously were first nominated and then elected by the members of the different railway societies; if so,</li>

<li>(2) whether there has been a change in procedure after the reclassification of the various societies; if so, (a) what change and (b) why;</li>

<li>(3) whether members of societies still have the right to vote for their representatives as previously; if so, whether their choice is final;</li>

<li>(4) whether the Administration has obtained authority to reject any candidates nominated by societies; if so,

<ol>

<li>(a) what authority and (b) when; and</li></ol></li>

<li>(5) whether the steps taken against Spoorbond resulted in the position described in (4).</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#minister_of_transport" to="#van_nierop">

<from>The MINISTER OF TRANSPORT:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) No.</li>

<li>(2) to (5) Fall away.</li>

</ol>

<p>I would mention, however, that the prescribed procedure for the election of staff representatives on such boards is that any servant in each respective group may be nominated or elected by members of the staff in the group concerned, irrespective of whether such servant or such members of the staff are members of a staff association or not.</p>

<p>This procedure has, however, been suspended owing to shortage of paper and the impracticability of arranging for members of the staff on active service to participate in the elections. In terms of rules framed under War Measure No. 88 of 1942, the staff representatives on departmental boards and committees are nominated by the recognised staff associations.</p>
