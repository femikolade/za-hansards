<heading>Bilingualism in Public Service: Pre-Union Officials.</heading>

<question by="#derbyshire" to="#prime_minister">

<num>III.</num>

<from>Mr. DERBYSHIRE</from>

<p>asked the Prime Minister:</p>

<ol>

<li>(1) Whether the assurance given by him at a public meeting in Durban in 1933 <span class="col_3907-3908" refersTo="page_0286"/>that transfers of pre-Union unilingual Government servants would be permitted to centres where the dual language qualification was not essential has been, is being and will be carried into effect as opportunity offers;</li>

<li>(2) whether pre-Union unilingual officials are eligible for promotion upon giving satisfactory service; if so,</li>

<li>(3) what proportion approximately of those applying for or entitled to promotion have been granted promotion during the past three years;</li>

<li>(4) whether retired pre-Union unilingual servants are given the opportunity of temporary re-employment as opportunity offers during the war period; and</li>

<li>(5) whether, in the case of pre-Union unilingual servants who are debarred from promotion or re-employment in the Government services, solely by reason of their being unilingual, the Government will consider the advisability of withdrawing any suchpenalisation and re-instating at the earliest opportunity the position of those who have been adversely affected by such language penalty in the past.</li>

</ol>

</question>

<answer by="#prime_minister" to="#derbyshire">

<from>The PRIME MINISTER:</from>

<ol>

<li>(1) The statement attributed to me is broadly in consonance with the policy of the Government as enunciated by the Minister of the Interior at the time if the words &#x201C;to posts in which&#x201D; are substituted for the words &#x201C;to centres where.&#x201D; This policy continues to be pursued.</li>

<li>(2) Yes, if otherwise considered qualified for the particular positions to be filled.</li>

<li>(3) Save in the limited number of instances in which specific vacancies are advertised it is not necessary to apply for promotion, since it is standard practice for the relative claims of all eligible officers to be considered as vacancies arise &#x2014; the process is selective and no officials entitled to promotion as of right.</li>

<li>(4) Yes.</li>

<li>(5) No. The Government can find no justification for compensating officials or ex-officials for lost opportunities of promotion directly due to lack of a qualification prescribed by statute&#x2014;in this case section 15 (3) of Act No. 27 of 1923.</li>

</ol>

</answer>
