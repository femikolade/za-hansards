<heading>AVAILABILITY OF APPROPRIATION BILLS AT COMMENCEMENT OF SECOND READING DEBATE</heading>

<summary>(Statement)</summary>

<speech by="#chairman_of_the_house">

<from>The <person refersTo="hansard_za">CHAIRMAN OF THE HOUSE</person>:</from>

<p>Order! Before calling for notices of motion, I wish to mention why the <i>Appropriation Bill</i> (<i>House of Assembly</i>) was not on members&#x2019; benches after the Minister had delivered his Budget Speech.</p>

<p>Appropriation Bills are not supplied to members and the public in advance because they often contain figures which the Minister only wishes to disclose in his Budget Speech.</p>

<p>Although the Bill was available, service officers of the House of Assembly have instructions not to distribute papers in the Chamber while the Minister is delivering his Budget Speech, as this may be distracting to the Minister as well as to members.</p>

<p>Standing Order No 53, however, provides that the Secretary shall supply printed copies of a Bill to members when a day has been appointed for the Second Reading.</p>

<p>This matter will be investigated in order to find a satisfactory solution to this anomalous position.</p>

</speech>

Thus, in pluralistic societies one must pay special attention to decentralising the system so that government activities can correspond to the preferences of diverse groups, and at the same time one must use the decentralisation to create a system that cannot be taken over or monopolised by any one group. I would say that these two objectives&#x2014;to permit diversity on the one hand, and to prevent the abuse of government authority by one faction on the other&#x2014;were both major goals in the design of the US Constitution, and they are equally important goals for the future of South Africa.</p>

<p>The challenge is for local government to provide specifically for diversity and to create a network of institutions whereby citizens become familiar with and responsible for governmental processes.</p>

<p>Local self-government, however, is not an end in itself. Groups of people face problems requiring collective action, and it is by using local government to solve common problems that true self-government can develop. In the United States the tradition of local self-government to resolve common problems predated the revolution and the constitution by over a hundred years. It was the very experience of local self-government that made it possible for the drafters of the constitution to understand that governmental systems need not be centralised and that citizens were much more likely to be satisfied with government if it were comprised of many independent units instead of a single monolith.</p>

<p>These, are not simply my own views but those of a very distinguished American academic, Professor Robert Bish, who edited a publication published by the University of Pretoria in 1983 entitled <i>Basic Principles of Political Centralisation to Local Authorities</i>, which I recommend to everyone who is interested in promoting good government in South Africa and elsewhere. I may also add an interesting statement that was once made by John Stuart Mill when he said:</p>

<block name="quote">It is but a small portion of public business of a country which can be well done, or safely attempted by the central authorities and the legislative portion of the governing body and it busies itself far too much with local affairs, using the supreme power of the state in cutting small knots which there ought to be other and better means of untying.</block>

<p>I tend to agree with him.</p>

<p>*In 1975 there were 36 000 local authorities in France. What is interesting is that as many as 62<i>%</i> of those 36 000 authorities governed fewer than 500 inhabitants. A further 18<i>%</i> governed fewer than 2 000. That is to say, 80<i>%</i> governed fewer than 2 000 inhabitants. This is an interesting illustration of the fact that even if communities are very small, they can nevertheless establish an effective governmental institution on a very small scale.</p>

<p>I want to continue with my example. Only <span class="col_1421-1422" refersTo="page_0745"/>0,1<i>%</i> of those authorities governed more than 100 000 people and only 0,005<i>%</i> governed more than half a million. That is to say, two out of 36 000 local authorities governed more than half a million inhabitants, and were therefore large cities. However, this did not detract from the effectiveness of those local authorities. In spite of the wide spectrum of numbers these bodies all have the same potential powers. They themselves decide what they want to undertake and what they see their way clear to doing. An interesting fact is that in most of the cases education, which in France is an optional function, was accepted by them.</p>

<p>In South Africa there are more than 700 local authorities, of which 92 of the White authorities are in Natal. I personally consider local government to be so important that I undertook in my own constituency to visit all 50 of my White local authorities within two months after the election. I have already met the largest local authority there.</p>

<p>&#x2020;It is obvious that the more functions a local government has, the more scope it will have in respect of self-determination. Self-determination at local government level is therefore directly related to the functions performed by a local government body in its own right. For this reason, it is essential that local government bodies should look collectively at what functions they want to have a say in or want to perform. They have been invited to do so by the hon the Minister of Local Government and I would appeal to them to do so as a matter of urgency so that they do not allow a golden opportunity for an expanded role in our society to go by default.</p>

<p>*Mr Chairman, owing to the immediacy and the proximity of local government, this institution can play a cardinal role in the creation of positive perceptions of government. It is my conviction that we can consider afresh these possibilities that local government entails for us. We can try to use them as building bricks for other constitutional structures so that they may in future mean a great deal to our country.</p>
