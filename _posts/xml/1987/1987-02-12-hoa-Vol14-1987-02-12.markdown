<debateSection name="#review_of_activities_and_traffic_tendencies">

<heading>REVIEW OF ACTIVITIES AND TRAFFIC TENDENCIES</heading>

<p>Increase in productivity is central to all our activities. Although revenue remained below the estimated levels, Transport Services managed to accomplish a fairly favourable financial result for the first nine months of the current financial year by means of economy measures and higher productivity.</p>

<p>It is Transport Services&#x2019; prime objective to render a quality service with a well-trained and motivated corps of personnel which utilises the appropriate infrastructure and equipment optimally. During the 1986-87 financial year the pursuit of efficiency continued. This varied from training in managerial and job-related skills to in-service development in order to effect a further improvement in the efficiency of work performance. In addition, the vital aspect of rendering an efficient service, especially in so far as relations with clients are concerned, was impressed upon members of the personnel during training courses.</p>

<p>Optimum stockkeeping was also scrutinised carefully. By accelerating the turnover of material and stores, it was possible to lower the stock levels, while the stock account was reduced by more than 26 per cent in real terms.</p>

<p>I shall now review the activities of Transport Services during 1986-87. Except where otherwise stated, the percentage change is always in respect of the period April to December 1986 as compared with the corresponding period the previous year.</p>

<p><i>Passenger Services</i></p>

<p><span class="col_703-704" refersTo="page_0380"/>In so far as passenger services are concerned the decline in the demand for intercity and commuter services continued. Owing to the unfavourable economic climate and increasing competition from the bus and combi-taxi, intercity passenger journeys declined by 27 per cent and commuter passenger journeys by 6 per cent in comparison with the previous year.</p>

<p>The rationalisation of intercity passenger train services referred to in the previous budget speech continued. With a view to achieving equilibrium between supply and demand, the frequency of these services was reduced by 42 per cent with the introduction of the new passenger train timetable on 3 November 1986.</p>

<p><i>Goods&#x2014;Rail</i></p>

<p>The total tonnage of revenue-earning traffic reflects a decrease of only one per cent. Hon members will recall that in the previous budget money was voted for the acquisition of automatic goods sorting systems. The present manual sorting system will be replaced with this mechanical method of sorting in September 1987 at Bellville and during December 1988 in Durban and Johannesburg.</p>

<p>This system also entails the application of the hub and spoke concept. This means that in a large area goods-shed traffic is gathered at one central point and then sorted for distribution to the various destinations. This is one of many examples of Transport Services&#x2019; policy of investing only where productivity can be increased.</p>

<p><i>Airways</i></p>

<p>For the South African Airways the past year was one of exceptional challenges and achievements. Notwithstanding economic pressure the airline succeeded in moving from a loss situation into a profit phase. The number of domestic passengers declined during the first half of 1986 but this trend was countered by means of schedule adjustments and sales promotion.</p>

<p>During the period under review the number of domestic and international passengers decreased by 8 and 9 per cent respectively. Since the curtailment of certain domestic flights with effect from August 1985, occupation of seats reflected a significant increase from 66 per cent to 75 per cent. To stimulate the demand further the airline&#x2019;s direct advertising campaign was intensified and several attractive domestic tour packages were introduced to the public.</p>

<p>During the past year the technical section also made substantial contributions towards the Airways&#x2019; success. Maintenance contracts with various airlines were for example responsible for revenue amounting to R17 million. Underutilised capacity was, therefore, used effectively and to the financial benefit of the airline.</p>

<p><i>Road Transport</i></p>

<p>So far this year the Road Transport Service reflects a decline of almost 14 per cent in the number of passengers and 10 per cent in the goods tonnage. At present its market share is less than 2 per cent.</p>

<p>In accordance with Government policy regarding privatisation Transport Services has decided not to expand its existing road transport services any further. Good progress has also been made with preparations in connection with the privatisation of Transport Services&#x2019; luxury Translux and Transcity bus services. In a deregulated transport market the Road Transport Service will have to be operated in accordance with the norms of profit and return on capital.</p>

<p><i>Harbours</i></p>

<p>The tonnage cargo handled at our harbours was almost 3 per cent lower than the previous year. Imports and exports decreased by 10 and 2 per cent, respectively.</p>

<p>As a result of the geographical distribution of industrial production the pressure on Durban harbour is still severe while the other South African ports have much spare capacity. In order to increase the utilisation of the harbours at East London, Port Elizabeth and&#x2019;Cape Town, it was decided to conclude competitive transport and shipping contracts with importers and exporters. This strategy should relieve the pressure on Durban harbour and offer users fast shipping, loading and transit times.</p>

<p>The departure from conventional break-bulk cargo to containerisation has resulted in considerable storage space, especially in sheds, now being underutilised in the harbours. This space will be made available to the private sector on a competitive basis.</p>

<p><i>Pipelines</i></p>

<p><span class="col_705-706" refersTo="page_0381"/>In so far as pipeline traffic is concerned the total volume of products handled decreased by approximately 6 per cent.</p>

<debateSection name="#sanctions_campaign">

<heading>SANCTIONS CAMPAIGN</heading>

<p>Mr Speaker, I believe that the least said about the measures to minimise the effect of the sanction campaign the better.</p>

<p>Sanction campaigns directly affected the South African Airways. On 2 October 1986 the American Congress passed legislation which, inter alia, deprived S A Airways of its landing rights in the USA. SAA flights to America were stopped on 14 November 1986.</p>

<p>Similarly, the Australian Government announced on 31 October 1986 that the South African Airways&#x2019; landing rights are suspended with effect from 1 November 1987.</p>

<p>The airline has nevertheless introduced various actions during the past year to maintain its role as one of the leading international airlines.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#financial_results_for_1986-87">

<heading>&#x2020;FINANCIAL RESULTS FOR 1986-87</heading>

<p>Transport Services revenue did not come up to expectations, but indications are that tariff generated revenue will decline by less than one per cent as against the original budget. Transport Services has succeeded in keeping expenditure well below the budget by means of various productivity enhancement and curtailment measures.</p>

<p>It is expected that the estimated revenue will decline by R23 million and that the estimated expenditure will decrease by no less than R260 million. Indications are now that instead of an estimated working deficit of R98 million a surplus of R139 million will materialise&#x2014;an improvement of R237 million. The personnel of Transport Services should be congratulated on this outstanding achievement.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#prospects_for_1987-88">

<heading>PROSPECTS FOR 1987-88</heading>

<p><i>Economic Prospects</i></p>

<p>As for the economic prospects for 1987-88 I wish to endorse the theme of the State President&#x2019;s conference with businessmen in November 1986, namely &#x201C;Forward in Confidence&#x201D;.</p>

<p>It is anticipated that the economic growth of the major industrial countries will be only moderate during the 1987-88 financial year.</p>

<p>Also taking into account the strong emphasis placed in these countries on industrial protection and the gradual change-over to high technology, it is evident why it is expected that the export volumes of the Republic may decline unless alternative markets are developed.</p>

<p>The expected balance on the current account of the balance of payments should as a result of especially the good performance of gold and sustained exports, continue to exert a favourable influence on the domestic economy.</p>

<p>In addition, the job creation and training programmes, which are being undertaken jointly by the government and the private sector, will stimulate employment and spending.</p>

<p>Spending can be further stimulated by low interest rates, more favourable agricultural conditions and stability in the exchange rate of the rand.</p>

<p>A factor which has a detrimental effect on the economy is the rate of inflation which is estimated at 17 per cent for the 1987-88 financial year.</p>

<p>In view of the aforegoing a real growth rate of 3 per cent in the Gross Domestic Product is anticipated.</p>

<p>If I may turn more specifically to the prospects of Transport Services for 1987-88, hon. members will no doubt agree with me that the activities of the organisation are diverse and complex.</p>

<p>The manner in which this transport organisation in recent years succeeded in emerging from a serious financial crisis is evidence that we have at our disposal a corps of workers with the will and resourcefulness to survive and to grow.</p>

<p>This proves, furthermore, that the set course of being more business-oriented is crowned with success.</p>

<p>I intend to pursue this course.</p>

<p><i>National Transport Policy Study</i></p>

<p>The views contained in the White Paper on National Transport Policy which was Tabled on 30 January 1987 are welcomed by Transport Services.</p>

<p>A deregulated transport market will offer market forces greater flexibility to function optimally.</p>

<p>According to the White Paper the current permit system will be replaced with road <span class="col_707-708" refersTo="page_0382"/>freight quality and road passenger quality systems and financial disparities will be phased out.</p>

<p>The financial disparities affect Transport Services directly and comprise, <i>inter alia,</i> the elimination of internal cross-subsidisation between services, compensation for the rendering of uneconomic services and the establishment of an equal and competitive basis for the recovery of infrastructure costs.</p>

<p>Transport Services will have more freedom to follow market trends and to accommodate the requirements thereof.</p>

<p>Hon members may rest assured that the resolutions contained in the White Paper will be phased in in such a manner that South Africa&#x2019;s economy is not disrupted.</p>

<p>This is a striking example of the Government&#x2019;s sincerity with the deregulation of the South African economy.</p>

<p><i>De Villiers Report</i></p>

<p>Dr Wim de Villiers was appointed in 1985 to inquire, together with the Management of Transport Services, into the financial and organisational aspects of Transport Services. The report was completed towards the end of last year and the relevant recommendations are at present receiving my attention.</p>

<p>Although I do not wish to reveal details of Dr De Villiers&#x2019;s recommendations at this stage, the report contains a comprehensive future strategy for Transport Services in a deregulated transport market.</p>

<p>It stands to reason, therefore, that this inquiry in many respects coincides with the report of the National Transport Policy Study.</p>

<p>Broad guidelines contained in the report of the National Transport Policy Study have already been accepted by the Government.</p>

<p>I wish to thank Dr Wim de Villiers for a job well done.</p>

<p>High-rated goods traffic at present contributes substantially towards the revenue required to compensate for internal crosssubsidisation.</p>

<p>The recovery of road infrastructure costs and the elimination of reciprocal concessions must also still be attended to.</p>

<p>These factors bring about financial disparities between the road and the rail which must be addressed in a deregulated market.</p>

<p><i>Wiehahn Report</i></p>

<p>Another important report which was completed at the end of last year is that of the Commission of Inquiry under the chairmanship of Prof Nie Wiehahn into the Establishment of a Negotiating Body for the SA Transport Services in connection with Salaries and Service Conditions.</p>

<p>The report is at present being fully evaluated.</p>

<p>I should like to express my sincere appreciation to the members of the Commission for the task performed by them.</p>

</debateSection>
