<debateSection name="#financing_of_the_1986-87_budget">

<heading>FINANCING OF THE 1986-87 BUDGET</heading>

<p>Although the present financial year only ends approximately six weeks from now, and the position of State finances could therefore still change considerably, it would appear as if the State Revenue Account could close with a small surplus.</p>

<p>At present it is expected that taxation revenue, before money is received from the Central Energy Fund in regard to the package are included, will amount to R33,740 billion, which is very close to the budgeted amount of R33,627 billion. Significant deviations from the budgeted amounts have, however, manifested themselves in some of the revenue votes. For example, it is now expected that revenue from the taxation of gold mines and other mines will be higher than was budgeted for. This is chiefly the result of the expected higher rand earnings from exports, which resulted from the depreciation of the rand. In contrast the slower upswing in the economy has resulted in the revenue from general sales tax and income tax on companies increasing at a slower rate than was initially projected.</p>

<p>Revenue of R1,012 billion, received from the Central Energy Fund as part of the package for stimulating the economy, brings the overall expected revenue for 1986-87 to approximately R34,75 billion. As far as the loan financing aspect of the 1986-87 Appropriation is concerned, it is estimated that total loan repayments will amount to R2,32 billion. This includes the earlier repayment of the 1983 loan levy announced last week. Loan receipts were also significantly higher than was budgeted for: The Public Investment Commissioners are expected to contribute R400 million more to Treasury financing than was initially budgeted for, whilst the sale of new securities, chiefly to the non-banking private sector, now runs to approximately R2,1 billion, as against the budgeted amount of only about R900 million. This higher than estimated sale of securities was largely the result of conscious efforts to reduce surplus liquidity by means of tap issues by the Reserve Bank&#x2014; particularly when State expenditure, as is customary, considerably exceeded tax revenue during the first few months of the financial year. In addition it is <span class="col_1155-1156" refersTo="page_0606"/>estimated that the sale of bonds will be R291 million higher than the amount provided for in the budget.</p>

<p>So total net financing of R5,531 billion is already available for the 1986-87 financial year to cover the deficit before borrowing&#x2014; the difference between expenditure and taxation revenue&#x2014;of R5,461 billion. This deficit represents approximately 3,7% of the expected gross domestic product, as against the 2,7% projected in the Appropriation. The greater deficit, however, can be fully justified in the light of the decision that greater stimulation was necessary&#x2014;and still is&#x2014;to bolster the hesitant upswing in the economy, without the State claiming too large a portion of the national product for itself, at the expense of the private sector.</p>

<p>This consequently leaves a balance of R70 million. This amount may change, however, depending on decisions that will be taken on the question of public debt administration, in the light of the liquidity situation, in the financial markets during the next six weeks.</p>

<p>From the above figures it should therefore be clear to hon members that not only has the Treasury once more been soundly financed during the 1986-87 financial year, but that it is also well-adapted to the particular state and needs of the economy. State expenditure is obviously not, as is sometimes maliciously alleged, out of control. So intelligent financial discipline has, in fact, been maintained.</p>

<p>Mr Chairman, I move:</p>

<p>In terms of the Admission of Advocates Act, 1964, an applicant who is seeking admission as an advocate must satisfy the court that he, amongst other things, has satisfied the requirements for the degree of Baccalaureus Legum of a university in the Republic and further that he has passed courses in English, Afrikaans and Latin prescribed or recognised for a baccalaureus degree by the university concerned.</p>

<p>On 4 September 1986 the Appellate Division of the Supreme Court of South Africa held that an LLB degree would not enable a graduate to be admitted as an advocate unless he had passed university courses in English, Afrikaans and Latin at post-matriculation level. This decision overruled previous decisions of two provincial divisions of the Supreme Court which held that a preliminary course in Latin was sufficient. It is not only the advocate&#x2019;s profession which has been affected by the decision of the Appellate Division, but also that of the attorneys.</p>

<p>The Attorneys Act, 1979, provides that a person may be admitted as an attorney after he has completed two years&#x2019; service as an articled clerk, if such a person was previously admitted as an advocate, or is entitled to be so admitted. There are many aspirant attorneys who have structured their studies and indeed their careers on this provision, and on the assumption that the decisions of the two provincial divisions regarding Latin were correct. Many clerks have entered into articles of clerkship in terms of the Attorneys Act on the assumption that they were entitled to be admitted as advocates. As a result of the Appellate Division decision they cannot be so admitted and find themselves being confronted with the possibility that the articles of clerkship are invalid, and that they will have to enter into new articles of clerkship and will have to extend their articles by a year.</p>

<p>As far as students now in the final phases of their study are concerned, but for this Bill, many of them would have had to restructure their curricula and many more would have had to register for at least another year at university so as to satisfy the language requirements in order to be admitted as either attorney or advocate.</p>

<p>As far as aspirant advocates are concerned, the position is very much the same. Many were nearing the completion of their studies and were intending to apply for admission as advocates in the near future. Again, but for this Bill, they would have had to return to university to supplement their academic qualifications.</p>

<p>I wish to emphasize, therefore, that the Bill must not be seen as a vote of no confidence in the decision of our judiciary; on the contrary, I have no doubt as to the correctness of this decision of our highest court. <p>The Bill consequently envisages accommodating the persons concerned by providing in effect that a course in the Latin language, which as far as its standard is concerned, does not meet the requirements of section 32(a) of the Admission of Advocates Act, but which is recognised by universities in the Republic for the purposes of a bachelor&#x2019;s degree, shall for a period determined by the Minister of Justice be regarded as sufficient for the purposes of the language requirement concerned.</p>
