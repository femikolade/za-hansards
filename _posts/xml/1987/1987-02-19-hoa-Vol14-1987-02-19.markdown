<heading>POST OFFICE PART APPROPRIATION BILL</heading>

<summary>(Second Reading)</summary>

<debateSection name="#introductory_speech_as_delivered_at_joint_sitting_on_19_february">

<heading>Introductory speech as delivered at Joint Sitting on 19 February</heading>

<speech by="#minister_of_communications">

<from><span class="col_1065-1066" refersTo="page_0561"/>The <person refersTo="hansard_za">MINISTER OF COMMUNICATIONS</person>:</from>

<p>Mr Speaker, I move:</p>

<block name="quote">That the Bill be now read a second time.</block>

<p>The Bill before the House provides for the appropriation of a total amount of R3 000 million for the continuation of Post Office services after the expiry of the present financial year until the main Post Office Budget for 1987-88 can be submitted for consideration and approval later in the year.</p>

<p>Although the Bill only provides for a global bridging appropriation, I wish to give hon members a brief review of the activities and financial performance of the Post Office in the present financial year and an indication of its expected financial position in 1987-88.</p>

</speech>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#finances_1986-87">

<heading>FINANCES 1986-87</heading>

<p>Thanks to active steps by the management and staff the Post Office succeeded, despite considerable price and cost increases over a wide spectrum, in keeping expenditure in the present financial year close to the amount appropriated without curtailing essential services. Operating expenditure is expected to total R3 458 million as against the appropriated amount of R3 421 million. This represents an increase of approximately 1,1%. Capital expenditure is estimated at R1 677 million, which is R47 million less than the original appropriation of R1 724 million. For other expenditure from the Post Office Fund on, for instance, loan redemption and an increase in the standard stock capital, an estimated amount of R278 million will be required, which is R22 million higher than that originally budgeted for. Total expenditure is therefore estimated at R5 413 million which is only R13 million, or 0,24%, more than the amount of R5 400 million appropriated in the original estimates.</p>

<p>Revenue for the present financial year is expected to amount to R3 733 million, which is R93 million, or 2,5%, higher than the original estimate of R3 640 million. Whereas total expenditure on operating and related spending, i.e. other than capital expenditure, is expected to amount to R3 736 million, the shortage will therefore be reduced from R36 million budgeted for to an expected R3 million.</p>

<p>Despite the more favourable operating results expected overall, an estimated operating loss on the non-profitable services rendered by the Post Office will nevertheless amount to R192 million. This loss is being defrayed from the profits realized on certain segments of the telephone service and, to the extent that these profits are inadequate, from loans. In my opinion this cross-subsidisation of services at the present levels can no longer be justified. Steps are now called for to phase out this practice over a realistic period. I will say more about this later on.</p>

<p>I wish to confine myself now to a few basic services rendered by the Post Office and the developments in the case of each during the current financial year.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#telephone_services">

<heading>TELEPHONE SERVICES</heading>

<p>It is expected that the additional telephone services being provided during the current financial year will total approximately 180 000. This will then bring the total number of telephones in the system to about 4 237 700, which represents a growth of 4,4%.</p>

<p>I wish to inform hon members that at the rate at which new telephone services are being provided, it is expected that the waiting list for telephone services will have been reduced by some 20 400, or 11% to approximately 165 000 by the end of the financial year&#x2014;the lowest in five years.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#telephone_system">

<heading>TELEPHONE SYSTEM</heading>

<p>Planning for the current financial year provided for the extension of the capacity of the automatic telephone system by about 350 000 lines. This entails, <i>inter alia,</i> the establishment of 28 new automatic exchanges, the replacement of 27 manual exchanges by automatic exchanges, the expansion of 152 existing automatic exchanges, the replacement of 39 automatic exchanges by larger units and the rearrangement of the lines of 99 automatic exchanges. It is expected that these projects will all be completed during this financial year.</p>

<p><span class="col_1067-1068" refersTo="page_0562"/>The planning also provided for the expansion of the electronic part of the telephone system to a capacity of 759 154 subscribers&#x2019; exchange lines, 387 637 junction lines and 105 976 trunk lines by the end of March 1987. It is expected that these goals will also be reached.</p>

<p>Mainly as a result of the requirements of the Post Office, facilities for the manufacture of digital microwave systems and optical fibre cable have been established in the Republic. This meets not only the needs of the Post Office but also those of other large users of telecommunications systems.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#data_transmission_services">

<heading>DATA TRANSMISSION SERVICES</heading>

<p>With the ever-increasing use of computers by all sectors of the community, data services are still showing a healthy growth and we expect that 7 800 additional services will be provided during the current financial year. This represents an increae of 11,9% and will bring the total number of data services to about 73 300 by the end of March this year.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#diginet">

<heading>*DIGINET</heading>

<p>Diginet, the Post Office&#x2019;s first fully digital data transmission network for high quality data transmission which has been available to data users in Johannesburg and Pretoria since May 1986, was extended also to Durban, Pietermaritzburg and Cape Town during the course of the year. It is expected that by the end of the financial year 2 300 Diginet terminals will already be in operation.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#telex_service">

<heading>TELEX SERVICE</heading>

<p>It is estimated that there will be 33 670 telex subscribers at the end of the current financial year. This represents an increase of 1 128 services and a percentage growth of 3,5 over the previous financial year.</p>

<p>On the other hand, inland telex traffic has decreased by an estimated 6% for the year, and international telex calls by 11,6%. In both instances the decrease is attributed to the prevailing economic climate and the fact that clients who are large users of the telex service are gradually changing over to the teletex service.</p>

<p>The entire telex network is now served by electronic exchanges. Furthermore, a new telex terminal with sophisticated editing and a variety of other labour-saving features, is also now available.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#teletex_service">

<heading>TELETEX SERVICE</heading>

<p>On 31 March 1986 there were 1 290 teletex subscribers in South Africa. This number is expected to increase to 2 170 by the end of March 1987. The number of inland teletex call units that will be registered up to that date is estimated at 28 million, which is approximately 13,5 million units more than that registered during the previous financial year. This represents an increase of 48%.</p>

<p>Equipment providing an interface between the circuit-switched teletex service and the Saponet packet-switched network was installed during the current financial year. This will make possible international dialling to those countries that have based their teletex service on the packet-switched network. The new facilities will lead to a bigger demand for the service as well as a considerable increase in international traffic.</p>

<p>Negotiations are under way to further extend the international teletex service which is at present available to seven destinations.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#international_telephone_service">

<heading>INTERNATIONAL TELEPHONE SERVICE</heading>

<p>Direct subscriber dialling to other countries was further extended during the current financial year. Telephone subscribers in South Africa connected to automatic exchanges can currently dial subscribers direct in 62 of the 208 foreign destinations to which telephone service is available. The total duration of international telephone calls for the year is estimated at about 107 million minutes which represents an increase of 1,8% over the previous year.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#motorphone_service">

<heading>MOTORPHONE SERVICE</heading>

<p>A new motorphone service supplementing the existing system, was taken into service in the Pretoria-Witwatersrand-Vereniging area on 26 May 1986. As part of its privatisation programme the Post Office provided the network infrastructure for the system and permitted private enterprise to supply and maintain the mobile units. At the end of December last year 351 units were already in use. The present growth rate is approximately 40 units per month. The system has a capacity of a thousand units that can be extended if necessary.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#beltel">

<heading><span class="col_1069-1070" refersTo="page_0563"/>BELTEL</heading>

<p>Beltel, the videotex service of the Post Office, which can provide service to 500 users simultaneously was officially launched on 2 June 1986.</p>

<p>This new type of communication service can retrieve information stored in a computer via a telephone line for display on a terminal. It also offers facilities such as the sending of electronic mail and a home banking service.</p>

<p>At the end of December 1986 there were 3 928 Beitel users, 74 information providers, 120 sub-information providers and 11 service providers. Beitel is at present growing at a satisfactory rate and the indications are that the service has a promising future.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#private_radio_communication">

<heading>PRIVATE RADIO COMMUNICATION</heading>

<p>There is still a steady demand for private radio communication services and it is expected that there will be 416 000 licensed stations by the end of the current financial year. This is 12,3% more than the number at the end of the previous year. It has been decided to allow the private sector to provide, operate and maintain new communal radio repeater station services. Such a system is capable of providing radio communication to a number of users by means of one pair of radio frequencies. The optimum utilisation of the extremely limited available portion of the radio spectrum was one of the main considerations in the introduction of the service. The Post Office will continue operating its existing communal radio repeater stations, but does not intend extending them.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#telephone_instruments">

<heading>&#x2020;TELEPHONE INSTRUMENTS</heading>

<p>The business sector increasingly needs more sophisticated telephone instruments and where the demand for them justifies the investment, the instruments are manufactured locally to the fullest possible extent. The following new and improved instruments, which are at present being subjected to field trials, will be marketed soon: A smaller and cheaper Erica pushbutton telephone; a Disa telephone with a 10-number memory; and the so-called multi-frequency Disa telephone that can store and display dialled numbers and can also be programmed for efficient use in conjunction with modern electronic branch exchanges.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#disa_plan_systems">

<heading>DISA PLAN SYSTEMS</heading>

<p>The various Disa plan systems are still very popular and some 16 000 of the smaller systems are expected to be installed during the current financial year. The demand for the larger systems also remains steady.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#business_telephone_systems_bts">

<heading>BUSINESS TELEPHONE SYSTEMS (BTS)</heading>

<p>This modem programme controlled replacement for manual switchboards&#x2014;it can accommodate up to 68 extensions&#x2014;is mainly provided to clients who previously used manual switchboards. The BTS system is continually being improved and has recently been equipped with an operator&#x2019;s console which can also be used by blind operators. All calls made or received are also recorded by the system.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#postal_services">

<heading>POSTAL SERVICES</heading>

<p>There was a small decrease in certain classes of mail, especially first class mail, during the present financial year. It is expected that the total number of postal articles that will have been handled will, as in the previous year, be just over 2 000 million.</p>

<p>About 60% of the total mail volume is handled at the four major sorting offices in Cape Town, Durban, Johannesburg and Pretoria where mechanized mail-sorting equipment is in use. The optimum utilisation of the equipment makes an important contribution to containing operating expenditure on the highly labour-intensive postal service. In this regard a contract to the value of R3,36 million was awarded in the course of 1986-87 for the supply and installation of five new mail facer canceller machines in Johannesburg and Cape Town to replace obsolete equipment.</p>

<p>As a result of increases in the cost of transport by road, rail, air and sea, total expenditure on mail transport increased by about 16% to approximately R56 million during the current financial year. The increase would have been higher had it not been contained by the negotiation of favourable conveyance rates with certain airlines and by the use of reserve freight capacity on domestic flights for the conveyance of surface mails at economic rates.</p>

<p>During the course of the year continuous service during the lunch hour was introduced at a further number of post offices where this was found feasible and warranted to satisfy a public need. This continuous service is now <span class="col_1071-1072" refersTo="page_0564"/>available at more than 100 offices countrywide. Extended business hours in the evenings were also introduced at certain post offices in the vicinity of important holiday centres and in high density urban areas.</p>

<p>These arrangements have improved and facilitated matters for the department and the public alike. I wish to make use of this opportunity to thank the members of the Post Office staff concerned as well as the staff associations for their co-operation and support in this regard.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#personnel">

<heading>PERSONNEL</heading>

<p>Notwithstanding the slackness of the economy, the constant general demand for post office services has once again made high demands on the department&#x2019;s labour resources. To curtail operating expenditure, expansion of the staff establishment has been limited to the absolute minimum and more emphasis has been placed on the optimum utilisation of the available staff. In addition, the continued implementation of more efficient working methods, work simplification and the use of labour-saving devices played an important part in realising higher productivity.</p>

<p>Representations for salary adjustments have been received from the staff associations of the Post Office. I wish to express my appreciation of the balanced manner in which they have put their case. Careful consideration is being given to the matter. I shall deal with it in the main Budget which is expected to be submitted to Parliament towards the end of May.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#finances_1987-88">

<heading>* FINANCES 1987-88</heading>

<p>As hon members will realise, the state of the economy also has an influence on the revenue of the Post Office. Economic trends in the following months will probably give a clearer indication of the income the Post Office may expect. I will then be able to draw up estimates for the 1987/88 financial year which are based on firmer assumptions. In the circumstances it will be appropriate to deal with general tariff adjustments during the main budget.</p>

<p>There is, however, one tariff that requires and justifies immediate attention. I have referred previously to the loss of approximately R192 million that will be incurred on non-profitable services. Of all services being operated at a loss, the biggest loss will be shown on the postal service, namely approximately R129 million. As hon members know, the loss on the postal service is a matter that has been broached repeatedly and over years in Parliament by my predecessors. By far the greater part of the volume of post consists of business mail&#x2014;the private individual makes relatively limited use of the postal service. The inordinate loss on the service in fact means that business concerns making extensive use of the postal service, are being subsidised excessively by the telephone user.</p>

<p>It has consequently been decided to increase the postage on standardised letters by 2c from the present 14c to 16c with effect from 1 April 1987. This must be seen as a step in the gradual adjustment of postal tariffs to make them more cost-related. It will increase postal revenue in 1987-88 by an estimated R33 million.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#financial_investigation">

<heading>FINANCIAL INVESTIGATION</heading>

<p>In his budget speech last year my predecessor referred to the investigation by an internal committee of the Post Office into its financial policy and related matters. Meanwhile, as was done in the case of Escom and the SA Transport Services, the Government has designated Dr W J De Villiers to also investigate and make recommendations regarding the capital expansion programmes of the Post Office and related financial matters. His investigation is already in progress and is aimed mainly at designing a policy strategy for the Post Office which will&#x2014;</p>

<block name="quote">ensure that future planning is better geared to conducting the business activities undertaken by the Post Office on business principles, with return on capital as an important norm;</block>

<block name="quote">provide the inhabitants of South Africa with postal and telecommunications services that are adequate but of a standard and extent that the country can afford;</block>

<block name="quote">limit capital expenditure to an essential minimum in order to make optimum use of the resources available to the country;</block>

<block name="quote">give attention to the rearrangement of investment priorities and apply strict costbenefit <span class="col_1073-1074" refersTo="page_0565"/>evaluation in considering proposed new projects;</block>

<block name="quote">strive at privatising as many activities as possible where this could be to the long-term benfit of the customers of the Post Office, the taxpayer and the community in general;</block>

<block name="quote">be focused on applying financial control to such an extent that more efficient and productive utilisation of resources and deregulation, on all levels, will materialise; and</block>

<block name="quote">geared to introducing more cost-related tariffs for those Post Office services being rendered at a loss in order to bring within reasonable limits cross-subsidisation and resultant price distortion that creates an artificial demand.</block>

<p>Good progress is being made with the investigation. During the main budget I shall deal with the findings and recommendations.</p>

</debateSection>
