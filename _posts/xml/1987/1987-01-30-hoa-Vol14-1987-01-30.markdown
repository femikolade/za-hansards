<debateSection name="#state_president&#x2019;s_opening_address">

<heading>STATE PRESIDENT&#x2019;S OPENING ADDRESS</heading>

<p>*Mr Speaker, Members of Parliament.</p>

<p>At the opening of this session of Parliament, South Africa finds itself in a period of challenge and decision. It is a year in which sides will have to be taken.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#&#x2020;international">

<heading>&#x2020;INTERNATIONAL</heading>

<p>South Africa&#x2019;s relations with the rest of the world are going through a testing phase. This is not of recent origin.</p>

<p>Today, we are witnessing an accumulation of historical forces and events that have been gaining momentum, especially since the end of the Second World War.</p>

<p>This was already starkly evident long before the National Party came to power.</p>

<p>Even the internationally-acclaimed United Party Prime Minister of South Africa, General Jan Smuts, had cause to complain bitterly about the prejudice, abuse and dishonesty South Africa had to endure at the hands of cynical and sanctimonious antagonists abroad. That was in 1946.</p>

<p>Since then there has been an intensification of this campaign of collusion and hypocrisy to create distorted perceptions.</p>

<p>In 1986, our international position was characterised increasingly by intensified pressures and punitive actions against our country. Undisguised attempts at interference in our domestic affairs reached unprecedented proportions.</p>

<p>This sad reflection on the conscience of a troubled world is both disturbing and ironical&#x2014;especially since it came about while we were proceeding apace with our programme of reform and renewal and had made significant progress in removing discrimination.</p>

<p>This unfortunate state of affairs should prove beyond doubt that the campaign against us has very little to do with peace, freedom and reform.</p>

<p>No foreign nation or the United Nations or any other international organisation has the slightest right whatsoever to meddle in the domestic affairs of South Africa.</p>

<p>It is a principle of international law and conduct that no self-respecting, sovereign country should be subservient to any other.</p>

<p>I am sure that all responsible South Africans are united in claiming this legitimate <span class="col_11-12" refersTo="page_0034"/>right for ourselves and in rejecting unwarranted foreign interference and prescription.</p>

<p>While noting this regrettable trend of international interference and regardless of the motives behind it, we shall have to reconsider both the content and direction of our foreign relations.</p>

<p>Ultimately, it is not to our advantage to become isolated from the international community. Therefore the Government will continue to conduct the Republic&#x2019;s foreign relations in accordance with our own national interests and those of our region.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#southern_africa">

<heading>SOUTHERN AFRICA</heading>

<p>The Republic is part of Africa in more than the purely geographical sense.</p>

<p>We recognise the interdependence of the countries and peoples of our sub-continent. Consequently, we shall continue to co-operate with them in the development of the whole region.</p>

<p>At the same time, the Government will not permit the people of South Africa to be threatened by those committed to violence and insurrection. Such elements are able to survive only if they receive support from abroad and are granted sanctuary and right of passage by countries in our region.</p>

<p>The South African security forces will leave no stone unturned in their endeavours to defend our country and its people. Every nation has the right to defend itself. We claim the same right.</p>

<p>However, in conditions of conflict, there is no hope of overcoming the difficulties we all face in developing our countries.</p>

<p>Clearly, the leaders of all the countries of Southern Africa need to come together.</p>

<p>Let us put aside our differences in the interests of our peoples and their well-being.</p>

<p>Let us devote our time and energy to ensure the stability, security and progress of our region as a whole.</p>

<p>It is a duty we dare not shirk.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#security">

<heading>*SECURITY</heading>

<p>The continuing unrest and violence in some of the Black residential areas remained the cause of unnecessary suffering and loss of life in 1986. We deeply regret every drop of blood spilt as a result of political violence.</p>

<p>Although the internal security situation improved initially, the unrest began to assume more serious proportions towards the middle of 1986.</p>

<p>Consequently, the Government was obliged to impose a nation-wide state of emergency. The correctness of this course of action has been substantiated by the decline in the occurrence of incidents of unrest since then. The revolutionary climate, however, necessitates the continued maintenance of these measures of control.</p>

<p>On both sides of the political spectrum there are people who have a simplistic view of the causes of the unrest situation and the means of dealing with it.</p>

<p>On the one hand there are those who say the Government and its policies are the cause of the violence.</p>

<p>On the other hand there are those who see violence merely as the work of a few instigators.</p>

<p>From ANC documents recently obtained by the Government, it appears beyond doubt that the ANC/Communist alliance is endeavouring to incite the Black communities, not only against one another, but also against the Whites in South Africa. It is evident that the ANC/Communist alliance is encouraging racial violence&#x2014;even though it could lead to bloodshed on a large scale. This terrorist savagery must be resisted.</p>

<p>The point of view that a paradise on earth can be achieved by violent revolution is nothing but a dangerous and totally naive dream. Those who wish to destroy the existing orderly system have not as yet come up with an answer that could provide South Africa with an improved dispensation.</p>

<p>It is impossible to create an ideal society overnight, especially not one as complex as South Africa.</p>

<p>Nobody should be misled by the fine phrases with which the ANC/Communist Party alliance has been seeking recognition and respectability at home and abroad in recent times. Discussions with the ANC are possible only if it severs its ties with and terminates its subservience to the Communist Party; abandons violence; and participates, as peaceful South African citizens, in constitutional processes in South Africa.</p>

<p>I am convinced that the vast majority of South Africans of all groups are basically peace-loving people with a sense of justice.</p>

<p>At the same time I must warn those who have committed themselves irrevocably to <span class="col_13-14" refersTo="page_0035"/>the violent overthrow of the State and the disruption of society, that the Government will not for a moment hesitate to act decisively against them.</p>

<p>The Government would like to lift the state of emergency, but will maintain its basic responsibility to purposely uphold law and order at all times.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#&#x2020;economy">

<heading>&#x2020;ECONOMY</heading>

<p>Although the South African economy faced new challenges in 1986, there were also several encouraging developments.</p>

<p>While the moderate economic recovery which began in the middle of 1985 was interrupted in the first quarter of 1986, it resumed in the last three quarters of the year.</p>

<p>The revival of domestic expenditure by the private sector was indicative of returning business and consumer confidence.</p>

<p>Supported by appropriate monetary and fiscal policy and sustained export efforts, large surpluses on the current account of the balance of payments were achieved and maintained.</p>

<p>Consequently the private and public sectors could continue to substantially reduce their foreign commitments.</p>

<p>An estimated nine thousand million rand was utilised in settlement of trade and bank credits, repayment of an IMF loan and other foreign liabilities.</p>

<p>This is indeed a remarkable achievement, especially since our economy is going through a period of drastic adjustment caused by various internal and external circumstances, among which the debt standstill. This achievement is even more remarkable when measured against comparable countries.</p>

<p>South Africa&#x2019;s gold and foreign reserves increased from April 1986 onwards while the rand showed greater stability. The rand appreciated gradually against the American dollar and in the recent past, also against other major currencies.</p>

<p>*The recent good rains appreciably improved the prospects of many farmers in the summer rainfall areas. This brought widespread relief from one of the worst droughts in the history of our country, and we hope that in those parts in which there has been no relief, better conditions will prevail during the next few months.</p>

<p>As in the past, the Government remains involved with large-scale agricultural aid in various ways. The Government is at present giving urgent attention to a report on the restructuring of agriculture prepared by the Economic Advisory Council of the State President.</p>

<p>Naturally, the Government has particular sympathy with the circumstances of the workers in this time of unemployment. That is why a special job creation and training programme was introduced. For this purpose, the Government allocated R450 million during the current financial year. By the end of September 1986, about a quarter of a million unemployed had been placed in employment as a result of this programme and by 31 March this year more than half a million should have been trained.</p>

<p>A White Paper containing the Government&#x2019;s viewpoints on the recommendations in the joint report of the Human Sciences Research Council and the National Training Board on the Training of Artisans in South Africa, will soon be tabled.</p>

<p>Inflation remains a serious problem. Several factors, including the stronger performance of the rand, should eventually contribute to a decline in the rate of inflation. This in itself, however will not solve the problem. What is also required is increased competition, higher productivity and restraint with regard to wage and price increases. In this regard I trust that we will rise above party-political interests, since it is a matter of serious concern for every citizen of this country.</p>

<p>As a result of the moderate recovery in the economy, a positive real growth rate is anticipated.</p>

<p>As far as its longer term economic policy, including privatisation and deregulation, is concerned, the Government will take due cognisance of the broad principles set out recently in the Economic Advisory Council&#x2019;s proposed framework for a long-term economic strategy.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#social_matters">

<heading>SOCIAL MATTERS</heading>

<p>In the social field, problems and development activities will continue to be given priority.</p>

<p>Good progress has already been made. For example, during the current financial year, the Government has allocated an amount of R1 000 million for housing and community <span class="col_15-16" refersTo="page_0036"/>amenities for all communities. This is by far the largest amount ever allocated in a single year for this purpose in the history of South Africa.</p>

<p>Nevertheless, it is necessary that the private sector becomes more involved in the provision of low-cost housing. Consequently, a Housing Trust, financed jointly by the Government and the private sector, was announced in November last year.</p>

<p>For this purpose, the Government has made available an additional amount of R400 million.</p>

<p>Orderly urbanisation, as part of a comprehensive development strategy, is one of the most important issues receiving the State&#x2019;s attention.</p>

<p>The Government&#x2019;s policy was enunciated clearly in the White Paper on Urbanisation which was adopted last year. Since then influx control has been abolished.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#education">

<heading>EDUCATION</heading>

<p>In the field of education important developments took place over the past year.</p>

<p>In all, an amount of R6 000 million was budgeted for educational affairs of all communities in the current financial year.</p>

<p>Against the background of the Government&#x2019;s commitment to equal educational opportunities for all, it is deplorable that education in the Black communities, in particular, is being abused by radical groups for political purposes.</p>

<p>Effective, meaningful education is possible only if politics are excluded from it and parents are positively realising their rightful involvement as partners in the education of their children.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#constitutional_developmen">

<heading>CONSTITUTIONAL DEVELOPMEN</heading>

<p>Together with ongoing development in the social and economic fields, constitutional reform will continue to enjoy high priority.</p>

<p>It is regrettable that the misconception is often created that little meaningful progress is being made with constitutional development.</p>

<p>This is not only unfair, but is done without adequate reference to objective criteria. It moreover reflects a short-sighted expediency which definitely does not serve the highest interests of South Africa.</p>

<p>Without any doubt, the basis of democratic government has been expanded remarkably since colonialism disappeared from South Africa.</p>

<p>This is substantiated by the degree to which the Coloured, Asian and Black communities are already participating in elected structures at the different levels of government. It is also evident from the existence of a large variety of political parties in the respective population communities.</p>

<p>The new constitutional dispensation was a significant step forward in this process.</p>

<p>It is the Government&#x2019;s declared intention that further expansion of our democratic base should be purposely pursued. In particular the constitutional position of our various Black communities has in the past, and will in future, receive the special attention of the Government.</p>

<p>I have repeatedly and comprehensively enunciated the Government&#x2019;s framework for further constitutional development.</p>

<p>I wish to underline it briefly again. The Government believes that any constitutional dispensation for our multi-cultural society must, at all levels of Government, meet the requirements of protection and self-determination for minority groups; power-sharing among the groups in respect of matters of common interest; and the prevention of domination by any group of the others.</p>

<p>Then I refer to the protection of minority groups, I also have in mind Black minority groups with their own languages, ways of life and traditions.</p>

<p>The demand is often made here and abroad that South Africa should adopt a so-called one-man-one-vote non-racial democracy.</p>

<p>In our circumstances, this demand cannot lead to a just dispensation in a multi-cultural society.</p>

<p>Rapid reform in South Africa is hampered by many factors in international politics.</p>

<p>These include the following:</p>

<ul>

<li>&#x2014; Misuse of the word and concept of &#x201C;democracy&#x201D; in naming and promoting Marxist dictatorships</li>

<li>&#x2014; Elected, multi-party democracies are decreasing because they are being threatened and subverted by these &#x201C;sham democracies&#x201D;;</li>

<li><span class="col_17-18" refersTo="page_0037"/>&#x2014; Democratic countries are finding it increasingly difficult to maintain democratic principles and liberties, because of the abuse of those liberties in the unhampered pursuit of revolutionary goals.</li>

</ul>

<p>South Africa&#x2019;s progress with constitutional adjustments and processes is not as slow as the progress made in the past by certain countries which now wish to prescribe to us.</p>

<p>This is the background against which the Republic has to continue the process of constitutional development. We shall not permit ourselves to be prescribed to in this regard. We shall not do it irresponsibly and hastily. We shall seek and find our own solutions here, in our own way, through our own dedication, knowledge and experience.</p>

<p>This will also have to be the result of consultation and negotiation among concerned groups and parties which truly believe in democracy and reject violence as a political instrument.</p>

<p>To give effect to these aims, a wide variety of steps at all levels of government have been in the process of planning and implementation for some time.</p>

<ul>

<li>&#x2014; At the local government level the Co-ordinating Council for Local Authorities continues to serve as an instrument through which the representatives of all the respective groups have the opportunity to promote the interests of the communities concerned.</li>

<li>&#x2014; At the regional level good progress has been made with the arrangements for the institution of Regional Services Councils.</li>

<li>&#x2014; At the provincial level the new system has already given a say to all population communities.</li>

<li>&#x2014; Statutory provision has also been made for the establishment of joint executive bodies between the provincial authorities and the self-governing territories.</li>

<li>&#x2014; Consideration is also being given to possible changes to the composition and functions of the President&#x2019;s Council.</li>

</ul>

<p>Each of these steps represents the broadening of the democratic base and/or the extension and improvement of participation, and confirms the principle of co-operation in respect of matters of common concern.</p>

<ul>

<li>&#x2014; Regarding the participation of all groups at the level of central government, comprehensive negotiations have been conducted in recent years.</li>

<li>&#x2014; A Bill proposing a National Council was published last year. This proposed council could be the precursor of a Council of State, as has already been envisaged. Such a joint Council of State could consist of leaders and other representatives of all groups and communities who could participate in the formulation of policy on the basis of consensus decision-making on matters of common interest. The establishment of the Council of State, which would enjoy statutory status and recognition and in which real accountability would be possible, will be further intensively investigated and negotiated.</li>

</ul>

<p>Overall constitutional and political progress, however, is only part of the way to a secure and prosperous future.</p>

<p>A Constitution merely reflects a community&#x2019;s political and social culture and all the underlying values, norms and principles. They cannot be created by a constitution.</p>

<p>In order to create the circumstances in which constitutional reform may flourish, constitutional development in the Republic of South Africa will have to be accompanied by other processes as well.</p>

<p>One of the most important preconditions for continued and orderly development is the maintenance of peaceful and stable communities.</p>

<p>This presupposes ownership of land and freehold right, and with it the right to an own community life through which cultural expression, for example by means of social and educational institutions, may be community orientated as far as possible.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#the_natal/kwazulu_indaba">

<heading>THE NATAL/KWAZULU INDABA</heading>

<p>There is another matter to which I wish to <span class="col_19-20" refersTo="page_0038"/>refer briefly. It is the Natal/KwaZulu Indaba which took place at the joint initiative of the KwaZulu Government and the Natal Provincial Council.</p>

<p>The Government&#x2019;s point of view on it is determined by the following factors:</p>

<ol>

<li>1. Firstly, it is Government policy that consultation and negotiation on matters of common concern should take place among the various population communities at all levels of government.</li>

<li>2. Secondly, there is no objection to the establishment by the governments of self-governing states and neighbouring provincial authorities in Natal as elsewhere, of joint executive authorities to deal with matters of common interest as agreed upon. Enabling legislation for this purpose was already adopted by Parliament last year.</li>

<li>3. Thirdly, such consultation and negotiation must take place on the premise that a suitable constitutional dispensation for the Republic must meet the requirements at every level of government, of protection and self-determination of minority groups, power-sharing among groups in respect of common affairs and the prevention of domination by any group of the others.</li>

</ol>

<p>Finality on constitutional structures is achievable only if authorised representatives of all groups and communities who reject violence as a political instrument, come together in a national institution which enjoys statutory recognised status, and in which real accountability can exist.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#election">

<heading>ELECTION</heading>

<p>Public accountability is only possible if political parties are periodically granted the opportunity to put their policy and viewpoints to the voters.</p>

<p>A considerable period has passed since the previous general election for the House of Assembly.</p>

<p>A democratic government must from time to time go to the country, and provide those who elect it the opportunity for decision.</p>

<p>Since the time of the previous election and the referendum, a large number of new voters have been added to our voters&#x2019; roll. They are mostly young people who have the right to participate in decisions about the future.</p>

<p>Although I have the right to dissolve Parliament under the Constitution, I have decided, after consultation with the three Ministers&#x2019; Councils, to dissolve only the House of Assembly at this stage.</p>

<p>The White voters of our country have during the past 40 years given their approval to many steps of renewal and constitutional development. I believe that with regard to our country&#x2019;s security, its economic and constitutional development, they will again decide with great responsibility.</p>

<p>South Africa must stand united against the spirit of revolution incited against us from abroad.</p>

<p>South Africa must unite against uncalled for and unwarranted interference in our domestic affairs.</p>

<p>South Africa must stand united to strengthen its economy through higher productivity, job creation and thrift to the advantage of all, and this must be done in the spirit of South Africa First.</p>

<p>South Africa must think and act like a living organism in order to build a democratic and stable constitutional future through consultation and the elimination of violence.</p>

<p>Information with regard to further steps on how to make progress in this regard will be submitted by me to the voters&#x2019; public.</p>

<p>The Proclamation date for the election will be promulgated on Saturday, 7 March.</p>

<p>The official nomination of candidates will take place on Tuesday, 31 March.</p>

<p>The election will take place on Wednesday, 6 May.</p>

</debateSection>

<heading>CONCLUSION</heading>

<p>I pray that the blessing of Almighty God may guide and sustain you in your labours and deliberations.</p>

<p>I now declare this Fourth Session of the Eighth Parliament of the Republic of South Africa duly opened.</p>
