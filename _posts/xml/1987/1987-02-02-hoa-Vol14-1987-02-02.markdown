<heading><span class="col_33-34" refersTo="page_0045"/>MOTION OF NO CONFIDENCE</heading>

<speech by="#leader_of_the_official_opposition">

<from>The <person refersTo="hansard_za">LEADER OF THE OFFICIAL OPPOSITION</person>:</from>

<p>Mr Speaker, I move the motion printed in my name on the Order Paper, as follows:</p>

<block name="quote">That the House has no confidence in the Cabinet.</block>

<p>As the hon the Leader of the National Party is not present, I must assume he is being represented here today by one of the two crown princes opposite. I do not know which one it is, so I will address myself to them collectively. They will know who it is I mean, and know who it is I hope will get the post. [Interjections.] Sir, the hon the Leader of the National Party has arrived here now and the other two gentlemen are therefore relieved of their responsibility. [Interjections.]</p>

<p>This is not the first occasion upon which the Leader of the Official Opposition has introduced a motion of no confidence in a National Party Government, but never before in 39 years has it been more appropriate and more justified than it is on this occasion. [Interjections.] Let us get the situation in perspective. Although this Government, as the result of the restricted political representation and misshapen constitutional structures which there are in our country, may control the levers of power, the fact is that that Government sitting opposite, is a minority government whose policies are rejected by the vast majority of all the citizens of our country. [Interjections.]</p>

<p>I believe that the coming election is going to show that, in spite of all the Government&#x2019;s abuse of power for party political purposes, that Government will not even enjoy the confidence of the majority of the White citizens of South Africa. With its party supporters disillusioned, dispirited and divided, spending their time apologizing to one another, the era of NP dominance in the Parliament of South Africa is fast coming to an end.</p>

<p>Regrettably for all South Africans at this time when the future of South Africa is balanced on a knife edge, we are saddled with an incompetent, wasteful, bullying Government whose policies have failed and whose ability to lead this country back to stability and harmony is utterly discredited. That is our charge against this Government.</p>

<p>At a time when people look for a message of hope, the Government&#x2019;s remarkable behaviour shows that it has no real plans for the present and neither does it have a clear vision for the future.</p>

<p>The policies of this Government were supposed to produce peace, progress and prosperity for the people of South Africa. Instead they have ended up by isolating our country, damaging our economy, dividing our nation, jeopardizing our future and undermining the living standards of millions of ordinary South Africans.</p>

<p>This wonderful country of ours is in a mess, thanks to the incompetence of a Government that has lost its way; but mercifully for all of us in South Africa, it is starting to lose both members and supporters.</p>

<p>During this debate members of the PFP are going to expose this Government&#x2019;s shabby record and point out how its policies and behaviour are jeopardizing the future well-being of all of us.</p>

<p>The State President, in his Opening Address to Parliament, gave a marginally optimistic view of the economy and especially of the improvement over the last two quarters of last year. I hope, for the sake of the people of our country, that his optimism is justified. I have no doubt that this Government is going to produce a variety of statistics between now and the election to tell the public how well things are going with the economy.</p>

<p>However, for the pensioner, the housewife and the family breadwinner as well as for millions of South Africans who do not live on graphs and statistics there is a different reality. It is a reality of financial insecurity, of economic hardship, of a daily battle to make ends meet and of deteriorating living standards. For the ordinary South African the reality is that inflation is running at an all time high. The buying power of his earnings and the value of his savings are being eroded by nearly 20% per annum.</p>

<p>Retrenchment is becoming commonplace and unemployment is at an all time high. Among Coloureds, Indians and Whites unemployment has doubled over the past year, while 30% of the Black labour force is unemployed&#x2014;and this percentage is even higher in areas like the Eastern Cape. Nearly 70 000 jobs have been lost in the building industry <span class="col_35-36" refersTo="page_0046"/>alone in the past four years. Civil summonses for debt are being issued at the rate of 80 000 per annum. Businesses are failing at the rate of 240 per month and personal insolvencies increased from 100 per month in 1983 to 354 per month in 1986. Fixed investment in the manufacturing sector, the barometer of growth and business confidence, dropped by nearly 50% from over R4 billion in 1980 to just over R2 billion at the end of 1985.</p>

<p>In the wake of these deteriorating economic factors there are also deteriorating social conditions for the people of South Africa. I shall only mention one. I believe that the level of crime&#x2014;and especially crimes of violence such as murder, rape, housebreaking, robbery and mugging&#x2014;has risen enormously. People no longer feel safe in the streets of their suburbs; indeed, they no longer feel safe even in their homes. The blame for the major part of this sorry state of affairs, we believe, lies fairly and squarely on the shoulders of that Government.</p>

<p>You see, Mr Speaker, there is nothing intrinsically wrong with the South African economy. What is wrong is that the way in which the Government has managed the affairs of this country combined with its failure to resolve the key political issues has undermined the confidence that there should be in the future of this country. If ever one wanted proof that the Government&#x2019;s policies have failed, one merely has to look at the state of emergency. I believe that to any objective observer the state of emergency is incontrovertible proof that the Government has failed to solve our country&#x2019;s problems.</p>

<p>The state of emergency, with its police powers, its abrogation of the rule of law, its assault on civil liberties, its muzzling of the Press, its denial of information to the public, its restrictions, its bannings, its gaol-without-trial is proof that the National Party, after 39 years in office, is incapable of governing this country by democratic means.</p>

<p>The emergency regulations have replaced political policies and authoritarian powers have replaced &#x201C;government by consent&#x201D; as the basis of law and order in our country. The Government has imposed a form of law and order in our country, but does it realize the price that South Africa is paying for this unnatural and enforced form of stability? Does the Government realize what it is doing to the very fabric of our society? I fear that as long as this Government is in power, the people of our country will not be able to enjoy again even the limited freedoms and qualified democracy which we once knew in South Africa.</p>

<p>Mr Speaker, the state of emergency is stamping its authoritarian imprint on the character and quality of our society in an indelible way. We run a very real danger that, as authoritarianism becomes the norm and not the exception, more and more White South Africans will become less caring about democracy&#x2014;because they are less accustomed to it&#x2014;and less jealous about personal liberty and the rule of law. On the other side, Black South Africans, in increasing numbers, will see Government structures as oppressive and Government agents as oppressors.</p>

<p>Does the Government realize that behind its facade of peace the process of polarization is continuing? Does this Government realise the extent of the damage it has done to the concept of a free Press in South Africa&#x2014;to the functioning of the Press, its quality, its role, its status; indeed, to the very concept of a free Press of which South Africa could rightly be proud in the past?</p>

<p>Does this Government realise the damage that the state of emergency is doing to even the limited parliamentary democracy that we have in South Africa? How can Parliament function properly when MPs are denied access to information about what is happening in their country?</p>

</speech>
