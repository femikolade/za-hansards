<p>Mr Chairman, I was pointing out yesterday why the Government&#x2019;s inability to bring about fundamental change and therefore provide security has resulted in a number of things, such as falling standards of living in our country, rising unemployment <span class="col_289-290" refersTo="page_0179"/>and an increase in crime. As result of these things we get greater frustration and more conflict, and the downward spiral continues in that way. The NP&#x2019;s big mistake is that it thinks it can get away with minimal reform introduced at a snail&#x2019;s pace and that it can cope with the unpleasant consequences simply by being tough.</p>

<p>This is a fallacy. Stability is essential but being tough is not enough. No lasting peace will come out of the barrel of a gun. Security management in recent years has often been counter-productive. It has polarised, politicized and radicalized whole communities in a way that no political party or organisation could have done.</p>

<p>The end result of 39 years of NP rule is more violence, more terrorism, more crime, less security and less stability than ever before. We must break out of the vicious downward spiral into which this Government has led us. This requires imagination and courage. It starts with fundamental change. It involves scrapping statutory racial classification as the basis for the social and political ordering of our society. Only when race relations improve will stability and confidence increase and the economy grow to provide jobs and a better quality of life.</p>

<p>We could then look forward to an upward spiral of peace, prosperity and hope for the future. The NP cannot do this. It is wedded to race. It will not bring about real reform, nor will there be lasting security.</p>

<p>I have pleasure in supporting the amendment of the hon member for Sea Point.</p>

<p>The viewpoints of the Royal Commission have international backing. <p>I want to make it clear that this Government subscribes to a free press but with due regard to the other fundamental principles. [Interjections.] It is subject to other fundamental principles accepted all over the world, be it self-constraint or statutory constraint. The object of both Government and the Press is to further the national interests. The right to freedom to expression and to information must be exercised in such a manner that it does not jeopardise other basic rights, the public order or national security.</p>
