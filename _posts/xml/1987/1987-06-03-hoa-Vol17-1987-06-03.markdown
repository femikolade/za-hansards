<p>Mr Chairman, various hon members have expressed their appreciation for the achievements of the Post Office during the past financial year. Various hon members also showed a clear understanding of the reasons for having to effect tariff adjustments. All the hon members involved also expressed their thanks for the dedicated service rendered by the officials of the Post Office, and mention was also made of the exceptional contribution made by the Post Office during the recent election, for example by providing election telephones and other special services. <p>Then there were other members who were critical. There were hon members who hinted at poor financial control, injudicious planning and so on. That is of course what we are here for, Mr Chairman. It is a good thing that we weigh up and assess all the arguments and counter-arguments. <span class="col_667-668" refersTo="page_0368"/>Before the adjournment of the House yesterday evening I quoted from the provisions of the Post Office Act as to how the Post Office should be managed. It is a statutory requirement that the Post Office shall be administered in accordance with business principles. At the same time, however, the Act states a further requirement, namely that this shall be done having due regard to the promotion of commerce, industry and agriculture, in all parts of the Republic. In our far-flung and sparsely populated country, with its limited capital resources this second requirement is not only difficult to implement but also difficult to reconcile with the requirements of business principles.</p>

<p>When one looks back over a large number of years at what has happened in the Post Office, it is clear that in general these divergent requirements have been well implemented in practice. Nothing stands still; the development of this country entails that new circumstances, new opportunities and new challenges arise, and one has to keep pace with them. The process of adjustment to these changes is an ongoing process, and this is what the Post Office has done.</p>

<p>The statement that to effect changes proves that matters are not healthy, as the hon member for Overvaal said <i>inter alia</i>, really says nothing but that they would rather prefer stagnation.</p>

<p>I think it is more likely to be a lack of adaptations that would demonstrate that matters were not on a healthy footing. I should like to mention an example in this connection. An example of changes which came with time is a change in the pattern of the demand for the telephone service. Analyses and investigations carried out in this connection recently indicated that over the past few years there has been a shift in demand from business to residential services. This is still taking place, particularly in the Black residential areas.</p>

<p>It goes without saying that business services produce more revenue than residential services. The shift in the demand consequently means that more capital has to be invested in the less profitable services, and that is the dilemma in which the Post Office finds itself.</p>

<p>As I said in my Budget Speech, it was clear that considerable adjustments to telephone subscriptions were necessary. The subsidisation of rentals by means of call charges is becoming excessive. The fact that rentals are too low also results in an artificial demand for services that are insufficiently used, and it is essential to adjust tariffs to this situation. If one is to take business principles into consideration at all, one has to address this problem.</p>

<p>The Post Office has to reconcile a few matters, including the fact that it has to render an increasing number of additional services at tariffs that are too low; it has to invest large amounts of scarce capital without a good return from it; and tariffs have been kept low for many years, particularly to keep the inflation rate in check as much as possible, but the point is now being reached at which the tariff pattern itself is becoming inflationary. I shall explain this: If the moneys received for telephone calls have to subsidise the rentals, it means that the cost structure of business enterprises&#x2014;that make the most calls&#x2014;and therefore their prices, are forced upwards; higher than they ought to be.</p>

<p>Attention will simply have to be given to this cross-subsidisation so as to eliminate the inflationary effect. I do not think that hon members on the opposite side of this House really have any objection to this logic. I sympathise with the hon member who acknowledged plaintively that it was necessary to reconcile tariffs with business principles, but asked why it was only the tariffs of all business undertakings that always had to be increased. I want to tell him that all of us would like the reply to that question.</p>

<p>There are still one or two little matters in connection with tariff increases to which I want to refer. I expected a reference to be made to the tariff increases which were only announced after the election. The reply to the question why we waited with the increases until after the election is easy. Firstly there was in fact an increase prior to the election, namely in the tariff for standard letters. At the time I indicated clearly why this was urgently necessary at that stage. In addition I just want to point out that the election meant that the Budget had to be postponed. For a healthy assessment of the true financial needs&#x2014;based on the latest figures, of <span class="col_669-670" refersTo="page_0369"/>course&#x2014;it was desirable to wait with general tariff increases as well. Consequently there is a very simple reason why we are only announcing the general tariff increases now.</p>

<p>Another question that was raised was whether Post Office tariffs would be increased again during the present financial year. I do not know where that idea comes from. Possibly those hon members are confusing the Post Office Budget with the Transport Services Budget. I should like to give hon members the assurance that it was not, and is not, the idea to introduce further tariff increases during the present financial year. [Interjections.] I am certain the Official Opposition will honour the undertaking given by the hon member for Overvaal, and now support the Bill. [Interjections.]</p>
