<p>Mr Speaker, I move:</p>

<block name="quote">That the Bill be now read a second time.</block>

<p>I have pleasure in submitting this Transport Services Appropriation Bill for the 1987-88 financial year to Parliament.</p>

<p><i>Economic Review 1986-87</i></p>

<p>The South African Transport Services based its budget for 1986-87 on a growth rate of 2,5<i>%</i>.</p>

<p>A growth rate of only 1<i>%</i> was realised, and this lower level is also reflected in the revenue of Transport Services.</p>

<p><span class="col_853-854" refersTo="page_0461"/>REVIEW OF ACTIVITIES AND TRAFFIC TENDENCIES</p>

<p>Mr Speaker, I shall now give a brief review of the activities of Transport Services during 1986-87.</p>

<p>Except where otherwise stated, the percentage change is always in respect of the 1986-87 financial year as compared with the 1985-86 financial year.</p>

<p><i>Passenger Services</i></p>

<p>The economic position and freer competition in the passenger transport market resulted in inter-city and commuter passenger journeys decreasing by 32<i>%</i> and 6<i>%</i> respectively.</p>

<p>In order to meet the demand during high-peak periods in the April and December holidays some 1 300 inter-city coaches, which are being utilised only a few days per year, have to be kept in service. This matter is receiving urgent attention.</p>

<p><i>Goods&#x2014;rail</i></p>

<p>The total tonnage of revenue-earning traffic reflects a decline of less than 1<i>%</i>. The prime considerations of transport users are price and quality.</p>

<p>If the quality of service is better than that rendered by competitors, users are prepared to pay a higher price.</p>

<p>With these considerations in mind, Transport Services is concentrating more and more on the provision of market-oriented services which can compete on the basis of quality and price.</p>

<p>Since their introduction, the popularity of min-containers is still increasing among clients. Eight per cent of all traffic previously handled in goods sheds is now being packed into mini-containers and despatched directly by consignors.</p>

<p><i>Airways</i></p>

<p>The number of both domestic and international passengers decreased by approximately 4<i>%</i>. As a result of this, various campaigns were introduced to ensure the profitability of the Airways. For instance, flights were curtailed with a view to bringing supply more into line with demand.</p>

<p>In the interests of our passengers, and in a positive effort to satisfy their needs, the catering is being improved and the cabins refurbished. Further improvements in the domestic service will soon be introduced to the public.</p>

<p><i>Road Transport</i></p>

<p>The number of passengers declined by almost 12<i>%</i> and goods tonnage by 6<i>%</i>.</p>

<p><i>Harbours</i></p>

<p>The total tonnage of cargo handled decreased by approximately 2<i>%</i>. Imports were approximately 5<i>%</i> lower, whilst exports decreased by 1,8<i>%</i>.</p>

<p>Since the inception of the containerisation concept almost ten years ago, the popularity of this method of cargo-handling has increased considerably. Quicker handling and turn-round times of ships are two of the advantages, and in this regard our harbours compare very favourably with the best in the world.</p>

<p><i>Pipelines</i></p>

<p>The volume of products handled decreased by less than 2<i>%</i>.</p>

<debateSection name="#financial_results_for_1986-87">

<heading>FINANCIAL RESULTS FOR 1986-87</heading>

<p>A working deficit of R98 million was budgeted for. The revised estimated revenue is R23 million less than the budget, but as a result of productivity enhancement and curtailment measures the original estimated expenditure could be scaled down by R260 million.</p>

<p>A surplus of R139 million is now envisaged for the financial year, which represents an improvement of R237 million. This surplus will be transferred to the Accumulated Surplus and Deficit Account which will reduce the accumulated deficit to approximately R460 million as at 31 March 1987.</p>

<debateSection name="#prospects_for_1987-88">

<heading>PROSPECTS FOR 1987-88</heading>

<p><i>Wiehahn Report</i></p>

<p>During the debate on the Part Appropriation Bill earlier this year I mentioned that I was in <span class="col_855-856" refersTo="page_0462"/>the process of evaluating the Wiehahn Report regarding the establishment of a negotiating body in connection with salaries and service conditions.</p>

<p>I have in the meantime referred the report to the trade unions and have since received their comments. As soon as their comments have been considered and all aspects of the report evaluated, the report and everything pertaining to it will be submitted to the Cabinet for consideration.</p>

<p><i>&#x2020;Strikes</i></p>

<p>Notwithstanding the efforts to uphold sound labour relations in Transport Services and to extend them, certain employees began to strike on 13 March 1987. The initial number of strikers increased to almost 20 000 on 21 April 1987, mainly as a result of intimidation. Despite being confronted with an illegal strike, Transport Services was exceptionally tolerant, notwithstanding outrageous misrepresentations concerning the reason for the strike. A distorted version was given of the normal disciplinary action taken against an employee who had committed a cash irregularity.</p>

<p>Transport Services endeavoured to hold talks with representatives of the workers throughout the strike and refrained from dismissing workers. In fact, Transport Services made it quite clear by means of posters, handbills, notices, announcements, press-releases, etc, that its doors were open for negotiation and invited strikers to return to work. The implications of their actions were also repeatedly pointed out to them. Transport Services persisted with this approach, even though the strikers constantly changed their demands.</p>

<p>Among the strikers themselves matters did not run smoothly at all. Those employees who were reluctant to strike were intimidated by means of violence.</p>

<p>It soon became evident that the strike had spread much further than merely the sphere of normal labour relations. It became an open campaign of intimidation and terrorism against Transport Service workers and the travelling public and resulted in the destruction of Transport Service assets.</p>

<p>The forces responsible for this were not bent upon settling disputes. They were intent on causing polarisation and provoking violent confrontation.</p>

<p>To counter the strike, firm action was inevitable. It necessitated the deployment of security forces at strategic depots and railway stations with a view to ensuring the safety of the workers, passengers, clients and their property.</p>

<p>It ultimately became necessary to resort to the drastic step of dismissing approximately 17 000 strikers. This decision was not taken lightly, since most of the workers who were dismissed had already completed many years&#x2019; service, had rendered good and loyal service and were evidently forced into the strike against their will. Transport Services did not want to lose their expertise and was concerned about the welfare of these people and their families.</p>

<p><i>De Villiers Report</i></p>

<p>When I presented the Part Appropriation Bill earlier this year, I mentioned in my speech that in his report Dr Wim de Villiers recommended a comprehensive strategy for the SATS in a deregulated transport market. The recommendations are being given careful attention and this report will also be submitted to the Cabinet for consideration as soon as possible.</p>

<heading>ESTIMATES OF REVENUE AND WORKING EXPENDITURE FOR 1987-88</heading>

<p>Confidence in the South African economy is recovering and a real growth rate of 3<i>%</i> is generally anticipated in the gross domestic product. The measures which were introduced in respect of the repayment of our country&#x2019;s foreign debt, coupled with a gold price which is still on the increase, hold out great promise.</p>

<p>The steady performance in the rand exchange rate, as the indications show, strengthens our ability to repay our debt. Only a moderate economic growth is expected in the major industrial countries. This, together with the protection of industries, the movement towards high-technology production processes and the consequent decline in the demand for raw materials on world markets will have an unfavourable effect on the Republic&#x2019;s export volumes.</p>

<p><span class="col_857-858" refersTo="page_0463"/>Many of Transport Services&#x2019; activities are linked to the export of raw materials and consequently our financial results will be adversely affected in the short term. The working estimates have been compiled on this basis.</p>

<p>Notwithstanding continued economy measures, the estimated financial results for 1987-88, without tariff adjustments, reflect a deficit of R570 million. Although very serious consideration was given to ways and means of balancing the budget, a tariff adjustment is unavoidable.</p>

<p>I wish to point out to hon members that the last general increase in tariffs was effected on 1 January 1986.</p>

<debateSection name="#tariff_increase">

<heading>TARIFF INCREASE</heading>

<p>Mindful of the changed market in which the SATS will have to operate, tariffs will have to be adjusted over a wide spectrum. The adjustments are therefore aimed at securing additional revenue by means of an increase in tariffs and fares and at essential tariff structural changes.</p>

<p>The tariff increase will be differentiated, which means that the same percentage will not apply throughout. The tariff increase on 1 July 1987 represents an average increase of 10<i>%</i>. Calculated over the period of 18 months since the last general tariff increase on 1 January 1986, the increase is equal to an annual rate of slightly more than 6<i>%</i>, which is well below the prevailing rate of inflation.</p>

<p>Tariffs on goods conveyed by rail are being increased on average by 11,9<i>%</i> from 1 July 1987. The tariffs applicable to high-rated traffic are to be increased on average by 7<i>%</i>.</p>

<p>Tariffs for containers conveyed by unit and non-unit trains are being increased by 7,2<i>%</i> and 8<i>%</i> respectively, but for different unit routes the increase on unit trains varies.</p>

<p>The tariffs in respect of mini-containers are to be increased on average by 9,9<i>%</i> on unit trains and 8<i>%</i> on non-unit trains. The conveyance of empty containers is extremely uneconomical and these tariffs are therefore being increased by 20<i>%</i>. Tariffs for petrol and diesel conveyed by rail are being increased by 8,1<i>%</i> and 13,4<i>%</i> respectively. The tariffs for low-rated traffic are being increased by 14<i>%</i>, whilst the tariff for manganese ore from Postmasburg to Port Elizabeth is to be increased by 16<i>%</i>. Tariffs for traffic conveyed at truck rates are being increased by 14<i>%</i> and the tariff scales for timber and those for specific types of exempted traffic are to be increased by 16<i>%</i>. The tariff for low-rated traffic conveyed in containers is to be increased by 15,2<i>%</i>. The tariffs applicable to coal not conveyed under contract conditions are being increased by 14<i>%</i> and those for livestock by 15<i>%</i>. Miscellaneous tariffs are being increased on average by 13,7<i>%</i>, whilst storage charges and similar levies are to be increased by 15<i>%</i>. Demurrage charges are not to be increased. The tariffs for mail and parcels conveyed by rail are to be increased by 15<i>%</i>.</p>

<p>In respect of road transport the average increase in tariffs and fares is 13,9<i>%</i>. Goods tariffs are being increased by 16<i>%</i> and fares by 12,5<i>%</i>. The tariffs for mail, parcels and miscellaneous services are also being increased by 12,5<i>%</i>.</p>

<p>Harbour tariffs will, with due regard to the cost coverage on the various services, be adjusted on average by 6,8<i>%</i> with effect from 1 July 1987. At the same time the minimum value of cargo on which wharfage is levied will be adjusted from R97 per ton to R120 per ton of 1 000 kg.</p>

<p>The cost coverage on marine services is at present approximately 47<i>%</i>. To prevent this position from deteriorating, tariffs are being increased, on average, by 15,3<i>%</i>.</p>

<p>It has also been decided to give further stimulus to the coastal route by doing away with the <i>ad valorem</i> principle applicable to wharfage on coastwise cargo and introducing a unit wharfage rate of R4-50 per ton per harbour.</p>

<p>For extremely low-value cargo, for which provision has already been made in contracts, wharfage based on the minimum value will still be paid, and this cargo will therefore be excluded from the wharfage rate per ton.</p>

<p>Insofar as rail-passenger fares are concerned, inter-city train services are being rationalised to improve the financial position.</p>

<p>Furthermore, everything possible is being done to keep cost levels as low as practicable <span class="col_859-860" refersTo="page_0464"/>by means of enhancing productivity and curtailing labour costs and expenditure on material and energy.</p>

<p>Despite all these efforts, the loss on passenger services remains unacceptably high and it is necessary to adjust fares.</p>

<p>I am of the opinion that smaller fare increases twice a year will be more acceptable than a relatively big increase once a year.</p>

<p>In view of this, commuter fares are being increased, on average, by 10<i>%</i> from 1 July 1987, with a further increase in October 1987. The extent of the latter increase will be determined by the prevailing conditions at that time.</p>

<p>In the case of inter-city services, fares are being increased on average by 15<i>%</i> for first-class, second-class and third-class journeys. This adjustment in inter-city fares is in accordance with the policy of placing the service on an economic footing within a reasonable period.</p>

<p>&#x2019;As for the S A Airways, an increase in the number of domestic passengers is envisaged, which should favourably influence the results of working.</p>

<p>I therefore have pleasure in informing hon members that air fares are not being increased at this stage. A watchful eye will, however, be kept on the position, and if the situation changes a further anouncement will be made in this regard.</p>

<p>Apart from the general tariff increase, it is also necessary to effect tariff structural changes. In the case of the rail goods service, the structural changes will be implemented on 1 October 1987. These changes are aimed at reducing the extent of internal cross-subsidisation, especially over longer distances. In general, relatively little traffic is conveyed over distances exceeding 1 500 km and the influence of the adjustments is therefore limited in extent.</p>

<p>The percentages for high-rated traffic vary between 1,3<i>%</i> and 14,6<i>%</i>, but in respect of certain distances tariffs are, in fact, reduced. In respect of low-rated traffic the figures vary between 6,6<i>%</i> and 20,1<i>%</i>, truck rates between 1,6<i>%</i> and 8,8<i>%</i>, tariffs for coal between 0,3<i>%</i> and 15,3<i>%</i> and those for specific types of exempted traffic between 10<i>%</i> and 10,3<i>%</i>.</p>

<p>In so far as rail passenger services are concerned, it is also necessary for the fare structure to be adjusted on 1 July 1987.</p>

<p>This structural adjustment will have the net effect of some increases being higher than the percentages indicated, while others will be considerably lower. It is important to note that the higher percentages mainly apply to the short distances. Although the increases are substantial from a percentage point of view, in real money terms they are negligible.</p>

<p>In addition, the rounding off to the nearest 10 cents for amounts less than R10, especially in respect of commuter fares over the short distances, will also have a fluctuating effect on the percentages indicated.</p>

<p>Structural changes in respect of pipelines will result in the tariffs for petrol and diesel being increased on 1 July 1987 by 7,4<i>%</i> and 12,1<i>%</i>, respectively. These structural changes are necessary in preparing for a deregulated transport market in which tariffs are to be a more accurate reflection of costs so as to enable users of transport to decide, in a rational manner, on the mode of transport they wish to use. The SATS revenue will, as a result, increase by only 1,4<i>%</i>.</p>

<p>Mr speaker, I wish to assure hon members that had it not been for the numerous measures which were introduced with a view to increasing productivity, these tariff and fare increases would have been much greater.</p>

<p>It should be noted that these tariff and structural adjustments will result in Transport Service revenue for the financial year increasing by only slightly more than 5<i>%</i>.</p>

<p>The effect of the tariff increase and the structural changes on the rate of inflation will be approximately 0,5<i>%</i>.</p>

<p>This, of course, is based on the assumption that trade and industry will handle the tariff increases in a responsible manner when fixing prices. Notwithstanding these tariff increases and structural changes, it is estimated that Transport Services will close the financial year with a deficit of R40 million.</p>

<p>I have, however, requested the Management <span class="col_861-862" refersTo="page_0465"/>to turn the deficit into a profit by means of further productivity-enhancement measures.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#capital_budget">

<heading>CAPITAL BUDGET</heading>

<p>In so far as the capital budget is concerned, one of the Transport Service strategies is continually to enhance the productive utilisation of its transport assets.</p>

<p>Until the end of the sixties it was possible to cope with the growth in traffic mainly by increasing the physical capacity of the infrastructure, such as the permanent way, traction, wagons and passenger coaches.</p>

<p>In the seventies the inflationary pressure, together with increasing capital costs, gave rise to a situation in which more and more emphasis was placed on a philosophy in terms of which managerial skills were acknowledged as a full-fledged production factor.</p>

<p>To curtail additional capital investment as much as possible, a strategy was accepted to cope to a greater extent with the growth in traffic by means of better operating techniques and higher productivity. The following are a few examples: The composition of trains in a more scientific manner, thereby reducing the amount of shunting required; operating longer trains; improving the utilisation of wagons, inter alia, by increasing the average speed of trains and by reducing the terminal turn-around time; minimizing the running-empty factor, and technically improving the wagon fleet by the modification of the existing wagons to increase their carrying capacity. The 1987-88 capital budget has also been compiled on the basis of this philosophy.</p>

<p>When investing, the emphasis is placed on improving Transport Service competitiveness and on a fair return on capital. Funding of new capital projects during 1987-88, for which appropriation is required, amounts to R750 million, and particulars are included in the publication which is being Tabled.</p>

</debateSection>

<debateSection name="#salaries_pensions_and_parity_programme">

<heading>SALARIES, PENSIONS AND PARITY PROGRAMME</heading>

<p>Hon members will recall that I recently announced that the salaries of Transport Service employees were to be increased by 12,5<i>%</i> with effect from the July 1987 pay-month. The expenditure for this purpose will amount to approximately R346 million for the remainder of the financial year.</p>

<p>The Transport Services has committed itself to the systematic elimination of disparities in salaries and other service conditions. In this connection agreement on a five-phase programme has been reached with the trade unions concerned, and the third phase which provides, inter alia, for parity in salaries in instances where the required qualifications, training and work content are equal, will be implemented with this salary adjustment.</p>

<p>However, it has been decided to expedite the programme, and the remainder of the programme, with the exception of travel and medical benefits; will now be implemented, provision being made in the estimates for an amount of approximately R72 million.</p>

<p>This will result in the provision of one set of service conditions for all personnel. The service conditions of all Coloured, Indian and Black personnel will be improved dramatically by this arrangement.</p>

<p>To enable the personnel who were dismissed on account of the strike, some of whom have already completed long terms of service, to share in the improved service conditions and pension benefits as well, Transport Services is prepared to re-employ most of those who were dismissed. I am also prepared to consider linking their service, which will result in their retaining their service conditions.</p>

<p>As announced, pensions will be increased as follows with effect from 1 June 1987:</p>

<block name="quote">One per cent for each completed year after retirement, with a minimum of 10<i>%</i> and a maximum of 20<i>%</i>.</block>
