<heading>POST OFFICE APPROPRIATION BILL</heading>

<summary>(Second Reading)</summary>

<p><i>Introductory speech as delivered at Joint Sitting on 25 May</i></p>

<speech by="#minister_of_communications">

<from>*The <person refersTo="hansard_za">MINISTER OF COMMUNICATIONS</person>:</from>

<p>Mr Speaker, I move:</p>

<block name="quote">That the Bill be now read a second time.</block>

<p>The past financial year, particularly the latter part, has been characterized by three developments, namely&#x2014;</p>

<p>Firstly, a turn for the better in the finances of the Post Office, mainly brought about by a drastic curtailment of expenditure and a moderate growth in telephone traffic.</p>

<p>Secondly, considerable progress in providing services, in particular telephone services, thereby reducing the waiting list by 17 873.</p>

<p>Thirdly, significant findings resulting from the investigation by Dr W J de Villiers into financial and related matters in the Post Office.</p>

<p>FINANCE 1986-87</p>

<p>I should now like to give hon members a review of the financial performance of the Post Office during the 1986-87 financial year. I am pleased to inform hon members that we achieved a better financial result than was originally expected. This is mainly due to strict economy measures and the application of financial discipline in every sphere of the Department&#x2019;s activities. In addition, a moderate improvement in telephone traffic, higher interest earned on the re-investment of temporary surplus funds and the strengthening of the rand against other monetary units contributed to the improved financial result.</p>

<p>Total expenditure came to approximately R5 319 million, which is R81 million or 1,5% lower than the amount of R5 400 million originally budgeted for. A saving on both operating and capital expenditure was realised.</p>

<p>Operating expenditure amounted to nearly R3 374 million, ie R47 million less than the original appropriation of R3 421 million. Capital expenditure was decreased by R47 million from R1 724 million to R1 677 million by the selective and judicious slowing down of certain building projects. On the other hand, an additional amount of R12 million was needed for standard stock capital for which R50 million had to be provided, compared with the original appropriation of R38 million.</p>

<p>As far as revenue is concerned, at the time of the introduction of the Part Appropriation Bill earlier this year, taking into account the promising indications of a slight revival in the economy at the time, I estimated that our revenue would be R93 million higher than the original estimate. I am pleased to tell hon members that as a result of the sustained improvement of the economy, the Post <span class="col_587-588" refersTo="page_0328"/>Office&#x2019;s total revenue comes to almost R3 792 million, which is R152 million higher than the amount of R3 640 million originally budgeted for.</p>

<p>With the expected lower operating and related expenditure of R3 641 million and the higher estimated revenue of R3 792 million, the operating shortage of R36 million originally budgeted for was transformed into an operating surplus of R151 million. After meeting the operating shortage of R108 million carried forward from the 1985-86 financial year, a surplus of R43 million was available to contribute to financing capital expenditure.</p>

<p>Before concluding the financial review, I should like to bring to hon members&#x2019; attention that a large saving, namely almost R51 million, was effected on personnel expenditure. This was achieved, for example, by keeping vacant posts unmanned where possible, withholding the creation of posts which were justified according to the norm and curtailing paid overtime. The result is that many thousands of hours of unpaid overtime are being performed voluntarily. I mention these facts so that hon members may realise that the favourable operating results were not achieved without great effort and sacrifice on the part of the personnel.</p>

<p>I accordingly wish to express my sincere thanks and appreciation to every member of the staff who contributed towards this achievement.</p>

<p>I shall now confine myself to a few of the basic services rendered by the Post Office, their pattern of development over the past financial year and our forward planning.</p>

<p>TELEPHONE SERVICES</p>

<p>Telephone services, including extensions, call-offices and miscellaneous services, increased by just over 178 000 during the past financial year. This brought the number of telephones to more than 4 200 000 at the end of March 1987 and represents a growth of 4,4%.</p>

<p>Of the total number of telephone, nearly 96% were connected to automatic exchanges.</p>

<p>The waiting list for telephone services was reduced by nearly 18 000, or almost 10%, to just over 167 000 at the end of March 1987, which is the lowest it has been for the past five years.</p>

<p>TELEPHONE SYSTEM</p>

<p>During the past financial year the capacity of the automatic telephone exchange system was expanded by nearly 292 000 allocatable lines&#x2014;the biggest number of lines ever provided in a single financial year.</p>

<p>In addition there were 122 fully electronic switching units and 146 remote concentrators in service at the end of March this year, providing 691 000 subscriber lines, 480 000 junction lines and 160 000 trunk lines.</p>

<p>It is planned to further extend the automatic exchange capacity by about 316 000 lines during 1987-88.</p>

<p>DATA TRANSMISSION SERVICES</p>

<p>Analogue data services increased by more than 6 000 during the past financial year, which represents a growth of 9,6%. It is estimated that a further 5 000 services will be provided during the 1987-88 financial year. The lower expected growth is attributed to the increasing demand for services on the digital network, known as Diginet, which provides data users with better availability and quality of service than analogue services. In view of the important cost and other advantages of the digital system, the expansion of the existing analogue transmission network has already been discontinued.</p>

<p>DIGITAL TRANSMISSION LINKS</p>

<p>The first of the inter-primary digital links will become operational during the 1987-88 financial year. These links include long-distance microwave and optical fibre systems which will connect Johannesburg and Pretoria to the other major centres in each province, namely Cape Town, Durban, Pietermaritzburg, Port Elizabeth, Bloemfontein and Kimberley, as well as providing links between all these centres. In addition, links have been planned to most of the secondary centres, some of which are already in use. Many more links will be operational within the next two to three years. <span class="col_589-590" refersTo="page_0329"/>With the establishment of a national digital transmission network all future speech, data and video requirements will be provided by suitably increasing the capacity of the digital links. New techniques, which are at present being evaluated for use in our network, include a variety of digital television coders which enable video signals to be carried over digital transmission systems, as well as 565 Megabit optical fibre systems. The latter technique can quadruple the potential traffic carrying capacity of our optical fibre systems.</p>

<p>DIGINET</p>

<p>Diginet is the Post Office&#x2019;s purely digital transmission network for high-grade data transmission. At the end of the past financial year 2 630 Diginet terminals were in operation, representing an annual growth of 125%. It is planned to expand the network during 1987-88 to all the major centres where digital transmission facilities are available. It is expected that some 55 new multiplexer sites will be established where data lines of different clients are concentrated and linked with Diginet. The total number of Diginet terminals should increase to 7 000 and the total number of Diginet multiplexer sites to 169.</p>

<p>&#x2020;SAPONET</p>

<p>Saponet, the public data network, provides both circuit and packet-switching services to customers. Owing to reduced demand for circuit switching services, only the packetswitching network will be expanded during the 1987-88 financial year. Following a decision during September 1985 to modernize and expand the packet-switching network, orders were placed for advanced packet-switching exchanges for delivery during the 1986-87 and 1987-88 financial years. The expanded network, which is expected to be completed by the end of 1987, will provide capacity for more than 300 000 channels on more than <i>4 000</i> ports.</p>

<p>The international packet-switched data service has been extended to Ireland, Israel, Luxembourg, the Netherlands and Sweden and is now available to 19 countries.</p>

<p>TELEX SERVICE</p>

<p>There were approximately 33 000 telex subscribers at the end of the current financial year, representing an increase of almost 1 000 services and a growth-rate of 3% over the previous financial year. Approximately 254 million inland telex call units were registered during 1986-87.</p>

<p>The service is still available to 196 destinations abroad, 118 of which can be dialled direct by subscribers. Some 14 million call minutes were registered during the year by South African subscribers.</p>

<p>TELETEX SERVICE</p>

<p>The number of teletex subscribers increased by nearly 1 000 to more than 2 000 during the past financial year. Almost 30 million inland teletex call units were registered up to 31 March 1987, 16 million units more than during the previous financial year. International dialling is already available to 8 countries, which is expected to lead to an increase in the demand for the service and result in a considerably higher volume of international teletex traffic. France, England, Switzerland and the Netherlands have expressed an interest in teletex access to the Republic and relevant negotiations with the administrations concerned are at present in progress.</p>

<p>PRE-PAYMENT PAYPHONES</p>

<p>The new pre-payment payphone is a great success from the point of view of both the user and the Department, for the user because he can use a variety of coins and for the Department because it renders vandalism and theft more difficult. It has accordingly been arranged with the suppliers to increase production of the wall-mounted unit to 600 units per month.</p>

<p>CARD-OPERATED PUBLIC TELEPHONES</p>

<p>Investigation into the use of a pre-paid card-operated public telephone is continuing and the Department intends undertaking a limited field-trial of a suitable unit within the next few months. The popularity of this type of payphone is rapidly increasing in many countries, not only because it is very convenient <span class="col_591-592" refersTo="page_0330"/>for the user, but also because it limits vandalism and is more reliable, therefore requiring less maintenance.</p>

<p>BUREAUFAX</p>

<p>Bureaufax is the name given internationally to the service which provides a facility for a customer to hand in a document at a Post Office for transmission, in the same way as in the public telegram service, except that it can also transmit graphics, for example, a signature. A pilot scheme to evaluat&#x00E9; the need for a Bureaufax system has been introduced between Johannesburg, Cape Town and Durban. If the demand justifies it, the service will gradually be expanded during 1987-88.</p>

<p>BELTEL</p>

<p>The system has 500 access ports and can serve 15 000 users. Users in Pretoria, Johannesburg, Cape Town, Durban, Pietermaritzburg, Port Elizabeth and Bloemfontein can obtain access to Beitel by means of a local call. In areas where Beitel cannot be reached by means of local calls, users can phone their nearest Beitel centre or gain entry to the system through the public data network.</p>

<p>At the end of March 1987, barely 9 months after the public Beitel service was launched, there were already more than 4 000 users, 144 information providers, 59 sub-information providers and 14 service providers registered on Beitel.</p>

<p>BUSINESS TELEPHONE SYSTEMS (BTS)</p>

<p>The BTS is a modern program-controlled substitute for manual switchboards. Development of a new digital version of the BTS, providing up to 100 extensions, has already reached an advanced stage. It is expected to be completed during 1989. This system has been developed entirely in the Republic by the supplier, in co-operation with the Department. Compared with similar apparatus available on the world market, the quality and potential of the system are such that it is predicted that it will also be in demand outside South Africa. It therefore has good export potential. If one considers the extremely fierce competition in the field of such systems world-wide, it will indeed be a compliment for South African technological ability should exports materialize.</p>

<p>POSTAL OPERATING</p>

<p>The economic slackness has, as one could expect, caused a slight decline in the volume of certain classes of mail. Large volumes are, however, still being handled and it remains the Department&#x2019;s aim to process and deliver postal articles to the addressees as soon as possible. At present there is no accumulation of mail at post offices. We are actually being hampered only by the long distances when air conveyance is not available.</p>

<p>In the light of the progress being made with the transmission, by means of the electronic media, of messages which would normally be channelled through the post, as well as with the electronic transfer of money and the greater utilization of credit cards which adversely affect mail volumes, there has been much speculation about the future of the postal service.</p>

<p>In extensive consultations in this regard with postal administrations in developed countries, we have found it to be the general viewpoint that the postal service will remain an important infrastructural service. In an advanced country such as the USA, for instance, mail volumes are still on the increase and our own investigations show that we must also expect this in South Africa.</p>

<p>PERSONNEL</p>

<p>Earlier I referred to the important contribution made by the personnel towards more favourable operating results. Among other things the creation of posts was restricted to a minimum. In a rapidly-growing business which does not develop uniformly on a country-wide basis, but has to meet demands at specific strong growth-points, the expansion of the staff establishment to maintain a satisfactory level of service is unavoidable. During the past financial year the staff complement in the Post Office consequently grew by 1,7%.</p>

<p>As far as training is concerned, the Post Office is still doing its part. The Department has at its disposal an extensive infrastructure to meet the technical training needs created by the highly sophisticated telecommunications <span class="col_593-594" refersTo="page_0331"/>technology. Country-wide almost 5 000 students of all population groups are receiving intensive formal training in various technical work-spheres at 13 well-equipped training centres, and the Department invests about R150 million annually in this training.</p>

<p>This contribution by the Post Office must be seen against the background of the disturbing phenomenon of technical training almost everywhere being curtailed.</p>

<p>Apart from the almost 5 000 students in the technical sphere, 453 persons are at present studying with Post Office bursaries at universities and technikons. For this purpose an amount of more than R1 million is being provided for this year.</p>

<p>A new development worth mentioning is the implementation in the Post Office of the system of Achievement Groups (Quality Circles), which is gaining ground world-wide.</p>

<p>An experiment has proved that the concept can be applied to advantage in the Department, thereby rendering a substantial contribution towards curtailing expenditure and increasing productivity. We intend implementing it as widely as possible in the Department.</p>

<p>Changes have been effected in the organizational field, with a view to rendering a more effective service to telephone clients in rural areas, by shortening lines of communication as much as possible. Satisfactory progress has already been made with the decentralizing of specific telephone commercial functions to selected rural offices. Seven such offices have already successfully been put into commission.</p>

<p>As hon members know, last month the Post Office experienced a strike by workers in certain areas. In all but two Black towns near Johannesburg, the Post Office succeeded in maintaining the service to the public at such a level that the strike was not disruptive.</p>

<p>I am keenly aware of the demands that were made on those staff members who did not strike, to keep the service running so smoothly. Time unfortunately does not allow me to elaborate on this, but suffice it to say that the country is greatly indebted to these people. I have already had my personal appreciation and that of the Department conveyed to each of them, but I wish to take this opportunity of again saying that we have every reason to feel very proud of them. With people who display so much loyalty towards their Department and who are voluntarily prepared to do more than is normally expected of them, we face the future with confidence.</p>

<p>*FINANCIAL INVESTIGATION</p>

<p>I informed hon members, during the introduction of the Part Appropriation Bill in February this year, about the investigation, similar to that undertaken at Escom and the South African Transport Services, which is being conducted for the Post Office by Dr W J de Villiers. The investigation mainly covers the capital expansion programmes of the Post Office and related financial matters.</p>

<p>The investigation, with which good progress is being made, has focussed on three areas, in particular during the past few months, namely:</p>

<ol>

<li>1. The cost-relatedness of the tariffs of the various services rendered by the Post Office, in conjunction with the external cross-subsidization between services and internal subsidization between subdivisions of the same services;</li>

<li>2. the enormous growth in the capital expansion programmes necessitated, in particular, by the exploding demand for telephone services and the changing demand patterns; and</li>

<li>3. the changes necessary in policy strategy in order to administer a public commercial undertaking, such as the Post Office, along business lines, as required by the Post Office Act.</li>

</ol>

<p>I shall now deal with these three matters briefly on the basis of the preliminary findings of the investigation. I expect that they will be dealt with fully in the report which we expect to be available later this year.</p>

<p><i>Cost-relatedness of tariffs and crosssubsidisation</i></p>

<p>It has been found that, owing to particular considerations and developments over a period of years, the unprofitable postal, telegram and money-transfer services are <span class="col_595-596" refersTo="page_0332"/>being subsidized by the more profitable telephone service. In addition it has been found that internal subsidization is also taking place between sub-divisions of the same service, the most important being the subsidisation of telephone rental by telephone call charges. It is estimated that telephone rental will subsidize telephone call charges by more than R500 million in 1987-88. As a result of the change in the revenue and demand patterns for the various services during the past decade&#x2014;more residential telephone services with a low call rate, for example, are being provided at an increasing rate&#x2014;the ability of the profitable telephone service to subsidize the unprofitable postal and other services as well as the unprofitable rental of telephone services, has to a large extent also been adversely affected.</p>

<p>It is essential that tariffs for the various services should be adjusted to relate them more closely to the cost of rendering the service.</p>

<p><i>Capital expansion programmes and demand for telephone services</i></p>

<p>During the period 1950 to 1978 the average annual growth in the demand for telephone services was 5%. This growth-rate increased to an average of 10,8% per year during the period 1978 to 1982, but levelled off to an average of 5,3% per year from 1982 to 1986. A growth-rate of about 7,3% is forecast for telephone services for the next few years.</p>

<p>In 1986 residential telephone services constituted 73% of the total number of telephone services. During the period 1950 to 1978, the demand for residential telephone services increased by an average of 5,5% per year.</p>

<p>In the high-growth period, 1978 to 1982, the increase accelerated to an average of 12% per year, but during the period 1982 to 1986 again declined to an average of 4,9% per year. The relatively high growth-rate in residential telephone services during the period 1978 to 1982 is chiefly attributable to the radical change which has taken place in the demand pattern, namely a relatively high demand by Asians, Coloureds and Blacks compared to Whites. This trend is clearly illustrated by the increase in telephone density in the case of the different population groups since 1978. The telephone density in respect of Whites increased from 71,5 per 100 dwellings in 1978 to 83,9 in 1986, in respect of Asians from 35,6 to 71,8, in respect of Coloureds from 19,3 to 53,2 and in respect of Blacks in metropolitan areas from 3,3 to 37,9 during the corresponding period.</p>

<p>This is undoubtedly an outstanding achievement for which the Post Office deserves to be commended.</p>

<p>To provide for the exploding demand for telephone services, and to supply commerce, industry and financial institutions with the advanced and rapid communication services which they demand, coupled with the decision to change over to an electronic telecommunications network, required an exceptionally high capital outlay, especially since 1978-79. The revenue and user patterns of services and the adjustment of tariff structures did not, unfortunately, keep pace with these high expenditure levels and have contributed largely to the weakening of the financial structure of the Post Office during the past few years.</p>

<p><i>Policy strategy</i></p>

<p>It is already clear that a change in policy strategy has become vital in order to deal with the basic problems of, firstly, the high demand for services that generate a low income, in particular residential services, and secondly, the cross-subsidization that exists between profitable and non-profitable services and between sub-divisions of services. It would appear that solutions to these problems are to be found in the re-organization of the existing functional structure into business units and the management of the Post Office on business lines, with return on capital and cost-related tariffs as the most important norms. Moreoever it appears desirable to undertake an investigation into the possibility of increasing the efficiency of parts of the telecommunications system.</p>

<p>&#x2020;FINANCE 1987-88</p>

<p><i>Expenditure</i></p>

<p>Apart from the financing of loan repayments which will amount to R305 million, total expenditure for 1987-88 is estimated at <span class="col_597-598" refersTo="page_0333"/>R5 845 million. This represents an increase of 9,9% on the previous financial year. Operating expenditure is estimated at R3 891 million, and is R517 million, or 15,3%, higher than in 1986-87.</p>

<p>The increase in operating expenditure stems mainly from higher estimated expenditure on salaries in the form of a general salary increase of 12,5% with effect from 1st July 1987, on international commitments as a result of an expected growth in international traffic, on financing costs of new loans, on exchange rate adjustments resulting from the redemption of foreign loans, and on general cost and price increases. Expenditure on other appropriations, namely standard stock capital, the Staff Housing Scheme and the provision to spread the redemption of loans and the effect of exchange rate changes over a suitable period, amounts to R334 million, which is R67 million more than in 1986-87. In view of the need to limit expenditure to what is absolutely unavoidable in order to make the best use of the financial resources of the country, capital expenditure for 1987-88 has been drastically cut to R1 620 million. This is R57 million less than in 1986-87 and represents the curtailment of the capital expansion programme by approximately 20% in real terms.</p>

<p><i>Revenue</i></p>

<p>At prevailing tariffs, revenue is estimated at R3 919 million in 1987-88. Despite the expected moderate improvement in revenue, and positive efforts to cut expenditure to a bare minimum, we shall still be facing an estimated operating deficit of R307 million. Unless tariffs are adjusted this deficit will have to be financed from loans, which would bring the total loan requirement in 1987-88 to R1 195 million.</p>

<p>The financing of operating deficits from loans is an unsound practice which no business undertaking can afford. Such action would not only weaken the financial structure of the Post Office, but would moreover adversely affect its ability to negotiate loans.</p>

<p>To maintain a sound financial position, the Post Office should each year have at its disposal an adequate operating surplus to contribute towards capital expenditure. Since the surpluses realized on the profitable services were insufficient, over the past few years, to cover fully the losses on the unprofitable services, the operating surpluses have been declining, and in 1985-86 an operating shortage was incurred for the first time in many years.</p>

<p>As a result of price and cost increases over the years, and the fact that the tariffs of the unprofitable services could, for particular reasons, not be adjusted sufficiently to keep pace with the rapidly increasing expenditure, the losses on these services have now reached such proportions that they cannot be borne by the profitable services for very much longer. Even with moderate tariff increases, which I unfortunately have to propose and which I shall deal with later, the losses that will be incurred on the unprofitable services in 1987-88 will still amount to almost R200 million. Of these losses R147 million will be incurred on the postal service, R13 million on the telex service and R28 million on the telegram service.</p>

<p>Having introduced every possible economy measure in running the unprofitable services, it is clear that three options remain open to us, namely&#x2014;</p>

<ol>

<li>1. The abolition or curtailment of services;</li>

<li>2. maintaining the status quo of cross-subsidisation; or</li>

<li>3. applying a cost-related tariff structure.</li>

</ol>

<p>We have thoroughly investigated the possibility of abolishing or curtailing services. We have considered possibilities such as postal delivery on alternate days instead of daily, the suspension of the special delivery of telegrams, the closure of uneconomic post offices, of which there are many, particularly in rural areas, and the reduction of service points at counters, to name but a few. These services are so basic that I believe that hon members would not wish any of these steps to be introduced in the communities they represent.</p>

<p>As regards the second option, namely the continuation of cross-subsidisation at the present rate, I believe I have made it clear that the revenue pattern of the telephone service, which until now has had to carry the burden, is changing to such an extent that it cannot be allowed to continue any longer <span class="col_599-600" refersTo="page_0334"/>without landing us in an unmanageable situation. Moreover, it causes price distortion and creates an unrealistic demand for the unprofitable services which continually compound our problems.</p>

<p>This leaves us with the last option only, which I believe will be the only equitable one for all users of Post Office services, namely to plan in such a way that ultimately a person making use of a service will pay for it without being subsidized by the users of other Post Office services.</p>

<p>We intend adjusting tariffs gradually to achieve this goal. I know that tariff increases should, under present circumstances, be kept as low as possible, and for that reason an adjustment that will increase Post Office revenue by only 8,8% in the 1987-88 financial year is contemplated with effect from 1 July 1987.</p>

<p>It will yield additional revenue of R345 million during the 9 months of the financial year, reduce the loan requirement for 1987-88 to approximately R888 million and leave a small operating surplus of about R38 million which can be appropriated towards the financing of capital expenditure. If the prevailing inflation rate is taken into account, the adjustments are very reasonable.</p>

<p>Details of the tariff adjustments will shortly be published in the <i>Gazette</i>. They are already available. For the information of hon members I should like to deal with some of the more important adjustments.</p>

<p>The telephone call unit charge is being increased from 12c to 13,5c; this also applies to telex and teletex calls.</p>

<p>The call unit charge for payphones is being increased from 10c to between 15c and 16c for long-distance calls and 20c for local calls. Although the increase may appear to be drastic, it should be borne in mind that the tariff of 10c has been applicable since 1 April 1982, that is, for more than five years. No rental is received for payphones although they require expensive apparatus. A higher call unit tariff than that applicable to calls from normal rented services is therefore fully justified.</p>

<p>The cost of handling a payphone call unit is at present in excess of 20c. At the current tariff a loss of R44 million will be sustained on payphones during 1987-88. The proposed tariff adjustment will reduce the loss to R13 million.</p>

<p>Telephone rentals are being increased by between R3 per month and R5,50 per month. The &#x201C;rental&#x201D; charged for a telephone service is, in fact, the minimum payment necessary from any telephone customer for the service to be viable. This amount should be paid even if no calls are made, and should cover the cost of giving the customer access to the system.</p>

<p>The current rental for a telephone service does not nearly cover the cost of providing and maintaining it and rentals, will as already mentioned, still have to be subsidized to the tune of more than R500 million per annum by telephone call charges. A considerable increase will therefore be required to put rentals on a cost-related footing. The following examples illustrate the situation:</p>

<p>As a sudden increase of rentals to a cost-related level would be too drastic, only a moderate increase is proposed at this stage. This is a basic tariff, and the fact that it is not cost-related results in an artificially high demand for services. Furthermore, it has the effect that the loss on rentals has to be made good by the business sector and persons who make full use of the service, thereby paying more for calls than is justified from a cost point of view. This naturally increases the cost structures of those users and also stimulates inflation. We also cannot afford the excessive capital outlay required for the provision of services which are not economically justified.</p>

<p>I do not like drawing comparisons with the tariffs of other countries, because in such comparisons so many factors come into play that incorrect conclusions are easily drawn. <span class="col_601-602" refersTo="page_0335"/>However, I have noted the rentals that are levied elsewhere, and whatever allowance is made for other factors, the proposed tariff is still among the lowest in the world.</p>

<p>The installation charge for a telephone is being increased from R90 to R125, but the tariff of R25 for social pensioners is left unchanged.
